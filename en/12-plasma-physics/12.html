Advanced topic · about 30–60 min

Wave heating and modes: whistler, O/X, and cyclotron resonance

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the wave-propagation lesson; the tokamak lesson. If a prerequisite is shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Waves of different frequencies/polarizations resonate with different particle populations: electron cyclotron resonance heating (ECRH), ion cyclotron (ICRH), lower-hybrid current drive (LHCD); the O/X-mode dispersion and the cutoff–resonance structure of a magnetized plasma determine the coupling window.

Resonance ω=ω_ce (ECRH); f_ce≈28 GHz/T

From assumptions to result

  1. Cold-plasma dispersion gives the R/L waves and the O/X modes.
  2. For the X mode, propagation forbidden bands exist between the left-hand cutoff, ω_ce, and 2ω_ce, so injection from the high-field side or O-X conversion is required.
  3. The resonance-absorption condition ω−k∥v∥=ω_c/γ hands the wave energy over to the particles.
  4. Antenna coupling is a core issue of device engineering.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

ECRH gyrotrons (140/170 GHz) have achieved megawatt-class continuous output, used for startup and profile control in ITER and W7-X; whistler waves propagating in Earth's magnetosphere (lightning "whistlers") share the same origin as thruster modulation.

Limits of validity

Linear cold-plasma dispersion; nonlinear sidebands and absorption saturation require kinetics.

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

The X mode has a cutoff/resonance forbidden band between ω_ce and 2ω_ce, so low-field-side injection is reflected; O-X-B conversion or high-field-side injection is required.
