Overview / Plasma Physics / Lesson 7

Undergraduate core · about 30–60 min

Magnetic confinement and the tokamak configuration

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: cyclotron motion; MHD. If a prerequisite is shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Charged particles gyrate around field lines; a toroidal magnetic field plus a helical poloidal field can confine plasma at a hundred million degrees; ignition performance is measured by the triple product nTτ.

Fusion triple product nTτ_E≳3×10²¹ keV·s/m³ (D-T)

From assumptions to result

  1. In a toroidal field, curvature drift causes charge separation, and the induced electric field leads to an overall outward drift.
  2. Superposing a poloidal field makes the field lines helical, and particles average out the drift as they travel along the helical surfaces.
  3. The energy confinement time is given by empirical transport scalings and cannot be derived analytically from the fundamental equations.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The ITER design point: n≈10²⁰ m⁻³, T≈10 keV, τ_E≈3.7 s, a triple product of about 3.7×10²¹, corresponding to the Q=10 steady-state gain design target (self-sustaining ignition requires higher parameters; the margin must be argued separately).

Limits of validity

A simplified equilibrium picture; real devices face instabilities, disruptions, and turbulent transport.

Transfer and counterexample

Why must a tokamak add a poloidal field?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

To eliminate the charge separation and the overall vertical drift caused by toroidal drift.
