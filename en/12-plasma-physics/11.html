Advanced topic · about 30–60 min

Landau damping and an introduction to kinetics

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the plasma-oscillations lesson; the concept of a distribution function. If a prerequisite is shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Even a collisionless plasma can "damp" electrostatic waves: particles resonant with the phase velocity exchange energy with the wave; particles are accelerated/trapped and the wave decays — Landau damping is the signature result of kinetic (Vlasov) theory and also appears in accelerator beams.

Resonance condition ω=k·v; γ_LD∝∂f/∂v|_(ω/k)

From assumptions to result

  1. The Vlasov equation describes the evolution of the collisionless distribution.
  2. After linearization, electrostatic waves resonate with particles whose velocity equals the phase velocity.
  3. Particles slightly slower than the wave are accelerated (the wave loses energy) and slightly faster ones are decelerated; the net effect is set by the slope of the distribution — a monotonically decreasing equilibrium distribution gives net damping.
  4. In the nonlinear stage, particles are trapped in phase-space wells and the damping saturates.

Worked example and check

The Landau damping rate can be ten orders of magnitude smaller than the collision frequency; solar-wind whistlers, turbulent transport in tokamaks, and intrabeam scattering in accelerators are all kinetic processes; Landau's 1946 solution founded plasma kinetics.

Limits of validity

Linear theory; nonlinear trapping and turbulent spectra require further treatment.
