Undergraduate core · about 30–60 min

Plasma frequency and wave propagation

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Debye shielding; electromagnetic waves. If a prerequisite is shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Free electrons give the electromagnetic field a characteristic collective frequency: waves below it are reflected (metallic luster, shortwave reflection off the ionosphere), while waves above it can penetrate.

ω_p=√(n_e e²/(ε₀ m_e)); dispersion ω²=ω_p²+c²k²

From assumptions to result

  1. Electrons are driven by the wave field, and the Coulomb restoring force of charge separation gives plasma oscillations.
  2. Combining this with the wave equation gives the dispersion relation; for ω<ω_p the wavenumber becomes imaginary, i.e. total reflection.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

A metal with n_e≈10²⁸ m⁻³ gives ω_p≈5.6×10¹⁵ rad/s (ultraviolet), so visible light is reflected; the ionosphere with n_e~10¹¹ m⁻³ gives f_p≈3 MHz, which shortwave communication relies on for reflection.

Limits of validity

Cold-plasma, collisionless approximation; collisions and an external magnetic field modify the dispersion relation.

Transfer and counterexample

Why do medium-wave broadcasts travel farther at night?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

After the D layer disappears at night, medium waves are reflected by the higher F layer, so a single hop covers a greater distance.
