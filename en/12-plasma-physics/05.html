Undergraduate core · about 30–60 min

Drift motion of charged particles: E×B, gradient, and curvature

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the lesson on cyclotron motion. If a prerequisite is shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

In a strong magnetic field, charged particles gyrate rapidly, and the slow drift of the gyrocenter determines confinement quality. An electric field causes the charge-independent E×B drift; magnetic-field inhomogeneity causes gradient and curvature drifts, which point in opposite directions for positive and negative charges — this is the physical root of why toroidal devices must add a poloidal field.

v_E=E×B/B²; v_∇B=(v⊥²/2ω_c)·B×∇B/B² (with the sign of the charge)

From assumptions to result

  1. Order the equation of motion by the small parameter ρ_L/L: the zeroth order is gyration; at first order, averaging over a gyroperiod removes the fast-varying terms and yields the guiding-center equation.
  2. Averaging the electric-field term gives the E×B drift.
  3. The magnetic moment μ=m v⊥²/(2B) is approximately conserved (an adiabatic invariant); gyro-averaging the magnetic-field gradient gives the gradient drift.
  4. The centrifugal term along curved field lines gives the curvature drift.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

With E=10⁴ V/m and B=1 T, v_E=10⁴ m/s, independent of charge and mass; in Earth's radiation belts, gradient drift moves protons westward and electrons eastward, forming the ring current — the main current system of geomagnetic storms.

Limits of validity

A magnetized plasma (ρ_L≪ characteristic scale) without strong collisions; turbulent diffusion and resonant wave–particle interactions go beyond the guiding-center picture.

Independent calculation

Show solution

v_E=E×B/B² is determined by the fields alone; positive and negative particles drift in the same direction at the same speed.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does gradient drift cause overall loss in a toroidal device?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

In a toroidal field, B is uneven between the inner and outer sides; gradient drift separates positive and negative charges vertically, and the induced electric field then causes an outward E×B drift that pushes the plasma toward the wall; adding a poloidal field to make the field lines helical is what averages this drift away.
