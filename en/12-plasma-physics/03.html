Undergraduate core · about 30–60 min

Electron plasma oscillations

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: electric fields; continuous media; small oscillations. If a prerequisite is shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Displace a uniform electron cloud slightly relative to a fixed ion background, and a restoring electric field appears. In a cold plasma, small disturbances oscillate because of collective charge separation; this is not a single-electron atomic orbital frequency.

ω_p=√(nₑe²/(mₑε₀))

From assumptions to result

  1. An electron displacement x produces a surface charge density nₑex.
  2. The internal electric field is approximately nₑex/ε₀.
  3. The electron equation of motion is mₑẍ=−nₑe²x/ε₀.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

If the electron density becomes 4 times larger, ω_p increases by a factor of 2. This frequency is an angular frequency; converting to Hz also requires dividing by 2π.

Limits of validity

Cold electrons, small amplitude, uniform background, and ions regarded as stationary for now; temperature and magnetic fields can change the wave dispersion.

Independent calculation

Show solution

ω_p is proportional to the square root of the density, so the factor is 5.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does a smaller electron mass mean a faster response in general?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

With the same restoring electric field the inertia is smaller, and ω_p is inversely proportional to the square root of the mass; the ionic time scale is usually slower.
