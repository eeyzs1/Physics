Advanced topic · about 30–60 min

Magnetic reconnection: sudden change of magnetic-field topology

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the MHD lesson; Ohm's law. If a prerequisite is shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

In ideal MHD, field lines are "frozen" into the plasma; when antiparallel magnetic fields meet in a thin current sheet, finite resistivity lets the field lines break and reconnect, converting magnetic energy into kinetic energy and heat in a burst — the common engine of flares, magnetospheric substorms, and sawtooth oscillations.

Sweet–Parker outflow ejected at v_A; reconnection rate set by the diffusion region

From assumptions to result

  1. Antiparallel fields compress into a current sheet at the X point; the resistive diffusion region has width δ≈√(ηL/V_A) (Sweet–Parker), and the outflow is ejected at the Alfvén speed.
  2. The classical rate is too slow; observations call for fast reconnection (Petschek shocks, kinetic regions).
  3. Reconnection restructures the magnetic topology, and the induced electric field accelerates particles to nonthermal energies.

Worked example and check

Solar flares release magnetic energy of order 10²⁵ J within minutes; Earth's magnetospheric substorms occur every 2–3 hours (linked to the space-weather lesson); the four-spacecraft MMS formation has crossed the electron diffusion region and measured structures on the ~100 km scale.

Limits of validity

Resistive MHD is a simplification; the electron diffusion region and collisionless reconnection require fully kinetic simulations.
