Collective response of a charged gas

Lesson goal: be able to explain "collective response of a charged gas" in your own words, and independently complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: electromagnetism; fluids; statistical physics. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Understanding the model

A plasma contains mobile charged particles and can respond collectively to electric fields. In many situations it is approximately electrically neutral on large scales, yet locally electric fields and charge separation still occur. The Debye length measures the spatial scale over which electrostatic disturbances are shielded; the plasma criterion is that the number of particles in a Debye sphere is N_D=nλ_D³≫1.

λ_D = √(ε₀k_BTₑ/(nₑe²))

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write down the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The precise meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

With the other parameters fixed, if the electron density becomes 4 times larger, the Debye length becomes half as large. This expression uses a simple thermal-equilibrium electron model and must not be applied to arbitrary strongly coupled plasmas without checking.

Check the boundary cases

Approximate charge neutrality does not mean there are no electromagnetic interactions.
