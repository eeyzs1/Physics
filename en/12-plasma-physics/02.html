Particles circling in a magnetic field

Lesson goal: be able to explain "particles circling in a magnetic field" in your own words, and independently complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: electromagnetism; fluids; statistical physics. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about this question first

With the other quantities unchanged, if the magnetic field increases by a factor of 4, by what number is the original radius divided? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

In a uniform magnetic field the Lorentz force is perpendicular to the particle's velocity and does no work. The perpendicular component of the velocity produces gyration, while the parallel component produces motion along the field; together they form a helical trajectory. Only nonrelativistic particles are considered here.

Work through the example with reasoning

For the same kind of particle at the same perpendicular velocity, doubling the magnetic field halves the gyroradius. Real magnetic confinement must also deal with collisions, drifts, and instabilities; the uniform-field circular-orbit model is only a starting point.

Check the boundary cases

A magnetic field can change the direction of the velocity, but the magnetic force alone cannot directly increase the kinetic energy of a single particle.
