Undergraduate core · about 30–60 min

The magnetic mirror: magnetic confinement by reflection at the open ends

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the drift lesson; the cyclotron-motion lesson. If a prerequisite is shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

A magnetic field that is strong at the two ends and weak in the middle forms a magnetic mirror: conservation of the magnetic moment reflects charged particles from the strong-field ends; particles with too small a pitch angle escape through the loss cone — the magnetic mirror was the earliest confinement scheme and is also the natural mechanism by which the magnetosphere traps energetic particles.

Magnetic moment μ=m v⊥²/(2B) conserved; sin²α₁=B₁/B₂

From assumptions to result

  1. In a slowly varying magnetic field the magnetic moment is an adiabatic invariant.
  2. Along a field line v⊥ grows and v∥ shrinks; reflection occurs where v∥=0.
  3. Conservation of energy and μ gives the reflection condition sin²α> B_mid/B_end.
  4. Particles inside the loss cone escape, and collisions continually refill the cone — the classical mirror can hardly reach fusion conditions, and the tandem mirror is its improvement.
  5. Earth's magnetosphere is a natural magnetic mirror that traps the radiation belts.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Earth's radiation belts (Van Allen 1958) are a real example of mirror + drift confinement; with mirror ratio B_end/B_mid=2, particles with equatorial pitch angles above 45° are trapped.

Limits of validity

The adiabatic invariant requires a slowly varying magnetic field; the loss cone and interchange instabilities limit the classical mirror.

Independent calculation

Show solution

sin²α_c=1/2, α_c=45°.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can the Van Allen belts hold MeV electrons for so long?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

Mirror reflection (along v∥) + drift closure (in longitude) + slow mirror-point scattering: a natural combination of three confinement mechanisms.
