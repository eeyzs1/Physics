Overview / Plasma Physics / Lesson 6

Undergraduate core · about 30–60 min

Magnetic pressure and plasma β: the key to magnetic-confinement equilibrium

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the MHD lesson; the Lorentz force. If a prerequisite is shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

A magnetic field carries the pressure B²/2μ₀ and squeezes field lines together laterally; β=particle pressure/magnetic pressure is the most important dimensionless parameter of a confinement device — β≪1 means the magnetic field dominates completely, while β approaching 1 is the compromise between the economics target and the stability boundary.

p_B=B²/(2μ₀); β=2μ₀p/B²

From assumptions to result

  1. Projecting the Maxwell stress tensor along the normal to a magnetic surface gives the magnetic pressure B²/2μ₀.
  2. The ideal-MHD equilibrium J×B=∇p is combined with ∇·B=0 (qualitatively leading toward the Grad–Shafranov equation).
  3. In a straight cylindrical plasma the internal pressure is balanced by the difference in magnetic pressure between the two sides, and β is precisely the ratio of pressure to magnetic pressure.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

A typical tokamak has p≈2×10⁵ Pa and B=5 T: magnetic pressure B²/2μ₀≈10⁷ Pa, β≈2%; the solar corona has B≈10⁻⁴ T, n≈10¹⁵ m⁻³, T≈10⁶ K: p≈0.014 Pa, magnetic pressure ≈0.004 Pa, β≈3 — the tokamak is magnetic-pressure dominated, while in the corona at these parameters the plasma pressure is already comparable to the magnetic pressure.

Limits of validity

Ideal MHD with isotropic pressure; in magnetic-mirror configurations the perpendicular/parallel temperature anisotropy is significant.

Independent calculation

Show solution

β∝1/B²; doubling the magnetic field → β shrinks to 1/4.

Transfer and counterexample

Why do fusion reactors pursue high β yet face stability limits?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

A high β means holding the same pressure with cheaper magnets; but high β approaches MHD thresholds such as ballooning modes, so a compromise within the stable window is required.
