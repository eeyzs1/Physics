Fluid-model validation of electron oscillations

This is a reproduction exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare the model against the evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to having produced original work.

Starting conditions: you can complete the written lessons of this field and fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced by public or synthetic data, and the source must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Integrate the electron response with the linear displacement model; vary the density; measure the frequency from the spectrum and the zero-crossing times.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and a falsifiable prediction.
  2. First work a minimal example that can be solved analytically or by hand, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusion is robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

ω_p grows with √n; when the initial amplitude is small enough the frequency is approximately amplitude-independent; the result converges as the step size decreases.

If your results disagree, first check the implementation, the units, and the assumptions before discussing new physical interpretations; agreement likewise cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

A density–frequency plot, the fitted scaling, and an integration-error report.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, and references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must carry uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Mistaking an angular frequency for an ordinary frequency; choosing too large a time step, which produces numerical oscillations.

Extension questions

Add damping and distinguish genuine collisional dissipation from algorithmic dissipation.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the paper-classification entry for this field, find a relevant topic and select a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send me the report and the computations for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; without a review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched for and verified again while the project is being carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

Testable question; complete theoretical derivation; reproducible results; credible error analysis; alternative explanations checked; clear argumentation and citations. For each item 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full score of 12 only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
