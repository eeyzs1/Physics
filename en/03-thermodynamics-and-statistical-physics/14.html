Equipartition of Energy and the Virial Theorem

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the partition-function lesson; Newtonian mechanics. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

In the high-temperature limit each quadratic degree of freedom contributes ½k_BT; multiplying the equations of motion by r, summing, and taking the long-time average gives the virial theorem, which links pressure to molecular kinetic energy and also underpins mass estimates on galactic scales (a bridge to the astrophysics field).

each quadratic term ½k_BT; 2⟨T⟩=⟨ΣF·r⟩

From assumptions to results

  1. Taking the long-time average of Σmr·r̈, bounded motion makes the total-derivative term average to zero, giving 2⟨T⟩=⟨ΣF·r⟩.
  2. For an ideal gas, the wall forces average to 3pV.
  3. The harmonic oscillator gives ⟨T⟩=⟨V⟩.
  4. Inverse-square gravitation gives 2⟨T⟩=−⟨V⟩.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Example and check

The 3 translational quadratic terms of a monatomic ideal gas give ⟨T⟩=3/2·Nk_BT, and substituting into the virial gives pV=Nk_BT; for a one-dimensional harmonic oscillator ⟨T⟩=⟨V⟩=½k_BT and the total energy is k_BT.

Range of applicability

Equipartition requires k_BT to be far larger than the level spacing; low-temperature quantum freezing invalidates it (see the heat-capacity staircase lesson).

Independent calculation

Show solution

Kinetic and potential energy each contribute ½k_BT, for a total of 1 k_BT.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the room-temperature heat capacity of diamond below 3R?

Check the transfer-problem solution

Carbon has a high Debye temperature (about 2200 K); at room temperature not all acoustic modes are fully excited, so the Dulong–Petit value is not reached.
