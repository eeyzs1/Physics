Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

The Skeleton of Statistical Mechanics: Microstates, Entropy, and the Three Ensembles

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: probability and combinatorics; the partition-function lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Statistical mechanics writes macroscopic quantities as averages over microstates: an isolated system uses the microcanonical ensemble (equal a priori probabilities), a system in contact with a heat bath uses the canonical ensemble, and one that can also exchange particles uses the grand canonical ensemble; the chemical potential is precisely the "price of a particle" introduced by the grand canonical ensemble.

S=k_B·lnΩ; Z=Σe^(−βE); Ξ=Σ_N z^N·Z_N, z=e^(βμ)

From assumptions to results

  1. The equal-a-priori-probability principle gives the microcanonical distribution and defines the Boltzmann entropy.
  2. Coupling the system to a heat bath and expanding the number of bath microstates to first order in a Taylor series gives the weight e^(−βE), i.e. the canonical distribution.
  3. Allowing particle exchange as well, the same argument gives z^N·e^(−βE), i.e. the grand canonical distribution.
  4. The multipliers β and μ are fixed by the bath's entropy and particle-number conservation.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Example and check

Two-level system: Z=1+e^(−βε), ⟨E⟩=−∂lnZ/∂β; for a monatomic ideal gas the single-particle partition function is Z₁=V/λ_T³ (λ_T=h/√(2πmk_BT)), giving F=−k_BT·lnZ₁^N and pV=Nk_BT; in the grand canonical ensemble ⟨N⟩=z·∂lnΞ/∂z and the fluctuations ∝√N.

Range of applicability

The equal-a-priori-probability principle applies to equilibrium isolated systems; in small systems (nanoparticles, mesoscopic circuits) the particle-number and energy fluctuations cannot be neglected.

Independent calculation

Show solution

p=e^(−βε)/(1+e^(−βε))=1/(3+1)=0.25.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the chemical potential of a photon gas zero?

Check the transfer-problem solution

Photon number is not conserved — there is no constraint of fixed N; absorption and emission freely adjust the particle number until the free energy is minimized, so equilibrium produces no μ.

σ_N=√⟨N⟩=20, 20/400=5%.
