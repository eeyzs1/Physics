Entropy and the Second Law

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the first law of thermodynamics; the concept of a reversible process. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Entropy is a function of state: along a reversible path it is obtained by integrating δQ/T. The second law constrains the direction and the upper limit of converting heat into work, and the entropy of an isolated system never decreases.

dS=δQ_rev/T; isolated system ΔS≥0

From assumptions to results

  1. From Carnot's theorem, construct the state function S and show that the integral along a reversible path is independent of the path.
  2. For an irreversible process the total entropy change is strictly positive.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Example and check

100 J of heat transferred directly from a 400 K reservoir to a 300 K reservoir: ΔS=−100/400+100/300≈+0.083 J/K>0; the process is spontaneous but irreversible.

Range of applicability

Entropy must be computed along a reversible path; the entropy of an open system itself may decrease, but the total entropy of system plus environment remains non-negative.

Independent calculation

Show solution

−400/500+400/400=−0.8+1.0=0.2 J/K.

Transfer and counterexample

A reversible heat engine transfers this 400 J between the same two reservoirs; what is the total entropy change?

Check the transfer-problem solution

0; the heat rejected is 400×400/500=320 J, and −0.8+320/400=0.

Advanced self-test

One step more than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; on review days 1, 3, and 7, switch to this problem to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

−800/600+800/300=−1.33+2.67=1.33 J/K.

Before moving to the next stage

  1. Close the page and explain to an imaginary classmate the assumptions, derivation, and conclusions of this lesson's model.
  2. Find a situation outside the range of applicability and determine which term of the formula fails first.
  3. Within two days, redo both self-tests without looking at the solutions; for each step you get wrong, return to the corresponding section.
