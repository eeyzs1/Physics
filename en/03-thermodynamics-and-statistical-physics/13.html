The Molecular Partition Function and the Heat-Capacity Staircase

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the partition-function lesson; the concept of equipartition of energy. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The single-molecule partition function approximately factorizes into the product of translational, rotational, and vibrational contributions (when the energy-level scales are widely separated). The heat capacity of a diatomic gas rises with temperature in steps: translation is already fully excited at room temperature, rotation contributes k_B above the rotational temperature, and vibration only unfreezes at several thousand K — the heat-capacity staircase directly measures the level spacings.

Z≈Z_trans·Z_rot·Z_vib; C_V staircase 3/2R→5/2R→7/2R

From assumptions to results

  1. For translation, integrating over the density of states of a three-dimensional box gives Z_trans=V/λ_T³.
  2. For rotation, summing the rigid-rotor levels gives the high-temperature limit Z_rot≈T/(σθ_rot) (σ is the symmetry number, θ_rot=ℏ²/2Ik_B).
  3. For vibration, summing the geometric series of the harmonic oscillator gives Z_vib = e^(−θ_vib/2T) / (1−e^(−θ_vib/T)).
  4. Differentiating the energy of each degree of freedom with respect to T gives the classical limit, from which the equipartition theorem emerges.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Example and check

For N₂, θ_rot≈2.9 K and θ_vib≈3390 K: at 300 K the heat capacity is close to 5/2R (vibration frozen out), and at 3000 K it crosses over toward 7/2R; for hydrogen θ_rot≈85 K, so at low temperature rotation freezes out first.

Range of applicability

The rigid-rotor and harmonic-oscillator approximations; at high excitation anharmonicity makes the levels dense, and electronic states join in only at extremely high temperatures.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Translation 3/2 plus rotation 1 gives 5/2=2.5 R.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does the low-temperature heat capacity of hydrogen deviate from 5/2R?

Check the transfer-problem solution

H₂ has a small moment of inertia (θ_rot≈85 K); at low temperature the rotational levels are not fully excited, and the heat capacity drops to 3/2R.

Advanced self-test

One step more than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; on review days 1, 3, and 7, switch to this problem to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

3/2=1.5 R.
