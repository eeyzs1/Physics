The Carnot Cycle and Heat Engine Efficiency

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: entropy; isothermal and adiabatic processes of an ideal gas. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The Carnot cycle consists of two isotherms and two adiabats and is the most efficient heat engine operating between two heat reservoirs; the efficiency depends only on the temperature ratio, not on the working substance.

η_Carnot=1−Tc/Th; Qc/Qh=Tc/Th

From assumptions to results

  1. For an isothermal process the work is W=nRT·ln(V₂/V₁); for an adiabatic process TV^(γ−1)=constant.
  2. The entropy gained in isothermal heat absorption equals the entropy given up in isothermal heat rejection, so Qc/Qh=Tc/Th.
  3. The work output is Qh−Qc.
  4. An efficiency exceeding the Carnot value would violate the second law.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Stuck? Show the key intermediate steps
  1. Step 1, isothermal expansion (Th): Qh=nRTh·ln(V₂/V₁).
  2. Step 2, adiabatic expansion: Th·V₂^(γ−1)=Tc·V₃^(γ−1).
  3. Step 3, isothermal compression (Tc): heat rejected |Qc|=nRTc·ln(V₃/V₄).
  4. Step 4, adiabatic compression back to the initial state: Tc·V₄^(γ−1)=Th·V₁^(γ−1).
  5. The two adiabatic relations give V₂/V₃=V₁/V₄; substituting yields Qc/Qh=Tc/Th and η=1−Tc/Th.

If you are still stuck, write down the specific equation before discussing it — that is more effective than rereading repeatedly.

Example and check

With Th=500 K and Tc=300 K: η=0.4; absorbing 1000 J delivers 400 J of work and rejects 600 J.

Range of applicability

Every step must be quasi-static; real engines have friction and finite-rate heat transfer, so their efficiency lies below the Carnot value.

Independent calculation

Show solution

1−300/600=0.5.

Transfer and counterexample

Used as a refrigerator extracting heat from 300 K and rejecting it to 500 K, what is its coefficient of performance?

Check the transfer-problem solution

COP=Tc/(Th−Tc)=300/200=1.5.

Advanced self-test

One step more than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; on review days 1, 3, and 7, switch to this problem to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

COP=Tc/(Th−Tc)=400/400=1.

Before moving to the next stage

  1. Close the page and explain to an imaginary classmate the assumptions, derivation, and conclusions of this lesson's model.
  2. Find a situation outside the range of applicability and determine which term of the formula fails first.
  3. Within two days, redo both self-tests without looking at the solutions; for each step you get wrong, return to the corresponding section.
