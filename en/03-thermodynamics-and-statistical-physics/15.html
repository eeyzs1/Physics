The Langevin Equation and Brownian Motion

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the concept of stochastic processes; Newton's laws. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A Brownian particle is subject to a fast random force and viscous drag; its velocity evolves according to the Langevin equation, which reduces to overdamped diffusion when inertia is negligible. The strength of the random force is tied to temperature by the fluctuation–dissipation theorem — the higher the temperature, the stronger the fluctuations.

m v̇=−γv+ξ(t); ⟨ξ(t)ξ(t′)⟩=2γk_BT·δ(t−t′)

From assumptions to results

  1. Averaging over v² gives m·d⟨v²⟩/dt = −2γ⟨v²⟩ + 2γk_BT/m; the steady state gives ⟨v²⟩=k_BT/m (equipartition).
  2. In the long-time limit the displacement variance is ⟨Δx²⟩ = 2(k_BT/γ)t, giving the Einstein relation D=k_BT/γ.
  3. Substituting Stokes' γ=6πηa gives the Stokes–Einstein formula.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Example and check

A 1 μm sphere in water (η=10⁻³ Pa·s): D=k_BT/(6πηa) = 4.1×10⁻²¹ / 1.88×10⁻⁸ ≈ 2.2×10⁻¹³ m²/s, with a root-mean-square displacement of about 0.66 μm in 1 s — connecting to the diffusion lesson in the biophysics field.

Range of applicability

The Markov white-noise approximation; memory friction and the active noise of biological systems require extensions.

Independent calculation

Show solution

D∝T, doubling linearly.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does Brownian motion become more vigorous at higher temperature?

Check the transfer-problem solution

Fluctuation–dissipation ties the random-force strength to k_BT; for fixed dissipation, the higher the temperature, the stronger the noise spectrum.
