Bose Statistics and the Photon Gas

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: partition function; chemical potential. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Bosons are not subject to occupancy restrictions and can accumulate in large numbers in the same state. Photon number is not conserved, which makes their chemical potential zero and yields the Planck distribution; at low temperatures an ideal Bose gas undergoes Bose–Einstein condensation.

n̄(ε)=1/(e^(β(ε−μ))−1)

From assumptions to results

  1. Counting the occupation arrangements of bosons combinatorially and maximizing the probability yields the Bose–Einstein distribution.
  2. Setting μ=0 for photons and integrating against the density of states gives the Planck spectrum and the Stefan–Boltzmann law.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Stuck? Show the key intermediate steps
  1. Photon density of states: with periodic boundary conditions the k-space density of states is V/(2π)³.
  2. Taking the spherical shell 4πk²dk, with two photon polarizations and E=ℏck, changing variables to energy gives g(ε)dε=Vε²dε/(π²ℏ³c³).
  3. Multiplying by the Planck distribution and integrating, ∫₀^∞ ε³dε/(e^(βε)−1)=π⁴/(15β⁴).
  4. This gives the energy density u∝T⁴, with the proportionality constant being the Stefan–Boltzmann constant; writing the energy density as a function of wavelength, u(λ), and setting du(λ)/dλ=0 yields Wien's displacement law.

If you are still stuck, write down the specific equation before discussing it — that is more effective than rereading repeatedly.

Example and check

With μ=0: a level with βε₁=ln2 has mean occupancy 1/(2−1)=1, and a level with βε₂=ln3 has occupancy 1/(3−1)=0.5; as the temperature is lowered the particles accumulate in the low-lying states; the condensation critical temperature of an ideal Bose gas is Tc≈3.31ℏ²n^(2/3)/(mk_B), of order 10⁻⁷ K for dilute alkali-metal atomic gases.

Range of applicability

Assumes non-interacting bosons; corrections are needed for strong coupling, finite size, or media that absorb photons.
