Maxwell–Boltzmann Velocity Distribution

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: ideal gas; the Gaussian distribution. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

In a gas at equilibrium, each component of the molecular velocity is independently Gaussian; the speed distribution follows from this. The three characteristic speeds (most probable, mean, and root-mean-square) differ from one another and serve different purposes.

f(v)∝v²·e^(−mv²/2k_BT); v_p=√(2k_BT/m); v_rms=√(3k_BT/m)

From assumptions to results

  1. From the equal-a-priori-probability principle (or the canonical distribution), each velocity component has probability ∝e^(−mv_x²/2k_BT).
  2. Changing to spherical coordinates in velocity space and integrating over the angles produces the 4πv² factor of the speed distribution.
  3. Extremizing f(v) gives the most probable speed; integration gives the mean and root-mean-square speeds.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Example and check

Nitrogen at 300 K (m=4.65×10⁻²⁶ kg): v_p≈422 m/s, ⟨v⟩≈476 m/s, v_rms≈517 m/s, in the approximate ratio 1:1.13:1.22; collision frequencies use ⟨v⟩, pressure and energy use v_rms, and escape criteria look at the tail of the distribution.

Range of applicability

A classical gas in equilibrium with no bulk drift; quantum degeneracy at low temperature and high density, or relativistically high temperatures, both require a different distribution.

Independent calculation

Show solution

v_p∝√T, √4=2.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does the collision frequency use the mean speed rather than the root-mean-square speed?

Check the transfer-problem solution

The collision rate is governed by the average of the relative speed to the first power; the root-mean-square is sensitive to v² and suits energy and pressure, not counting.

One step more than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; on review days 1, 3, and 7, switch to this problem to avoid memorizing the answer.
