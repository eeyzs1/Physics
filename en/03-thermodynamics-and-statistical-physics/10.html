Transport and Diffusion: The Kinetic Theory of Molecules

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: kinetic theory of gases; probability fluctuations. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A gas near equilibrium transports momentum, energy, and particles through molecular collisions. The mean free path links the viscosity, thermal conductivity, and diffusion coefficient, and is the entry point to nonequilibrium statistical mechanics.

λ=1/(√2 nσ); D≈½ v̄ λ (order-of-magnitude estimate)

From assumptions to results

  1. The mean free path follows from the number density and the collision cross section.
  2. Molecules crossing a surface carry the momentum or energy difference between the two sides, so the transport coefficients are estimated to be proportional to v̄λ.
  3. This is consistent with Fick's law of diffusion.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Example and check

Air at standard conditions: n≈2.7×10²⁵ m⁻³ and σ≈4×10⁻¹⁹ m² give λ≈6.5×10⁻⁸ m; v̄≈450 m/s (273 K; about 470 m/s at 300 K) gives D≈1.5×10⁻⁵ m²/s, the same order of magnitude as measured.

Range of applicability

The picture of hard-sphere molecules in a dilute gas; dense gases or long-range interactions require Enskog-type corrections.

Check the transfer-problem solution

The gas enters the molecular-flow regime: molecules collide mainly with the walls rather than with each other, and the transport laws change accordingly.
