Fine Structure, the Zeeman Effect, and the 21 cm Line

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximations break down.

Prerequisites: the hydrogen atom lesson; spin. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

The Bohr levels are only the first layer: relativistic kinetic energy and spin–orbit coupling give fine structure of order α²; an external magnetic field splits the levels by m (the Zeeman effect); hyperfine coupling of the electron and proton spins splits the hydrogen ground state into the 21 cm line — a cornerstone of radio astronomy.

Fine structure ∼E_n·α²; normal Zeeman ΔE=μ_B·m_ℓB; hydrogen hyperfine ν=1420.4 MHz

From assumptions to results

  1. The relativistic correction and the spin–orbit term H_SO∝(1/r)(dV/dr)·L·S are both of order (v/c)²; combined, the levels depend on n and j.
  2. In an external field the orbital magnetic moment shifts the levels by m_ℓ, splitting the transitions into π and σ components.
  3. The magnetic interaction of the electron and proton spins separates the parallel/antiparallel states by about 5.9 μeV, corresponding to ν=ΔE/h≈1420.4 MHz.

Example and check

μ_B=5.79×10⁻⁵ eV/T: in a 1 T field, adjacent m_ℓ levels differ by exactly this amount; the spontaneous lifetime of the 21 cm transition is about 10⁷ years, but the Milky Way contains enormous numbers of hydrogen atoms, so the line is widely used to map the Galaxy's rotation curve.

Range of applicability

Weak-field normal Zeeman effect (Zeeman energy smaller than fine structure); the anomalous Zeeman effect and the strong-field Paschen–Back effect require full angular-momentum coupling; the hyperfine constant depends on the nuclear magnetic moment.

Independent calculation

Show solution

ΔE=μ_B·B=5.79×10⁻⁵ eV.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the 21 cm line suitable for tracing cold neutral hydrogen rather than ionized hydrogen?

Compare with the transfer solution

The line comes from the hyperfine transition of neutral hydrogen atoms; ionized hydrogen has no bound-electron spin states, and ionized regions must be traced by other indicators such as recombination lines or free–free radiation.
