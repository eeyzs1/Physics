Rabi Oscillations of a Two-Level Atom

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximations break down.

Prerequisites: quantum states; complex amplitudes; ordinary differential equations. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

A coherent light field not only changes populations but also builds coherence between levels. Under the rotating-wave approximation and on resonance, a two-level system can transfer periodically between the ground state and the excited state.

P_e(t)=sin²(Ωt/2)

From assumptions to results

  1. In the resonant rotating frame, H=ℏΩσₓ/2.
  2. From U=exp(−iHt/ℏ), the amplitude is −i sin(Ωt/2).
  3. Its modulus squared is the excitation probability.

Please fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Starting from the ground state, a pulse area of Ωt=π flips the system completely to the excited state, and an area of 2π returns it to the ground state. Ω is in rad/s.

Range of applicability

Detuning, spontaneous emission, decoherence, and additional levels are neglected; the long-time behavior in an experiment usually does not maintain perfect oscillations.

Independent calculation

Show solution

sin²(π/4)=1/2.

Transfer and counterexample

If the light intensity doubles, with dipole coupling and everything else equal, how does the duration of a π pulse change?

Compare with the transfer solution

Ω is proportional to the field amplitude, and the amplitude is proportional to the square root of the intensity, so the duration changes by a factor of 1/√2.

Advanced self-test

This one takes one more step than the previous problem or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, switch to this problem to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

A π/2 pulse rotates the population onto the equator; the two states share it equally.
