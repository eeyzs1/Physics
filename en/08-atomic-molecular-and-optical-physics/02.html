Molecules Have More Energy Scales

Lesson goal: be able to explain "molecules have more energy scales" in your own words, and complete the calculations and validity-condition checks below on your own.

Prerequisites: quantum mechanics; optics. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

First, think about a question

Two atoms each have a mass of 4 mass units; what is the reduced mass in units? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Besides electronic states (on the order of eV), molecules have vibrational levels (about 0.05–0.5 eV) and rotational levels (about 10⁻⁴–10⁻² eV): E_vib=ℏω(n+½), E_rot=ℏ²J(J+1)/(2I). A simplified diatomic model treats the bond as a spring and turns the relative motion into the vibration of a reduced mass; the actual potential is no longer parabolic at large amplitudes.

ω = √(k/μ)；μ = m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂)

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Check the boundary cases

Molecular vibration is not an electronic transition inside the atom; different degrees of freedom correspond to different energy scales.
