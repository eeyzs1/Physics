Why Do Spectra Have Lines

Lesson goal: be able to explain "why spectra have lines" in your own words, and complete the calculations and validity-condition checks below on your own.

Prerequisites: quantum mechanics; optics. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

First, think about a question

An energy-level difference of 4 eV corresponds to a wavelength of roughly how many nm? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Bound states of an atom have discrete energies. The energy of an absorbed or emitted photon corresponds to the difference between two energy levels, so a spectrum can be used to work backward to the level structure. Which transitions can occur is further constrained by selection rules and coupling strengths.

ΔE = hf = hc/λ

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

A worked example with reasoning

Using hc ≈ 1240 eV·nm, an energy-level difference of 2 eV corresponds to a photon wavelength of about 620 nm. A larger level difference corresponds to a shorter wavelength.

Check the boundary cases

An electron energy-level diagram is not a picture of electrons orbiting the nucleus.
