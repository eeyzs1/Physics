Spectral Linewidth and Lifetime

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximations break down.

Prerequisites: Fourier analysis; exponential decay. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

A finite lifetime limits the coherence time and thereby produces spectral broadening. Frequency and angular frequency have different units; when discussing linewidths you must also state whether the FWHM of the intensity spectrum or some other convention is being used.

Δν_FWHM=1/(2πτ) (lifetime limit)

From assumptions to results

  1. If the excited-state population decays as e^(−t/τ), the field-amplitude envelope decays as e^(−t/2τ).
  2. The modulus squared of the Fourier transform gives a Lorentzian line shape with an angular-frequency full width of 1/τ.

Please fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

With τ=1 ns, the lifetime-limited frequency linewidth is about 159 MHz. Collisions, the Doppler effect, and the laser's own noise add extra broadening.

Range of applicability

A single exponential lifetime with no additional pure dephasing; real spectra are not always a pure Lorentzian line shape.

Independent calculation

Show solution

The linewidth is inversely proportional to τ, so it is reduced to 1/4.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does a broader measured spectral line not immediately imply a shorter lifetime?

Compare with the transfer solution

Doppler broadening, collisions, instrumental response, or pure dephasing may be present; the broadening mechanisms must be separated first.

Advanced self-test

This one takes one more step than the previous problem or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, switch to this problem to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

Δν=1/(2π τ)=1/(2π×2×10⁻⁹)≈79.6 MHz.
