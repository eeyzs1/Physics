Sub-Doppler Cooling and the Magneto-Optical Trap

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximations break down.

Prerequisites: the laser cooling lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

The Doppler limit is not the end of the road: polarization-gradient (Sisyphus) cooling uses optical pumping to make atoms repeatedly climb "uphill" in the light field, bringing the temperature down to the recoil limit; an anti-gradient magnetic field plus polarized counterpropagating beams forms a magneto-optical trap (MOT) that confines cold atoms in space — the entry apparatus for BEC.

Recoil limit T_rec=ℏ²k²/(2mk_B); MOT restoring force F=−κr

From assumptions to results

  1. Counterpropagating beams with opposite circular polarizations: the polarization gradient makes the energies of the ground-state Zeeman sublevels rise and fall periodically with position.
  2. Optical pumping always carries atoms to the top of the barrier, and spontaneous emission drops them into a valley — the Sisyphus cycle pushes the temperature below the Doppler limit.
  3. The MOT's anti-Helmholtz field makes the Zeeman shift detune from resonance with position, and the scattering force provides a displacement-dependent restoring force plus damping.

Please fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Cesium has a Doppler limit of 125 μK; Sisyphus cooling reaches 2–3 μK, and the recoil limit is about 0.1 μK; a typical MOT traps 10⁶–10⁸ atoms at about a hundred μK in a 10⁻⁸ mbar vacuum, and evaporative cooling then takes them into BEC (below 10⁻⁷ K).

Range of applicability

Requires closed transitions and careful magnetic compensation; light-assisted collisions limit the density, and BEC requires switching to evaporative cooling.

Independent calculation

Show solution

T_rec=(ℏk)²/(2mk_B)≈0.1 μK.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is evaporative cooling still needed after a MOT to reach BEC?

Compare with the transfer solution

Light scattering keeps heating the atoms and the density is limited; evaporative cooling selectively removes high-energy atoms, and after cooling and increasing the density, condensation occurs.
