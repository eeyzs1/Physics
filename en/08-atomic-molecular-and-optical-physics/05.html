Laser Cooling and Cold Atoms

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximations break down.

Prerequisites: photon momentum; the Doppler effect. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

In absorption–spontaneous-emission cycles the photon momentum gives repeated recoils; red-detuned light preferentially slows atoms moving toward the beam, cooling a gas to the μK range and trapping it — the foundation of quantum simulation, atomic clocks, and Bose–Einstein condensation.

Doppler limit T_D=ℏΓ/(2k_B)

From assumptions to results

  1. For a moving atom, red-detuned light is Doppler-shifted upward closer to resonance; the oncoming beam is absorbed preferentially, producing viscous damping.
  2. Random recoils from spontaneous emission provide diffusion.
  3. Balance between damping and diffusion gives the minimum temperature.

Please fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Stuck? Show the key intermediate steps
  1. Two-level approximation: the resonant scattering force is F=ℏkΓ·s/2, where s=I/I_s is the saturation parameter.
  2. For two counterpropagating beams the frequency difference is approximately linear at low velocity; the Doppler shift gives a viscous force F=−αv.
  3. Each spontaneous emission gives a random recoil ℏk; the momentum diffusion is D≈(ℏk)²·Γ·s/4.
  4. The balance is set by the fluctuation–dissipation relation k_BT=D/α, giving the limit T_D=ℏΓ/(2k_B).
  5. Sub-Doppler cooling to still lower temperatures instead uses optical-pumping gradient forces among the Zeeman sublevels of the ground state.

If you are still stuck, write down the specific equation and discuss it; that works better than rereading over and over.

Example and check

For the rubidium D2 line, Γ/2π≈6 MHz: T_D=ℏΓ/(2k_B)≈144 μK; sub-Doppler mechanisms can lower this by roughly one more order of magnitude, and a magneto-optical trap provides trapping at the same time.

Range of applicability

Two-level and weak-light approximations; trapping requires a magnetic-field gradient, and the deepest cooling requires evaporative cooling.

Independent calculation

Show solution

T_D=ℏΓ/(2k_B), with Γ=2π×6×10⁶ s⁻¹, giving ≈144 μK.
