Atomic Shells and the Periodic Table

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximations break down.

Prerequisites: the hydrogen atom; identical particles and the Pauli principle. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Electrons fill one shell at a time according to the Pauli principle; the filling order and the outermost configuration determine chemical properties. The structure of the periodic table is a direct consequence of quantum mechanics plus the Pauli principle.

Principal-shell capacity 2n²; the ground state fills from low to high energy

From assumptions to results

  1. Hydrogen levels have an n² degeneracy (doubled to 2n² once spin is included).
  2. Screening in many-electron atoms makes the levels depend on ℓ.
  3. Filling in order of energy, combined with Hund's rules, gives the periods and groups of the periodic table.

Please fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Neon has a filled shell, 1s²2s²2p⁶, and is chemically inert; sodium's 11 electrons leave one extra 3s electron, which is very easily lost — the origin of alkali-metal reactivity.

Range of applicability

Fine structure and relativistic effects are neglected; in superheavy elements the shell order is rearranged by relativistic effects.

Independent calculation

Show solution

2n²=2×2²=8.

Compare with the transfer solution
