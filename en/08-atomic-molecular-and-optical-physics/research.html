Research training · Suggested 1–3 weeks, adjusted to prerequisites and tasks

From Ideal Rabi Oscillations to Decoherence

This is a reproduction-type exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare model against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Starting conditions: be able to complete the written lessons of this field, and fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced by public or synthetic data, and sources must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Compute the time evolution of a resonant two-level system; add detuning; then compare the decay with an explicitly written-out dissipative model.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, variables, assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First do a minimal example that can be solved analytically or by hand, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions, and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

With resonance and no dissipation, a π pulse inverts the population; the total probability is 1; detuning reduces the maximum transfer probability.

If the results disagree, first check the implementation, units, and assumptions before discussing new physical interpretations. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

Population-versus-time curves, a pulse-area scan, the parameter conventions, and the model comparison.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: problem, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common pitfalls

Mixing up the definitions of Ω and Ω/2; treating the population lifetime and the coherence lifetime as the same quantity.

Extension questions

Compare the detuning sensitivity of square pulses and smooth pulses.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field paper-classification entry point find a relevant topic, and choose a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures and tables.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and computations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability before a review has taken place.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be re-searched and verified when the project is carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. Each item scores 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full score of 12 only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
