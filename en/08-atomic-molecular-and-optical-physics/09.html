Three Sources of Spectral-Line Broadening and the Voigt Profile

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximations break down.

Prerequisites: the linewidth–lifetime lesson; the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

The natural linewidth (Lorentzian), Doppler broadening (Gaussian), and collisional broadening (proportional to pressure) jointly determine a real spectral line, convolving into a Voigt profile — from the line shape one can work backward to the temperature, pressure, and lifetime.

Δν_D=(ν₀/c)·√(8k_BT·ln2/m); Voigt=Lorentzian⊗Gaussian

From assumptions to results

  1. Molecular velocities map through the Doppler relation ν₀(1−v/c) into a Gaussian line shape.
  2. Collisions interrupt the phase as a Poisson process, giving Lorentzian broadening proportional to pressure.
  3. Independent mechanisms convolve: the variances of two Gaussians add, and the widths of two Lorentzians add linearly.
  4. The mixed Voigt line shape is separated into its components by numerical fitting.

Please fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

The Na D line at 589 nm and 500 K: the mean thermal speed is about 1000 m/s, so Δν_D≈ν₀×1000/c≈1.7 GHz; the natural width of the same line is only about 10 MHz — thermal motion dominates, as in a low-pressure gas; in high-pressure discharge lamps, collisional broadening dominates instead.

Range of applicability

Assumption of statistical independence; under strong lasers, saturation and power broadening also appear.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does a low-pressure gas show mostly Gaussian broadening while a high-pressure lamp shows mostly Lorentzian?

Compare with the transfer solution

The collisional width is proportional to pressure: at low pressure it approaches zero and thermal Doppler broadening dominates; at high pressure the reverse.
