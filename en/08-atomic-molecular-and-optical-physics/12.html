Optical Pumping and the Principle of Atomic Clocks

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximations break down.

Prerequisites: the Zeeman lesson; the laser cooling lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Circularly polarized light continuously pumps atoms into a particular Zeeman sublevel (preparing spin polarization); an atomic clock locks onto a hyperfine transition: the 9 192 631 770 Hz transition of cesium-133 defines the second — the line Q and the signal-to-noise ratio determine the stability.

Second definition ν(Cs)=9192631770 Hz

From assumptions to results

  1. Circularly polarized light drives only σ⁺ transitions, while spontaneous decay can fall back into several sublevels; repeated cycles pile the population into the outermost m_F state (polarization preparation).
  2. In a cesium beam or fountain the microwaves are swept across the transition, and the crystal oscillator is locked where the fluorescence peaks.
  3. Stability improves as 1/(Q·√N), and the fountain geometry reduces transit-time broadening.

Example and check

The cesium fountain clock NIST-F2 has an uncertainty of about 1×10⁻¹⁶; optical clocks (Sr/Yb optical lattices) have reached 10⁻¹⁸ and are driving a redefinition of the second; the rubidium/cesium clocks carried on GPS satellites require relativistic corrections (38 μs per day, linking to the relativity lessons).

Range of applicability

Magnetic shielding and environmental control are required; the blackbody-radiation frequency shift is one of the main systematic errors of optical clocks.

Transfer and counterexample

Why are optical clocks several orders of magnitude more stable than microwave clocks?

Compare with the transfer solution

Optical frequencies are about 10⁵ times higher; for the same linewidth the Q is higher, and per-second stability improves as 1/Q.
