Building evidence from data

Lesson goal: be able to explain "building evidence from data" in your own words, and independently complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: Learning Preparation; basics of probability and statistics. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about one question first

Measured value 8, predicted value 10: what is the residual? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understand the model

Experimental design first fixes the independent variable, the dependent variable, and the controlled conditions. A fitted curve should be judged together with its residuals; if the residuals bend systematically, that may mean the linear model is inappropriate. Correlation by itself is not enough to prove causation.

rᵢ = yᵢ − y_pred,i

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The precise meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

Predicted temperature 20 ℃, actually measured 21.5 ℃, so the residual is +1.5 ℃. If the residuals are all positive at the high-temperature end and all negative at the low-temperature end, you should check the calibration, the model structure, and omitted variables.

Check the boundary cases

Do not pick out only the data that support your conclusion; outliers should be handled based on evidence about the instrument and the process.

Look back at the example: name at least one factor that was neglected, and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?
