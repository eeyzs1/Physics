Error propagation and covariance

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: partial derivatives; variance; correlation. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this same field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Once you substitute measured quantities into a function, the output uncertainty depends on the local sensitivities and on the correlations among the inputs. The same instrument or the same calibration constant makes the inputs correlated, so you cannot always add errors in quadrature as if they were independent.

u_f²≈∇fᵀΣ∇f

From assumptions to results

  1. Take a first-order Taylor expansion for small errors: δf≈Σ(∂f/∂xᵢ)δxᵢ.
  2. Take the expectation of the square, which yields a quadratic form containing the covariance cross terms.

Worked example and checks

f=x+y, with both terms having standard deviation 1: if independent, u_f=√2; if perfectly positively correlated, u_f=2. The difference x−y, in contrast, can cancel a common bias.

Limits of validity

First-order propagation applies to locally linear, small errors; marked nonlinearity or non-Gaussian distributions calls for methods such as propagation by simulation.
