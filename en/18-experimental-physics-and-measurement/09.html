Poisson counting and chi-square goodness of fit

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the fitting lesson; the Poisson distribution. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this same field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Counting-experiment data are integers: for a small mean use the Poisson distribution (variance = mean); at large counts it tends to the Gaussian. Fit quality is judged with the chi-square test: when χ²/degrees of freedom is close to 1 and the p value is not extreme, the model is compatible with the data — turning "it looks like it fit" into a statistical statement.

σ_N=√N; χ²=Σ(oᵢ−eᵢ)²/eᵢ; ν=N_data−N_parameters

From assumptions to results

  1. The Poisson distribution P(n;μ)=μⁿe^(−μ)/n! has variance equal to the mean, and the error on the counting rate is √n/t.
  2. Given the model predictions eᵢ, normalizing the deviations by the variance gives the χ² statistic.
  3. For independent Gaussian deviations, χ² follows a chi-square distribution with ν degrees of freedom, and the p value gives the probability of a fit "at least this bad".

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and checks

The statistical error on 100 counts is 10% (√100/100); a fit gives χ²=9, ν=10 → χ²/ν=0.9, p≈0.5, so the model is acceptable; if instead χ²=45, ν=10 → p≈10⁻⁶, you must suspect the model structure or underestimated systematic errors.

Limits of validity

Gaussian approximation at large counts; the expectation per bin should be ≳5; every parameter fitted consumes one degree of freedom.

Transfer and counterexample

Is a χ²/ν far below 1 necessarily better?

Compare with the transfer solution

Not necessarily: the errors may be overestimated, or overfitting may have smoothed away real structure; the fit passes only when the residual pattern and the p value are both normal, and values too small as well as too large are both suspicious.
