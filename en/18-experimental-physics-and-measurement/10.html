Fitting, likelihood, and residual diagnostics

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: probability density; function fitting; calculus. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this same field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

With independent Gaussian measurement errors and known standard deviations, maximum-likelihood fitting is equivalent to weighted least squares. Goodness of fit must be judged together with the degrees of freedom, the residual structure, and the error model; you cannot just look at whether a curve lies close to the data.

χ²=Σ[(yᵢ−f(xᵢ;θ))/σᵢ]²

From assumptions to results

  1. Multiply the individual Gaussian densities to get the likelihood.
  2. Take the negative logarithm; apart from constants, only χ²/2 remains.
  3. Minimize it to obtain the parameter estimates.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and checks

Three standardized residuals 1, −1, 2 give χ²=1+1+4=6. If parameters were fitted, the degrees of freedom are not the number of observations itself.

Limits of validity

When the errors are correlated, use the covariance matrix; at very small counts a Poisson likelihood may be more appropriate than the Gaussian approximation.
