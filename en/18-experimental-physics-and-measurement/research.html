Research training · Suggested 1–3 weeks, adjusted to prerequisites and tasks

Measurement experiment with a shared calibration bias

This is a reproduction-type exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare models against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to having produced original work.

Starting conditions: you can complete the written lessons of this field, and you fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. A real experiment may be replaced with public or synthetic data, and the source must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Generate data containing random errors and a shared bias; compare the independent-error formula with full covariance propagation; repeat many sets of synthetic experiments.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First do a minimal example that can be solved analytically or by hand, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, the sample size, or the model assumptions, and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

The shared bias does not vanish as the number of measurements grows; a difference can cancel a common additive bias; the predicted variance agrees with the simulation.

If the results disagree, first check the implementation, the units, and the assumptions, and only then discuss new physical interpretations. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

An error-budget table, and plots of coverage and of residual structure.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: problem, method, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must carry uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Misjudgments that arise easily

Shrinking all errors as √N after repeatedly measuring with the same bias; omitting the covariance.

Extension questions

Change the additive bias into a proportional bias and compare which combinations can cancel it.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field's paper taxonomy entry point, find a relevant topic and select a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one unresolved question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and calculations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; before a review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page has not fabricated specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched for and verified again while carrying out the project.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. For each item, 0 = missing, 1 = partial, 2 = adequate. A full 12 points only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
