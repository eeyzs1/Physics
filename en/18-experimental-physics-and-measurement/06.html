SI 2019: units defined by fundamental constants

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the metrology lesson; quantum concepts. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this same field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Since 20 May 2019, the kilogram, ampere, kelvin, and mole no longer depend on man-made artifacts or a particular experiment: they are defined by the fixed numerical values of h, e, k_B, and N_A — the units of measurement are from then on thoroughly "constant-based".

the kilogram is defined via h (6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J·s)

From assumptions to results

  1. The old platinum–iridium kilogram prototype drifted, and uniqueness was limited to a single artifact.
  2. The redefinition fixes the numerical values of the constants, so a unit becomes a quantity reproducible from any qualified experiment: the Kibble balance links mass to h through electrical power, and the X-ray crystal density method counts atoms for the mole.
  3. The uncertainty of a unit's realization shifts from the "definition" to the "realization", and keeps improving with technical progress.

Worked example and checks

Between 1989 and 2014, the international prototype and the official copies drifted in mass by about the order of 5×10⁻⁸ kg — the drift of artifact standards directly drove the switch to constants; the meter had been defined by the fixed value of the speed of light as early as 1983, and 2019 completed that line of thinking.

Limits of validity

Once the definitions are constant-based, the realization accuracy depends on experimental technique; everyday measurements demand far less precision than the realization uncertainty, so the societal impact is concentrated in precision metrology.
