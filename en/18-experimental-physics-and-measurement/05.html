Metrology, traceability, and systematic errors

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: error analysis; probability. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this same field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

A measurement result must be traceable to the SI units through a chain of calibrations; systematic errors are discovered by changing the method or cross-checking with a different instrument — repeating the same measurement cannot remove them.

u_c=√(Σ(∂f/∂xᵢ)²uᵢ²+2Σ covariance terms); U=k·u_c

From assumptions to results

  1. Propagation with the covariance matrix gives the combined standard uncertainty.
  2. Type A (statistical) and Type B (all other sources) components are combined in quadrature.
  3. The k=2 expanded uncertainty gives about 95% confidence under the normal approximation.

Worked example and checks

After the speed of light was fixed by definition, the meter is realized exactly from the second and c; the traceability chain of metrology-grade measurements is calibrated step by step up to the national metrology institute.

Limits of validity

Gaussian approximation; heavy-tailed distributions require a larger k or nonparametric methods.
