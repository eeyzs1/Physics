Bayesian inference and priors

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the likelihood lesson; the probability lesson. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this same field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Treat parameters as random variables: posterior ∝ likelihood × prior, with observations updating beliefs step by step; Bayesian inference turns the "error bar" into a complete probability statement and naturally accommodates systematic errors and model comparison — the other language behind "5σ discoveries" in particle physics.

posterior P(θ|D)∝L(D|θ)·π(θ)

From assumptions to results

  1. Bayes' theorem reverses the conditioning of the probabilities.
  2. The prior encodes existing knowledge (noninformative priors / physical constraints), the likelihood comes from the measurement model, and the posterior is characterized by sampling (MCMC; links to the Metropolis lesson).
  3. Credible intervals follow from direct integration of the posterior.
  4. Bayes factors perform model comparison.
  5. Conjugate priors (e.g. β-binomial) have analytic solutions.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and checks

Medical-testing example: sensitivity 99%, specificity 95%, prevalence 1% — the posterior given a positive result is only about 17%, the quantitative version of the base-rate fallacy; parameter estimation for gravitational-wave events relies entirely on posterior sampling (the standard procedure in LIGO science runs).

Limits of validity

The choice of prior affects small-sample conclusions (a sensitivity analysis is required); the computational cost is high.
