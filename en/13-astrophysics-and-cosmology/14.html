Advanced topic · About 30–60 min

The Friedmann equation and the contents of the Universe

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: basics of relativity; differential equations; the scale factor. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, return to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

In a homogeneous and isotropic universe, the scale factor is governed jointly by the energy density, the curvature, and the cosmological constant. Different components dilute with the scale factor in different ways, setting the expansion history of the different epochs.

H²=(8πG/3)ρ−kc²/a²+Λc²/3

From assumptions to result

  1. Define the critical density ρ_c=3H²/(8πG) and the density parameters Ω_i=ρ_i/ρ_c (the sum of the Ω determines the sign of the curvature).
  2. Matter density dilutes with volume a³, so ρ_m∝a⁻³.
  3. For radiation, besides the dilution of number density, each photon's energy is further redshifted by a⁻¹, so ρ_r∝a⁻⁴.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and cross-check

If the scale factor doubles, the nonrelativistic matter density becomes 1/8 and the radiation density becomes 1/16. Here ρ is the mass-equivalent density; the late-time Λ-dominated expansion proceeds approximately as a∝e^(Ht), accelerating, consistent with the observed Type Ia supernova Hubble diagram.

Boundaries of applicability

FLRW symmetry assumption; local structures, complex early-Universe components, and modified gravity require separate treatment.
