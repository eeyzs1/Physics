The chain of evidence for dark energy: supernovae, the CMB, and BAO

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Friedmann lesson; the lesson on the expansion of the Universe. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, return to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Dark energy was not invented from a single observation but is the convergence of three independent lines of evidence: distant Type Ia supernovae are dimmer than in a decelerating Universe, the CMB acoustic peaks show that the Universe is flat, and the baryon acoustic oscillations in galaxy clustering fix the matter fraction — together they point to Ω_Λ≈0.7.

Flatness ΣΩ=1; observed Ω_m≈0.3 → Ω_Λ≈0.7

From assumptions to result

  1. The distance–redshift relation of distant Type Ia supernovae is systematically dimmer above z≈0.5, consistent with Λ-dominated accelerated expansion.
  2. The position of the first CMB peak gives a total density close to flat.
  3. The BAO peak in the galaxy correlation function at a scale of about 150 Mpc/comoving pins down Ω_m·h.
  4. The intersection of the three lines lies at Ω_Λ≈0.7 and Ω_m≈0.3.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and cross-check

In 1998 two supernova teams independently discovered the accelerated expansion (Nobel Prize 2011); the Planck satellite's Ω_m h²≈0.14 and the "cosmic triangle" formed with supernovae and BAO meet at the ΛCDM point.

Boundaries of applicability

The chain of evidence supports the claim that "accelerated expansion requires a negative-pressure component", but the physical nature of dark energy (cosmological constant vs. dynamical field) remains unresolved.
