Inflation: solving the horizon and flatness problems

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Friedmann lesson; the CMB lesson. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, return to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

If the early universe underwent exponential expansion (inflation), the horizon and flatness problems are solved at once: today's observable universe was causally connected before inflation, and the curvature was diluted to nearly zero; primordial quantum fluctuations were stretched into the seeds of structure.

a(t)∝e^(Ht); N≥60 e-folds; |Ω−1|∝e^(−2N)

From assumptions to result

  1. The standard Big Bang cannot explain the isotropy of the CMB (the horizon problem) or Ω lying so precisely near 1 (the flatness problem).
  2. During inflation a∝e^(Ht) dilutes the curvature term by e^(−2N); N≥60 suffices.
  3. Quantum fluctuations of the inflaton are exponentially amplified, giving an almost scale-invariant spectrum (n_s≈0.965, as measured by the Planck satellite).
  4. Reheating at the end of inflation refills the universe with matter.

Worked example and cross-check

N=60 pushes |Ω−1| down to e⁻¹²⁰≈10⁻⁵²; the 10⁻⁵ amplitude of the CMB temperature fluctuations is just the magnitude predicted by slow-roll inflation; the gravitational-wave background of inflation models (the r parameter) is the current focus of observation.

Boundaries of applicability

Inflaton-field models are diverse; B-mode gravitational waves have not yet been detected.
