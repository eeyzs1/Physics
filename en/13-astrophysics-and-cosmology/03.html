The virial theorem and estimating gravitational mass

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: kinetic energy; gravitational potential energy; time averages. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, return to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

For a bound, sufficiently steady gravitational system, the time-averaged kinetic and potential energies satisfy a definite ratio. The velocities of the objects in the system can therefore constrain its total gravitational mass; the coefficient is affected by the structure and by observational projection.

2〈K〉+〈U〉=0; Mscale∼Rv²/G

From assumptions to result

  1. Take the second time derivative of the moment of inertia I=Σm r².
  2. Combine with the equations of motion to get Ï/2=2K+Σr·F.
  3. In the steady state the averaged derivative vanishes, and the inverse-square gravitational force gives the potential-energy term.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and cross-check

For a similar structure, if the radius is unchanged while the velocity dispersion doubles, the dynamical mass scale increases by a factor of 4; the effects of disequilibrium and anisotropy cannot be ignored.

Boundaries of applicability

This is a bound, steady-state approximation; surface terms, the mass distribution, and line-of-sight projection must be considered, and it cannot be applied directly to an arbitrarily expanding galaxy group.

Independent calculation

Show solution

M∼Rv², so the ratio is 2×3²=18.

Transfer and counterexample

When the observed mass exceeds the estimate from visible matter, which model factors should be checked first?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

Distance, velocity projection, whether the system is in equilibrium, the mass-to-light ratio, and selection effects; then discuss the matter distribution together with independent gravitational evidence.

Advanced self-test

One step beyond the previous problem, or requiring a unit conversion; when you review on days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.
