Research training · Suggested 1–3 weeks, adjusted to prerequisites and tasks

Matter- and radiation-dominated expansion models

This is a reproduction training: first reproduce known results, then compare the model against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to original research output.

Starting conditions: you can complete the written lessons of this field and fill in the mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced by public or synthetic data, whose sources must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Substitute the density scalings into the flat Friedmann equation; derive the single-component scalings; numerically integrate the two-component model.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First build a minimal example that is analytic or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: reproduce these first

Matter dominated: a∝t^(2/3); radiation dominated: a∝t^(1/2); at the epoch when the two components are equal, a can be predicted from the initial density ratio.

If your results disagree, first check the implementation, the units, and the assumptions, and only then discuss new physical interpretations. Agreement is no substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

A dimensionless expansion history, the asymptotic slopes, and a statement of the initial conditions.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, and references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must carry uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Starting the integration directly at the a=0 singularity; confusing physical time with conformal time.

Extension questions

Add a cosmological constant and compare the late-time acceleration with the early-time asymptotic behavior.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field's paper-classification entry point, find a relevant topic and select a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, a stable link, the research question, the key assumptions, and the core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write out at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send me the report and the calculations for review, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to evaluate them; do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability before review.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be re-searched and verified while carrying out the project.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. For each item, 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full 12 points only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
