Linear structure growth and the power spectrum

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the dark-energy lesson; statistical fluctuations. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, return to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Density fluctuations grow proportionally in the linear regime: δ∝a in the matter-dominated era, and suppressed in the radiation-dominated era; the power spectrum of today's galaxy distribution records the primordial spectrum and the transfer function — the bridge from CMB anisotropies to large-scale structure.

Linear growth δ∝a (matter dominated); P(k)=A·kⁿ·T²(k)

From assumptions to result

  1. Small density perturbations are amplified by the growth factor under the linear evolution equation, proportional to a in the matter-dominated era.
  2. Free-streaming damping and horizon entry modify the primordial scale-invariant spectrum through the transfer function T(k).
  3. Baryon acoustic oscillations imprint a characteristic scale of 150 Mpc in P(k) (the BAO standard ruler).
  4. Beyond the nonlinearity threshold δ~1, perturbations collapse into dark-matter halos.

Worked example and cross-check

From a=0.5 (z=1) to today, linear perturbations grow by a factor of 2; fitting the P(k) measured by galaxy surveys (SDSS/DESI) to ΛCDM yields an upper bound on neutrino masses; the BAO peak position has become a geometric probe of dark energy.

Boundaries of applicability

Linear approximation; nonlinear halo formation requires N-body simulations.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can the BAO scale serve as a "standard ruler"?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

It is the sound-crossing distance in the early baryon–photon fluid, with known physics (about 150 Mpc at the present epoch); measuring its angular diameter traces the expansion history.
