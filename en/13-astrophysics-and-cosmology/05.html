Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

The cosmic microwave background and the early Universe

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: expansion of the Universe; blackbody radiation. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, return to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The 2.725 K blackbody radiation left over from the Big Bang is the oldest light in the observable Universe: its 10⁻⁵ temperature fluctuations record the primordial density perturbations, and the acoustic peaks support the ΛCDM model.

T(z)=T₀(1+z); photon decoupling redshift z≈1100

From assumptions to result

  1. The photon temperature falls as 1/a with the expansion.
  2. At z≈1100 hydrogen recombines, free electrons drop sharply, and photons decouple from matter and propagate freely.
  3. Baryon acoustic oscillations leave characteristic peaks in the power spectrum.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and cross-check

The spectral peak of a T=2.725 K blackbody lies at about 1 mm (Wien's law); at decoupling T≈2.725×1100≈3000 K, precisely the recombination temperature of hydrogen.
