Accretion and X-ray binaries

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: gravitational potential energy; the lesson on compact remnants. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, return to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Accretion releases gravitational energy in a disk and, for a neutron star, near its surface. A black hole has no material surface. Standard thin-disk radiative efficiency depends on spin: about 5.7% for Schwarzschild and up to about 42% in the ideal extreme prograde Kerr limit, compared with about 0.7% for hydrogen fusion.

L=η·ṁc²; L_Edd=1.3×10³¹(M/M☉) W

From assumptions to result

  1. Gravitational potential energy gives only a scale estimate
  2. a standard relativistic thin disk has η=1−E_ISCO/c²
  3. Schwarzschild gives η=1−√(8/9)≈0.057
  4. balancing radiative force and gravity gives the Eddington luminosity
  5. a thin disk sums local near-blackbody spectra at different temperatures, not a single power law
  6. magnetic fields, coronae, and outflows change the spectrum and efficiency.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and cross-check

A 10 M☉ black hole has Eddington luminosity about 1.3×10³² W; typical accreting neutron-star X-ray luminosities are about 10³⁰–10³¹ W. Quasars radiate through accretion around supermassive black holes.

Boundaries of applicability

Steady thin-disk approximation; magnetic fields and relativistic jets require a more complete model.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is accretion more "efficient" than nuclear fusion?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

For a standard thin disk, Schwarzschild accretion radiates about 5.7% of accreted rest energy and spin can raise this efficiency, versus about 0.7% for hydrogen fusion. Real efficiency depends on the accretion state; radiatively inefficient flows can be dim.

Advanced self-test

One step beyond the previous problem, or requiring a unit conversion; when you review on days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

η≈0.05719, or 5.7%. This assumes a thin disk and approximately zero inner-boundary torque; the common 10% scale estimate is not the Schwarzschild value.
