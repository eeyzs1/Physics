An introductory model of the expanding Universe

Lesson goal: be able to explain "an introductory model of the expanding Universe" in your own words, and complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below independently.

Prerequisites: mechanics; thermal physics; optics; relativity needed for the advanced parts. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

First, think about a question

The emitted wavelength is 500 nm and the observed wavelength is 600 nm; what is the redshift z? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your reasoning changed.

Understanding the model

In the low-redshift Universe, the recession velocity of more distant galaxies is approximately proportional to their distance. Redshift is defined by the change of the observed wavelength relative to the emitted wavelength; in cosmology it should be understood through the change of the spatial scale with time.

v ≈ H₀d; z = (λobs−λem)/λem

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write down the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

Taking the sample values H₀ = 70 km/s/Mpc and a distance of 10 Mpc, the approximate recession velocity is 700 km/s. The numbers here are for practice and do not represent the latest parameter measurements. At high redshift, v = cz cannot be used directly.

Check the boundary cases

The expansion of the Universe does not mean that all bound systems expand in the same proportion along with it.

Look back at the example: name at least one neglected factor and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After one condition is changed, does the original formula still apply?
