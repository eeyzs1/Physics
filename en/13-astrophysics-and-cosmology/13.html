Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

The life of a star: beyond the main sequence and compact remnants

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the stellar-structure lesson; the concept of a degenerate Fermi gas. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, return to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Once the nuclear fuel is exhausted, the contest between gravity and degeneracy pressure decides the outcome: low-mass stars blow off a planetary nebula and leave a white dwarf, while massive stars go through core-collapse supernovae and leave a neutron star or black hole; the Chandrasekhar limit draws the boundary between white dwarfs and deeper collapse.

M_Ch≈1.4 M_☉; neutron-star radius about 10 km

From assumptions to result

  1. Electron degeneracy pressure p∝n^(5/3) (nonrelativistic) resists gravity.
  2. Increasing mass makes the electrons relativistic and softens the degeneracy pressure to p∝n^(4/3), producing a limiting mass M_Ch≈5.83/μ_e²·M_☉≈1.4 M_☉.
  3. Above it, electron capture triggers collapse and a core-collapse supernova; neutron degeneracy takes over at nuclear density and yields a star of about 10 km, and at still higher mass collapse proceeds to a black hole.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and cross-check

After about 10 billion years on the main sequence, the Sun passes through the red-giant branch and the helium flash and becomes a white dwarf of about 0.6 M_☉ (Earth-sized); a 25 M☉ star's iron core collapses into a Type II supernova with peak luminosity up to 10⁹ L_☉, leaving a neutron star (magnetic fields up to 10⁸ T) or a black hole; Type Ia supernovae are thermonuclear explosions of white dwarfs accreting up to M_Ch.

Boundaries of applicability

Simplified single-star evolution; binary interactions, metallicity, and wind mass loss significantly modify the tracks.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can Type Ia supernovae serve as standard candles while Type II cannot?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

Type Ia are ignited at the Chandrasekhar limit, so energy and luminosity are nearly fixed; Type II energies vary widely with the progenitor and envelope and require indirect calibration.
