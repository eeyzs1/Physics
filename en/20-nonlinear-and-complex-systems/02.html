Lyapunov exponents and predictability

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: logarithms; iterated maps; numerical error. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A key feature of chaos is that perturbations of nearby orbits grow exponentially over time. A positive largest Lyapunov exponent sets a local predictability time scale, but it is not the same thing as “looking messy.”

λ≈(1/N)Σln|f′(x_n)|; δ_n≈δ₀e^(λn)

From assumptions to results

  1. Linearize to obtain δ_(n+1)≈f′(x_n)δ_n.
  2. Multiply the factors together and take the logarithm, turning the product growth rate into an average along the orbit.

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

If λ=ln2 per step and the initial error is 10⁻⁶, the linear estimate gives an error of 1024×10⁻⁶ after 10 steps; once the error is large enough, the linear estimate saturates and fails.

Limits of validity

Finite-time estimates, initial transients, and floating-point precision all affect the numerical result; multiple orbits and convergence checks are required.

Independent computation

Show solution

e^(3ln2)=2³=8.

Transfer and counterexample

Can you confirm deterministic chaos from a single short, noisy experimental sequence?

Show the transfer solution

No — random noise can also produce irregular signals; you need surrogate data, model evidence, and uncertainty analysis.

Advanced self-test

This problem adds one step to the previous one or requires changing units; in the days-1, 3, and 7 reviews, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

e^(2ln10)=10²=100.
