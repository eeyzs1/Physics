Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Pattern formation and the Turing instability

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the bifurcation lesson; the concept of diffusion. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A uniform state can become unstable even in the presence of diffusion: in reaction–diffusion equations the combination of a slowly diffusing activator and a rapidly diffusing inhibitor produces stable spatial patterns (Turing patterns) — stripes, spots, and spiral waves, a shared grammar from cheetah skin to chemical reactions.

Turing condition: a band of unstable wavelengths

From assumptions to results

  1. Linear stability in k-space gives the growth rate σ(k).
  2. If σ(k) is positive only in a finite band of wave numbers, the uniform state is replaced by a periodic pattern (characteristic wavelength √(D_u·D_v)/rate).
  3. Bénard convection, slime-mold aggregation, and the spiral waves of cardiac fibrillation involve different physics but fall under the same symmetry-breaking framework (linking to the phase-transition and bifurcation lessons).

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Turing patterns have been observed in the laboratory in reaction–diffusion systems (the CIMA reaction); stripe changes in zebrafish mutants test the gene–Turing link; the convection rolls of Bénard convection are the prototype of a flow instability (linking to the fluids field).

Limits of validity

Pattern orientation in two dimensions and defect dynamics require nonlinear analysis; the biological realization is still under active research.

Independent computation

Show solution

A slowly diffusing activator amplifies locally while a rapidly diffusing inhibitor suppresses at long range; together they set a self-organized wavelength.

Transfer and counterexample

Ordinarily diffusion smooths out differences — so why can the Turing mechanism create patterns?

Show the transfer solution

When the two components' diffusion rates differ greatly, the cross-coupling makes fluctuations in a certain band grow instead — the uniform state is unstable to that band.
