Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

A taxonomy of bifurcations: saddle-node, transcritical, pitchfork, and Hopf

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the lesson on fixed points and stability; linearization. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Period doubling is only one kind of bifurcation. Normal forms write the common bifurcations as the simplest equations: saddle-node (fixed points are created or annihilated in pairs), transcritical (an exchange of stability), pitchfork (symmetry breaking), and Hopf (a fixed point loses stability and a limit cycle is born).

ẋ=r−x² (saddle-node); ẋ=rx−x² (transcritical); ż=(r+i)z−|z|²z (Hopf)

From assumptions to results

  1. Reduce a parametrized system near the bifurcation point by center-manifold reduction to a one- or two-dimensional normal form.
  2. The discriminant and how the Jacobian eigenvalues cross the imaginary axis determine the type: two real eigenvalues colliding (saddle-node), exchanging (transcritical), a symmetric solution losing stability in a symmetric system (pitchfork), and a complex-conjugate pair crossing the imaginary axis (Hopf, giving birth to a small-amplitude oscillation).

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The Hopf theorem gives a limit-cycle amplitude ∝√r and a frequency close to the linear oscillation frequency; laser thresholds, chemical oscillations, and neural firing models all start by locating the Hopf point; the pitchfork corresponds to symmetry-breaking transitions such as superconductivity and ferromagnetism (the same mathematics as the Landau model in the statistical physics lessons).

Limits of validity

Normal forms are a local theory; global bifurcations (homoclinic/heteroclinic) and transitions to chaos require global analysis of the phase space.

Transfer and counterexample

Where does the saddle-node bifurcation differ from the transcritical bifurcation?

Show the transfer solution

In a saddle node, fixed points appear or disappear in pairs; in a transcritical bifurcation the two fixed points always exist and merely exchange which one is stable.
