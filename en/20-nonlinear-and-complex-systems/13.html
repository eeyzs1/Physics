Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

The Lorenz equations and the strange attractor: from convection to chaos

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the bifurcation lesson; three-dimensional phase space. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A three-dimensional nonlinear flow is enough for chaos: Lorenz reduced atmospheric convection to three coupled equations and obtained the butterfly-shaped strange attractor — trajectories never intersect and never escape, and they are exponentially sensitive to initial conditions; a Poincaré section reduces the continuous flow to a two-dimensional map to reveal its structure.

ẋ=σ(y−x); ẏ=x(ρ−z)−y; ż=xy−βz

From assumptions to results

  1. Truncating the convection equations to three Fourier modes yields the Lorenz system.
  2. For ρ<1 the origin is globally stable; as ρ crosses 1 a pitchfork bifurcation produces two convection fixed points, and for ρ≳24.7 these lose stability, with trajectories confined to a fractal attractor.
  3. The Lyapunov spectrum has one positive, one zero, and one negative exponent; the positive exponent gives a predictability time ~1/λ.
  4. Slicing the trajectories with a plane of fixed z gives a Poincaré section.

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

With the classic parameters σ=10, β=8/3, ρ=28: the largest Lyapunov exponent is about 0.9 (per time unit), and an initial difference of 10⁻⁶ grows to O(1) after about 15 time units — which explains the fundamental two-week limit on day-to-day weather forecasting.

Limits of validity

A severely truncated model: the real atmosphere is an infinite-dimensional, forced, dissipative system; the Lorenz equations illustrate the mechanism rather than make quantitative forecasts.

Independent computation

Show solution

Three: x, y, z — a three-dimensional autonomous system is the simplest carrier of chaos.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is it said that on a strange attractor “trajectories cannot be forecast long-term, yet statistics can”?

Show the transfer solution

A single trajectory is sensitive to initial conditions, so long-term point-by-point forecasting fails; but the attractor's overall structure and long-time statistics (climate quantities) are stable — this is where weather forecasting and climate prediction part ways.
