Phase space, fixed points, and stability

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the logistic map; linear algebra. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The state of a dynamical system is represented by a point in phase space; linearization near a fixed point gives the classification into nodes, saddle points, foci, and centers, and the stable and unstable manifolds separate different long-term behaviors.

ẋ=f(x): near a fixed point ẋ≈Df(x*)(x−x*)

From assumptions to results

  1. Taylor-expand at the fixed point and keep the linear term.
  2. The real parts of the eigenvalues determine exponential growth or decay, and the imaginary parts determine rotation.
  3. Two-dimensional systems are classified by the trace–determinant plane.

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

In the damped oscillator's phase plane the origin is a stable focus, in the undamped case a center, and the inverted pendulum's upright position is a saddle point; small perturbations leave exponentially along the unstable direction.

Limits of validity

Linearization describes only the neighborhood of a fixed point; global behavior requires phase-portrait and manifold analysis.
