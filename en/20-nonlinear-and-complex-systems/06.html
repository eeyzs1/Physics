Solitons and integrable systems

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the wave equation; nonlinearity. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Dispersion spreads a wave packet while nonlinearity steepens the waveform; in special integrable equations the two balance exactly, so a pulse keeps its shape and recovers it after collisions — a soliton. Shallow water (KdV) and optical fibers (nonlinear Schrödinger) are both prototypes.

KdV: u_t+6uu_x+u_xxx=0; soliton u=2κ²sech²[κ(x−4κ²t)]

From assumptions to results

  1. Balance dispersion against nonlinearity to obtain the exact soliton solution.
  2. The inverse scattering method proves integrability and elastic-collision behavior: after one soliton overtakes another, each recovers its shape and speed.

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The KdV soliton with κ=1 has speed 4, and with κ=2 the speed is 16 — larger-amplitude solitons are faster and narrower, and after one overtakes a smaller soliton neither is deformed.

Limits of validity

Integrability is a special case; in generic dissipative or perturbed systems solitons radiate and decay.
