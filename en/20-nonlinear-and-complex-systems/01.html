The logistic map and stability

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: function iteration; derivatives. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A nonlinear discrete system follows a definite rule at every step, yet can produce complex or even chaotic behavior. The stability of a fixed point is determined by whether a small nearby perturbation is amplified or shrunk after one iteration.

x_(n+1)=rx_n(1−x_n); stability requires |f′(x*)|<1

From assumptions to results

  1. The fixed points satisfy x*=rx*(1−x*), giving 0 and 1−1/r.
  2. At the nonzero fixed point f′=2−r, so it is locally stable for 1<r<3.

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For r=2 the nonzero fixed point is x*=0.5, with local derivative zero; once r exceeds 3 the fixed point loses stability and a period-doubling bifurcation can occur.

Limits of validity

This map is usually discussed for 0≤x≤1 and 0≤r≤4; local stability of a fixed point is not a global conclusion about all nonlinear systems.

Independent computation

Show solution

1−1/2.5=0.6; the derivative is −0.5, whose magnitude is less than 1.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does a negative local derivative produce behavior that alternates while approaching the fixed point?

Show the transfer solution

Each step multiplies the perturbation by approximately a negative number, so the sign alternates; if its absolute value is less than 1, the magnitude still decays.
