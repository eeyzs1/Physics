Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Fractal dimension and the characterization of strange attractors

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Lyapunov lesson; box counting. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A chaotic attractor is self-similar under different magnifications: quantify it with the box-counting dimension D=−d lnN/d lnε; the dimension of a strange attractor lies between integers (the Kaplan–Yorke conjecture for the Lyapunov dimension), and the correlation dimension can be reconstructed from a single-variable time series — the geometric fingerprint of chaos.

D=−d lnN/d lnε; Kaplan–Yorke D_KY=j+Σλᵢ/|λ_(j+1)|

From assumptions to results

  1. Cover the attractor with boxes of side ε; the slope of the power-law count gives the capacity dimension.
  2. Reconstruct the phase space from a time series by delay embedding (Takens' theorem); the Grassberger–Procaccia correlation integral gives the correlation dimension.
  3. Sort the Lyapunov spectrum and assemble the information dimension from the Kaplan–Yorke formula.
  4. Surrogate-data tests distinguish chaos from colored noise.

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The coastline of Britain has D≈1.25, the Lorenz attractor D≈2.06, and the Hénon map D≈1.26; climate and electrocardiogram data are commonly screened for chaos using the correlation dimension and surrogate-data tests.

Limits of validity

Finite data and noise bias dimension estimates upward; estimates are unreliable for dimensions above 5.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can the whole attractor be reconstructed from a single time series?

Show the transfer solution

The coordinates of a delay embedding independently sample the same dynamical system (Takens' theorem), and the topological structure is preserved under the embedding.
