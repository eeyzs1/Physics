Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Bifurcations and the period-doubling route to chaos

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the logistic map. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A bifurcation is a point at which the system's qualitative behavior changes abruptly as a parameter is varied slowly. The logistic map enters chaos through a period-doubling cascade, and the cascade rate converges to the universal Feigenbaum constant.

x_{n+1}=r x_n(1−x_n); bifurcation spacing ratio→4.669…

From assumptions to results

  1. Fixed-point stability is decided by |f′(x*)|<1.
  2. When f′ crosses −1, the fixed point loses stability and a stable 2-cycle is born; the period then doubles in sequence.
  3. The ratio of successive bifurcation spacings tends to a constant δ independent of the details of the map.

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For r<3 the fixed point is stable; a period 2 appears at r≈3.0, period 4 at r≈3.449, and chaos sets in at r≈3.5699.

Limits of validity

This is a picture for one-dimensional maps; bifurcations of continuous-time systems, such as Hopf, require phase-plane and manifold analysis.
