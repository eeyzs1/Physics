The forced Duffing oscillator: from bistability to chaos

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the limit-cycle lesson; the bifurcation-taxonomy lesson. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Forced oscillation in a bistable potential (the Duffing equation) condenses the whole script of nonlinear vibration: bending of the amplitude–frequency curve (jump hysteresis), subharmonics, period doubling up to chaos — another major route to chaos in mechanics and circuits.

From assumptions to results

  1. A symmetric double-well potential oscillates within a single well under small forcing.
  2. As the amplitude grows past the barrier, large cross-well excursions appear.
  3. A frequency sweep shows a bent amplitude–frequency response and jump hysteresis.
  4. For certain combinations of parameters and forcing, the system enters chaos through period doubling or intermittency (echoing the logistic route).
  5. The Melnikov method gives an analytic criterion for the onset of chaos.

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Magnetic snap-through of buckled beams, tunnel-diode circuits, and ship rolling all follow the Duffing script; experiments on panel flutter and MEMS resonators have verified jump hysteresis and subharmonics.

Limits of validity

Deterministic chaotic regimes and noise-induced jumps must be distinguished; the fine structure of parameter space is intricate.
