Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Limit cycles and self-sustained oscillations

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: phase space and fixed points. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A nonlinear system can spontaneously enter a stable periodic orbit — the limit cycle: energy is balanced in a self-sustained way by the nonlinear terms, which is fundamentally different from the periodic motion of conservative systems. Heartbeats, chemical oscillations, and many lasers are limit cycles.

van der Pol: ẍ−μ(1−x²)ẋ+x=0

From assumptions to results

  1. At small amplitudes negative damping makes the oscillation grow, and at large amplitudes the damping becomes positive; the two effects balance to a single isolated amplitude.
  2. The Poincaré–Bendixson theorem constrains how closed orbits can arise in two dimensions.

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

As μ increases, the van der Pol oscillator changes from nearly sinusoidal to relaxation oscillations; cardiac pacemaker cells return to their rhythm automatically after a perturbation — a biological limit cycle.

Limits of validity

This is two-dimensional theory; in three dimensions and above, chaos and strange attractors can appear.

Transfer and counterexample

What is the essential difference between a limit cycle and the family of closed orbits of an undamped oscillator?

Show the transfer solution

The latter form a continuous family that is neither isolated nor attracting, whereas the former is isolated and attracts (or repels) nearby trajectories.
