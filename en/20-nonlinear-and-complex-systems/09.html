Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Hamiltonian chaos and the KAM theorem

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the phase-space lesson; the concept of canonical transformations. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Learning Preparation. Two introductory lessons within the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The motion of an integrable system lies on invariant tori; under small perturbations the KAM theorem guarantees that most tori survive, resonance regions break up first into island chains, and the chaotic layers grow with the perturbation until they merge into a sea — a unified picture from the stability of the solar system to particle-accelerator dynamics.

KAM: nonresonant tori survive under small perturbations; Chirikov resonance-overlap criterion

From assumptions to results

  1. In action–angle variables, the tori of an integrable Hamiltonian are labeled by frequency ratios.
  2. At rational frequencies (resonances) the perturbation accumulates, breaking tori into island chains and stochastic layers.
  3. KAM proves that for sufficiently nonresonant frequencies and small perturbations, the overwhelming majority of tori (by measure) survive.
  4. The Chirikov criterion estimates when overlapping neighboring resonance bands produce global chaos.
  5. In volume-preserving maps, chaotic regions and stable islands coexist (the phase portrait of the standard map).

Work out the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The solar system's long-term stability coexists with Pluto-like chaos (Lyapunov time ~10⁷ years); an accelerator's dynamic aperture is bounded by resonances and chaotic layers (linking to field 23); the chaotic-island structure of the three-body problem is the geometric expression of its nonintegrability.

Limits of validity

The theorem is nonconstructive; long-term diffusion (Arnold diffusion) is extremely slow and hard to measure.

Independent computation

Show solution

A sufficiently small perturbation destroys only the tori near resonances; the great majority of nonresonant tori survive.

Transfer and counterexample

Why do resonance regions become chaotic before nonresonant ones?

Show the transfer solution

Resonance keeps the perturbation's phase synchronized so that it accumulates over time, and the tori inside the resonance band are the first to tear into island chains.
