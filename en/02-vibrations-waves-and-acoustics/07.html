Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Sound radiation, ultrasound, and nonlinear acoustics

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new situation, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the wave equation lesson; the sound intensity lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

A vibrating object radiates sound waves into the medium: monopole, dipole, and quadrupole radiation decrease in efficiency step by step; ultrasound (>20 kHz) is used for imaging and detection; at high intensity, nonlinear effects accumulate into shock waves, and acoustic metamaterials use periodic structures to control sound propagation.

Radiation efficiency: monopole>dipole>quadrupole

From assumptions to results

  1. In small-disturbance linear acoustics, a pulsating sphere is a monopole source (volume change), the oscillation of a rigid body is a dipole (force), and turbulent stresses form a quadrupole (the main source of jet noise).
  2. Ultrasonic transducers use piezoelectric resonance to generate MHz waves; acoustic impedance matching determines imaging quality (medical ultrasound links to biophysics).
  3. In a finite-amplitude wave the propagation speed rises with the local pressure, so crests catch up with troughs and a shock wave forms.
  4. Periodic structures (locally resonant units) realize band gaps and a negative effective modulus.

Worked example and check

Jet-engine noise is dominated by quadrupoles — inefficient yet loud; B-mode ultrasound at 3–15 MHz resolves millimeter-scale tissue; underwater sonar propagates over long distances in the SOFAR channel; the low-frequency band-gap units of acoustic metamaterials are two orders of magnitude smaller than the wavelength.

Range of applicability

Linear-acoustics approximation; in real media, absorption grows as f².

Transfer and counterexample

Why is jet noise harder to suppress than propeller noise of the same power?

Transfer-exercise solution for comparison

A quadrupole source radiates inefficiently, but its strength grows with the eighth power of the flow speed and it is widely distributed in space — noise reduction must alter the fluid dynamics, not just apply sound-absorbing layers.
