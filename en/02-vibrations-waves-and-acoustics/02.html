The wave moves on — how does the medium move

Lesson goal: be able to explain "the wave moves on — how does the medium move" in your own words, and independently complete the calculations and validity-condition judgments below.

Prerequisites: classical mechanics; trigonometric functions. If the formulas are unclear for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Start with one question

A wave speed of 300 m/s and a frequency of 150 Hz — what is the wavelength in m? Note down your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

A mechanical wave propagates a disturbance and energy; particles of the medium usually oscillate near their equilibrium positions. The frequency is set by the source, the wave speed is determined by the medium and its state, and the wavelength adjusts accordingly. Sound waves in air are mainly longitudinal.

v = fλ

Order of use: identify the system and its conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The precise meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

A sound wave of frequency 340 Hz has a wavelength of 1 m in air where the wave speed is 340 m/s. If the frequency doubles at constant wave speed, the wavelength halves; there is no such necessary correspondence between sound intensity and frequency.

Check the boundary cases

Do not mistake the oscillation velocity of a particle of the medium for the propagation speed of the wave.

Look back at the example: name at least one neglected factor, and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?
