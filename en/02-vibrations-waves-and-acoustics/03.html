Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Damping, driving, and resonance peaks

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new situation, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: complex numbers; second-order ordinary differential equations. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

A periodic driving force continuously injects energy, while damping continuously dissipates it. The steady-state response is set by the driving frequency; the transient response is set by the initial conditions and gradually decays. The position of the resonance peak depends on the response quantity and on the damping.

mẍ+bẋ+kx=F₀cosωt；A=F₀/√[(k−mω²)²+(bω)²]

From assumptions to results

  1. Take the steady-state x to be a linear combination of cosωt and sinωt.
  2. Substitute back into the equation and match coefficients separately.
  3. The phase difference absorbs the dissipative term, giving the denominator of the amplitude.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

With m=1, k=4, b=1, F₀=2, driven at ω=2 rad/s, A=2/2=1 m; here ω happens to be the undamped natural angular frequency.

Range of applicability

Linear damping, a linear spring, and a stable steady state; the displacement resonance peak need not lie exactly at the undamped natural frequency.

Independent calculation

Show solution

A=F₀/k=2/4=0.5 m, the static-loading limit.

Transfer and counterexample

With ω=2 held fixed, double b — how does A change?

Transfer-exercise solution for comparison

Here k−mω²=0, so A=F₀/(bω); doubling the damping halves the amplitude.

Advanced self-test

This one adds a step or requires a unit conversion; on your day 1, 3, and 7 reviews, do this problem instead of the previous one to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

A=F₀/k=0.5 m.
