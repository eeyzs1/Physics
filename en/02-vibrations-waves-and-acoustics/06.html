Overview / Vibrations, Waves and Acoustics / Lesson 6

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

From forces on a string to the wave equation: wave speed, dispersion, and group velocity

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new situation, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Newtonian mechanics; partial derivatives. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Isolate a short segment of the string and write Newton's second law for it; the tension supplies the restoring force and yields the wave equation — this is where the wave speed v=√(T/μ) comes from. In the dispersion relation, the difference between group velocity and phase velocity determines the propagation speed of the wave packet and of the energy.

∂²y/∂t²=v²·∂²y/∂x²，v=√(T/μ)；v_g=dω/dk

From assumptions to results

  1. For a string element dx, set the transverse force 2T·∂²y/∂x²·dx/2 (small-angle approximation) equal to μdx·∂²y/∂t², obtaining the wave equation.
  2. Substituting y∝e^(i(kx−ωt)) gives the dispersion relation ω=vk (no dispersion).
  3. In a general medium, when ω(k) is curved, v_g=dω/dk≠v_p=ω/k, and the wave packet carries its energy along at the group velocity.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For a string with T=100 N and μ=0.04 kg/m: v=√2500=50 m/s; for deep-water gravity waves, ω=√(gk): the phase speed √(g/k) is twice the group speed — watch ripples on a water surface and you will see new crests continually born at the rear of the wave packet, moving forward through it.

Range of applicability

Dissipation-free, small-amplitude approximation; in nonlinear waves (shock waves, solitons) the superposition and group-velocity pictures fail.

Independent calculation

Show solution

c_p=ω/k=√(g/k)，c_g=½√(g/k)，c_p=2c_g。

Transfer and counterexample

Why does a wave packet on a nondispersive string keep its shape, while a deep-water packet deforms?

Transfer-exercise solution for comparison

Without dispersion, all k components travel at the same speed; dispersion gives the components different speeds, so the packet broadens and deforms as it moves.

Advanced self-test

This one adds a step or requires a unit conversion; on your day 1, 3, and 7 reviews, do this problem instead of the previous one to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

v=√(T/μ)=√400=20.
