Overview / Vibrations, Waves and Acoustics / Lesson 5

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Standing waves, boundaries, and the normal-mode frequencies of a string

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new situation, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: simple harmonic motion; the wave equation. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

A string fixed at both ends can sustain only certain wavelengths: the boundaries force nodes, producing standing waves and a harmonic series. Wind instruments, resonant cavities, and the longitudinal modes of lasers all share the same mathematical structure.

fₙ=n v/(2L)；v=√(T/μ)

From assumptions to results

  1. Superposed traveling waves must give zero displacement at both ends, forcing the wavelengths to become discrete.
  2. The tension and the linear density of the string set the wave speed.
  3. The sequence of frequencies consists of integer multiples of the fundamental.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

A guitar string with L=0.65 m and fundamental 110 Hz: v=2Lf₁=143 m/s; pressing a fret shortens the effective length to 0.5 m, raising the fundamental to 143 Hz.

Range of applicability

An ideal flexible string with rigidly fixed ends; stiffness and damping slightly detune the higher harmonics.

Independent calculation

Show solution

fₙ=nf₁; for n=3, three times.

Transfer and counterexample

If the tension increases 4-fold, by what factor does the fundamental change?

Transfer-exercise solution for comparison

√4=2 times.

Advanced self-test

This one adds a step or requires a unit conversion; on your day 1, 3, and 7 reviews, do this problem instead of the previous one to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

f₂=2v/(2L)=v/L=286 Hz.
