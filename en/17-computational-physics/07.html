Numerical eigenvalue problems: power iteration and QR

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: linear-algebra eigenvalue lessons; the concept of iteration. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two intro lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Eigenvalue problems are everywhere in physics (potential-well spectra, vibration modes, principal axes); once the matrix gets large you must iterate: power iteration captures the dominant eigenvalue, and the QR algorithm gets them all in one framework — the diagonalizations in research training use exactly these algorithms.

power iteration: v←Av/|Av|; QR: A=QR, A′=RQ

From assumptions to results

  1. Expand an arbitrary vector in eigenvectors; repeatedly multiplying by A makes the largest-|λ| component grow relative to the others (ratio |λ₂/λ₁| per step), and after normalization the iteration converges to the dominant eigenvector, with the Rayleigh quotient giving the eigenvalue.
  2. Orthogonally factor A=QR and iterate A′=RQ; the diagonal entries converge to all the eigenvalues, and shift techniques accelerate convergence — this is the core of LAPACK.

Worked example and check

Finding the ground-state spectrum uses a variant of power iteration: first estimate the largest eigenvalue, then iterating on (A−σI)⁻¹ (inverse iteration) converges quickly near σ to the closest eigenvalue; in this handbook's research training, diagonalizing a 500×500 Hamiltonian matrix completes in milliseconds on a laptop.

Limits of applicability

Iterative methods favor large sparse matrices or cases where only part of the spectrum is needed; for small dense matrices direct methods (Jacobi/Lagrange) are more robust.

Transfer-problem solution for comparison

Inverse iteration on (A−σI) (the inverse power method) amplifies the eigenvalue closest to σ into the largest — placing the shift at the bottom of the spectrum converges to the ground state.
