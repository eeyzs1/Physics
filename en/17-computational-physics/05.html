Molecular dynamics and ensemble sampling

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: integrating Newton's equations; statistical ensembles. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two intro lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Molecular dynamics numerically integrates particle trajectories, and macroscopic quantities come from time averages along the trajectory; constant temperature and pressure require thermostats/barostats to select the ensemble; equilibrium and sampling quality are checked through fluctuations.

Velocity-Verlet: x(t+Δt)=x+vΔt+FΔt²/(2m)

From assumptions to results

  1. Taylor-expand to second order and update the velocities synchronously.
  2. The step size is checked via energy drift.
  3. The Nosé–Hoover thermostat couples the system to a heat bath to realize the canonical ensemble.

Please fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Water systems commonly use a 1 fs step size: 10⁶ steps make a 1 ns trajectory; if doubling the step size causes visible drift in the total energy, the integration is unstable.

Limits of applicability

Classical-nuclei approximation and empirical potentials; quantum nuclear effects and reactions require path integral or QM/MM.
