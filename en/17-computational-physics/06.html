Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Runge–Kutta and stiff equations: getting time evolution right

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: the Euler-method lessons; Taylor expansions. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two intro lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Explicit Euler is only first order; RK4 combines four stages to reach fourth-order accuracy and is the default choice when there is no automatic control. But stiff systems, where fast decay coexists with slow evolution, call for implicit methods — stability, not accuracy, becomes the protagonist.

RK4 global error O(Δt⁴); stiff systems use implicit methods (backward Euler/A-stable)

From assumptions to results

  1. Combine the increment as a weighted sum of the slopes at four points in the interval, with coefficients chosen so the Taylor expansion matches to fourth order.
  2. For the test equation y′=λy, explicit methods have a finite stability domain (RK4 needs |λΔt|≲2.8); for stiff systems |λ|max·T forces step sizes so small as to be impractical.
  3. Backward Euler is unconditionally stable for Re λ≤0, at the cost of solving an algebraic equation at every step.

Worked example and check

A decay constant λ=−10⁶ s⁻¹ coexisting with an oscillation of period 1 s (chemical reaction + heat diffusion): an explicit method needs Δt≲3×10⁻⁶ s, so simulating 1 s takes a million steps; an implicit BDF method can advance stably with Δt=0.01 s — the practical value of stiff solvers.

Limits of applicability

Fixed-step RK4 has no error control; production code uses adaptive step sizes (RKF45/DOPRI5) and stiffness detection to switch.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is "stable but coarse" implicit stepping acceptable?

Transfer-problem solution for comparison

In stiff systems accuracy is set by the slow rates; the implicit large step damps the numerical blow-up of the fast modes while still tracking the slow dynamics correctly.
