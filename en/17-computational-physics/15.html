Monte Carlo error and correlated samples

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: probability; sample means; variance. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two intro lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Random sampling estimates an expectation value with the sample mean. The error typically decreases only as the inverse square root of the sample size; when adjacent samples from a Markov chain are correlated, the effective number of independent samples is smaller.

SE≈s/√N; N_eff≈N/(1+2Σρ_k)

From assumptions to results

  1. The variance of a sum of independent variables is the sum of the variances; dividing by N² gives the variance of the mean, s²/N.
  2. With correlations, the covariance terms no longer vanish, introducing the correlation-time correction.

Please fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

With 10000 samples but a correlation correction factor of 25, the effective sample count is about 400, and the error of the mean should shrink as √400 rather than √10000.

Limits of applicability

Requires stationarity, thorough mixing, and a reliable estimate of the correlation sum; with insufficiently explored multimodal distributions the error bars can be overly optimistic.

Independent calculation

Show solution

N_eff=9000/9=1000.

Transfer and counterexample

To halve the standard error, by what factor must the number of independent samples increase?

Transfer-problem solution for comparison

4×, because SE scales as N⁻¹/²; if the correlation structure changes, the effective sample size must be recomputed.
