Quantum many-body computations: DMRG and QMC

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: the reproducibility lesson; the statistical-ensemble lesson. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two intro lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Strongly correlated many-body systems cannot be fully diagonalized: the density matrix renormalization group (DMRG) compresses entanglement via the area law (connecting to the entanglement-entropy lesson), while quantum Monte Carlo samples via path integrals but runs into the sign problem — the twin gems of condensed-matter computation.

DMRG: matrix-product-state truncation; QMC: path integral/imaginary time

From assumptions to results

  1. Represent the state as a matrix product and truncate Schmidt values at finite bond dimension
  2. gapped local one-dimensional ground states typically obey an area law, while critical chains often have logarithmic entanglement
  3. QMC samples imaginary-time paths and its sign problem depends on model and representation
  4. common sign-free examples include the half-filled repulsive Hubbard model on a bipartite lattice and unfrustrated antiferromagnetic Heisenberg models
  5. PEPS/MERA address other entanglement structures.

Worked example and check

DMRG reaches 10-digit accuracy for the ground-state energy of the Heisenberg chain; QMC phase diagrams of the Hubbard model support comparisons with high-temperature superconductivity and cold-atom simulations; time-dependent DMRG tracks nonequilibrium transport.

Limits of applicability

The sign problem has no general solution; long-time evolution far from equilibrium remains expensive.
