Benchmarking integrators for the harmonic oscillator

This is a reproduction-type exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare models against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Entry conditions: you can complete the written lessons of this field and fill in the mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced with public or synthetic data, with the source clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Compare explicit Euler, symplectic Euler, and one higher-order algorithm with the same initial values and total duration; record the energy and phase errors.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, variables, assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First do a minimal example that is analytically or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must reproduce first

At small step sizes the trajectory approaches the analytical solution; the error scales with step size according to the method's order; long-term geometric behavior is distinguished from local accuracy.

If the results disagree, first check the implementation, units, and assumptions before discussing new physical interpretations. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

Runtime, energy error, phase error, and convergence plots.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must come with uncertainties or numerical-convergence evidence.

Common misjudgments

Comparing only single-step errors; comparing different total step counts without stating the computational cost.

Extension questions

Add near-resonant driving or a stiff scale, and check whether the methodological conclusions still hold.

Paper reading and peer review

  1. Find a relevant topic from the field paper-classification portal, and select a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and computations to me for review, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability before review.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be re-searched and verified when the project is carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

Question testable; theoretical derivation complete; results reproducible; error analysis credible; alternative explanations checked; argumentation and citations clear. For each item 0 = missing, 1 = partial, 2 = adequate. A full 12 points only means this project's report meets the bar, not mastery of the field.
