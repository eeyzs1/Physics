Root finding and optimization: from bisection to Newton

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: applications of derivatives; the concept of iteration. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two intro lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Solving f(x)=0 and finding minima are the daily bread of computational physics: bisection is robust with linear convergence, Newton uses derivatives for quadratic convergence, golden section needs no derivatives — pick the tool to match the problem.

Newton: x_{n+1}=x_n−f/f′, error e_{n+1}≈C·e_n²

From assumptions to results

  1. Bisection halves the interval each step; linearly convergent but never fails.
  2. Newton's method extrapolates along the tangent; quadratically convergent near a simple root but sensitive to the initial guess.
  3. Quasi-Newton and the secant method avoid second derivatives.
  4. Golden-section search for one-dimensional minima keeps a 0.618 fraction of the interval each step.
  5. Multidimensional optimization runs from gradient methods to quasi-Newton (BFGS) and global heuristics (simulated annealing, genetic algorithms).

Newton's iteration for √2 starting from 1.5 reaches 1.41421356 in two steps (the error squares each step); energy minimization of Lennard-Jones clusters is a typical use of quasi-Newton methods in physics.

It requires derivatives and a good initial guess; multiple roots degrade to linear convergence and need correction.
