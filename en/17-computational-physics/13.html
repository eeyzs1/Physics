Finite elements and spectral methods

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: the Poisson lesson; the special-functions lesson. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two intro lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Finite differences strain on complex geometries: finite elements partition the domain into elements and solve in variational (weak) form — the king of engineering simulation; spectral methods expand in global orthogonal functions and converge exponentially for smooth problems — the two major advanced discretization frameworks.

weak form + element basis functions; spectral convergence ∝ e^(−N)

From assumptions to results

  1. Multiply the PDE by a weight function, integrate, and integrate by parts, trading one order of derivatives for boundary terms (connecting to variational methods and Sturm–Liouville theory).
  2. Interpolate with low-order polynomials inside each element and assemble the global stiffness matrix.
  3. Spectral methods use Chebyshev/Fourier bases; the error of an N-term expansion falls exponentially, but it only pays off when the solution is smooth over the whole domain.
  4. Spectral elements and DG combine the strengths of both.

Worked example and check

Heat conduction in an L-shaped heat sink uses adaptive finite elements (refinement at the singular corner); global atmospheric models use spectral methods (spherical harmonics); 95% of commercial solvers in structural mechanics belong to the FEM family.

Limits of applicability

Mesh generation and stiffness-matrix assembly are costly; spectral methods fear discontinuities (Gibbs).

Transfer-problem solution for comparison

Elements conform to the geometry, the two sides of an interface are interpolated separately, and the weak form allows piecewise-constant coefficients — something finite differences cannot do.
