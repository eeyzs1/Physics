Finite elements and spectral methods

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: the Poisson lesson; the special-functions lesson. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two intro lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Finite elements assemble a weak-form discretization over mesh elements and handle complex geometry and interfaces. Spectral methods use global basis expansions. Analytic solutions with compatible boundaries typically give exponential convergence; infinitely smooth solutions can converge superalgebraically. Finite smoothness or discontinuities do not guarantee exponential convergence.

Weak form + element basis; analytic solutions with compatible boundaries typically give spectral error ∝e^(−cN)

From assumptions to results

  1. Multiply the PDE by test functions and integrate by parts to lower derivative order while keeping boundary terms
  2. choose element basis functions and assemble the discrete matrix
  3. Chebyshev or Fourier spectral convergence depends on analyticity, smoothness, and boundary compatibility
  4. spectral elements combine local meshes with high-order bases.

Worked example and check

Adaptive FEM can refine singular corners in an L-shaped heat-conduction domain; spherical problems can use spherical harmonics. Choose a discretization from solution regularity and geometry rather than assuming accuracy from the method name.

Limits of applicability

Mesh generation and stiffness-matrix assembly are costly; spectral methods fear discontinuities (Gibbs).

Transfer and counterexample

Why does FEM handle complex boundaries and material interfaces naturally?

Transfer-problem solution for comparison

Elements can fit geometry and material interfaces, and weak forms accommodate piecewise coefficients. Finite differences also have embedded-boundary, interface, and adaptive variants, with different costs and domains of use.
