Problem and physical picture

Hundred-million-point grids and long simulations rely on parallelism: domain decomposition and message passing scale to a hundred thousand cores; GPUs reshape throughput; scientific machine learning uses neural networks as surrogate models/operator learning, complementing traditional solvers under physical constraints (PINNs) — how modern computational physics works.

scalability: strong/weak; PINN: physics residual as loss

From assumptions to results

  1. Parallel solvers communicate boundaries via domain decomposition (iterative methods connecting to multigrid).
  2. Strong scaling is limited by the communication ratio (Amdahl); weak scaling is more realistic as the problem grows.
  3. Mixed precision and GPU acceleration have become the default.
  4. Surrogate models trained on already-computed samples replace hour-long simulations with seconds; PINNs write the PDE residual into the loss and are combined with data assimilation.

Worked example and check

Climate models and turbulence DNS run on top supercomputers (hundreds of millions of core-hours); AI weather models (GraphCast etc.) already match numerical models on some metrics; data assimilation merges observations into dynamical systems (connecting to the Bayesian lesson).

Limits of applicability

Reliability and uncertainty quantification of black-box models are active research directions; HPC engineering is complex.
