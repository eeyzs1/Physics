Magnetosphere, Solar Wind, and Space Weather

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: magnetic fields and plasma concepts; the lesson on drift motion. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The Sun continuously blows past the Earth with a supersonic plasma flow at about 400 km/s; the geomagnetic field is compressed on the sunward side into a magnetopause (about 10 Earth radii) and drags out a long tail on the downwind side; solar eruptive events and geomagnetic storms constitute space weather.

magnetopause r≈10R_E (pressure balance); solar wind ~400 km/s

From assumptions to results

  1. Balance between the solar-wind dynamic pressure and the geomagnetic pressure at the magnetopause gives the standoff distance.
  2. The solar-wind flow carries the interplanetary magnetic field; a southward B_z reconnects with the geomagnetic field and injects energy into the magnetosphere.
  3. Particles precipitate along field lines into the polar upper atmosphere and produce auroras.
  4. The ring current reduces the horizontal component of the geomagnetic field at low latitudes (the Dst index).

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

The 1859 Carrington event disabled telegraph systems; the 1989 magnetospheric storm tripped the Quebec power grid for 9 hours; modern power grids, satellites, and polar aviation routes all take protective measures based on space-weather forecasts.

Range of validity

An average magnetosphere picture; the microphysics of magnetic reconnection and the substorm cycle remain at the research frontier.

Independent calculation

Show solution

At 1 AU the solar wind is about 300–800 km/s, with a typical value of 400.

Transfer and counterexample

Why do geomagnetic storms occur mainly when the interplanetary magnetic field is southward?

Solution to the transfer problem

A southward B_z is antiparallel to the northward geomagnetic field at the magnetopause, reconnection is most efficient, and solar-wind energy enters the magnetosphere most effectively.
