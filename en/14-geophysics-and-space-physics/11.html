Zero-Dimensional Energy-Balance Model and Climate Sensitivity

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the greenhouse lesson; radiative equilibrium. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Shrink the Earth to a single point and take its energy budget: incoming = outgoing gives the equilibrium temperature; adding a perturbation ΔF (e.g. 3.7 W/m² for doubled CO₂) and a feedback λ gives the sensitivity ΔT=λ·ΔF — the minimal skeleton for understanding the conclusions of climate models.

ΔT=λ·ΔF; ΔF of 2×CO₂ ≈3.7 W/m²

From assumptions to results

  1. A no-feedback blackbody equilibrium gives a baseline sensitivity of about 1.2 K per 2×CO₂.
  2. The water-vapor positive feedback (Clausius–Clapeyron), the ice-cover albedo positive feedback, and cloud feedbacks revise λ.
  3. Observations and multi-model syntheses give an equilibrium sensitivity of 2.5–4 K (most likely about 3 K).
  4. The zero-dimensional model's μ value and the box heat capacity make the transient response slower than the equilibrium one.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

For an illustrative calculation, assume forcing ΔF=2.7 W/m² and warming ΔT=1.1 K. The apparent response coefficient is ΔT/ΔF≈0.41 K/(W/m²). These are assumed inputs, not a live estimate of current climate or an equilibrium climate sensitivity; ocean heat uptake and forcing history affect transient response.

Range of validity

Zero dimensions, no spatial structure; feedback signs and clouds remain the main source of model disagreement.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can the equilibrium sensitivity not be computed from the no-feedback case alone?

Solution to the transfer problem

Moistening by water vapor as the temperature rises (positive feedback) and the retreat of snow and ice (positive feedback) amplify the bare value of 1.2 K to about 3 K.
