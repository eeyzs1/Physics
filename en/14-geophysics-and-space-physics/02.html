How Heat Is Transported Through Rock Strata

Lesson goal: be able to explain “how heat is transported through rock strata” in your own words and independently complete the calculations and validity-condition judgments below.

Prerequisites: mechanics; waves; electromagnetism; thermal physics. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Start with a question

With k = 2 and a temperature gradient of +0.04 K/m, what is qₓ in W/m²? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Conduction in solids links the temperature gradient to the heat flux. The minus sign indicates that heat flows in the direction of decreasing temperature. Subsurface temperature is also affected by radiogenic heating, convection, and boundary conditions, so it cannot be extrapolated linearly from depth alone.

qₓ = −k·dT/dx

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

Take x pointing downward; with a temperature gradient of +0.03 K/m and a thermal conductivity of 3 W/(m·K), qₓ = −0.09 W/m², indicating an upward heat flow. The minus sign expresses direction, not negative energy.

Check the boundary cases

A high temperature does not guarantee a large heat flux; the gradient and the material's ability to conduct heat are equally crucial.

Look back at the example: name at least one neglected factor and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?

Now try it closed-book

Show answer and hints

Answer: -0.08. Find this lesson's formula, first write down the known and unknown quantities, then make the units consistent and substitute. If it is a proportionality problem, hold the other variables fixed and compare only the changed one.
