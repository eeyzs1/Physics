Post-Glacial Rebound and Mantle Viscosity

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: glacier concepts; viscous rheology. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

After the thick ice sheets of the last ice age melted, the crust is still slowly rebounding (Fennoscandia ~1 cm/yr): the rebound rate is controlled by the mantle viscosity, showing that on ten-thousand-year timescales the mantle behaves as a viscous fluid (convection is possible), which supplies the mechanical basis for driving the plates.

relaxation time τ≈η/(ρgL); η~10²¹ Pa·s

From assumptions to results

  1. The ice load bends the lithosphere elastically and squeezes the asthenosphere aside.
  2. After unloading, viscous flow slowly refills it, with an exponential relaxation time τ≈η/(ρgL).
  3. Observed uplift histories of ancient shorelines fit an upper-mantle viscosity of ~10²¹ Pa·s, with a higher value in the lower mantle.
  4. Viscosity is the “resistance to convection” and sets the rate of mantle convection and the order of magnitude of plate speeds.

Example and check

Ungava shorelines rose several hundred meters over 8000 years; the concurrent coastline migration changed the salinity of the Baltic Sea; GPS and gravity satellites (GRACE) now measure the unload rebound of Greenland, and viscosity inversion is coupled to ice-mass balance.

Range of validity

Maxwell viscoelasticity; lateral viscosity variations and hydration weakening are complicated.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does the rebound show that the mantle is “both stiff and soft”?

Solution to the transfer problem

Seismic wave speeds show rigidity (elastic behavior) on kilometer scales, while on ten-thousand-year scales it flows like honey at 10²¹ Pa·s — the viscoelastic timescale determines which face it shows.
