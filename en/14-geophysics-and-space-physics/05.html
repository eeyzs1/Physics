Atmospheric Circulation and the Greenhouse Effect

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: thermal radiation; fluids. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The latitudinal imbalance of solar radiation drives the Hadley circulation, jet streams, and ocean circulation (this lesson focuses on energy balance; circulation dynamics belongs to the fluids field); greenhouse gases absorb infrared radiation and re-radiate it, raising the effective emitting surface and making the surface warmer than the effective temperature.

effective temperature T_eff=[S(1−α)/(4σ)]^(1/4)

From assumptions to results

  1. Treat the Earth as a gray body in equilibrium: the incoming S(1−α)πR² equals the outgoing 4πR²σT⁴, and solve for T_eff.
  2. The infrared opacity of the atmosphere raises the emitting surface to the cold upper regions, and the surface warms accordingly.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

With S=1361 W/m² and α=0.30: T_eff≈255 K; the observed mean surface temperature is 288 K, and the 33 K difference is the measure of the greenhouse effect.

Range of validity

A globally averaged gray-body model; cloud, ice, and water-vapor feedbacks are complex.

Independent calculation

Show solution

T_eff=(1361×0.7/4σ)^(1/4)≈255 K.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the greenhouse effect not as simple as a blanket trapping heat?

Solution to the transfer problem

The heating mechanism is the lifting of the effective emission level by optical thickness plus downward infrared re-radiation, not simple thermal insulation.
