Earth's Layered Structure and Seismic Imaging

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: seismic-wave arrival times; refraction. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Body-wave travel times reveal the Earth's interior: crust, mantle, liquid outer core, and solid inner core; velocity discontinuities correspond to compositional or mineral phase changes.

ray parameter p=r sinθ/v is conserved along the ray

From assumptions to results

  1. In a layered medium Snell's law gives a conserved ray parameter; kinks in the travel-time curve and shadow zones correspond to velocity jumps.
  2. Shear waves cannot penetrate liquids: no pure S phase crosses the outer core, and the S-wave shadow zone proves the outer core is liquid (the SKS phase is itself still observed, since in its outer-core leg K it has already converted to a P wave).

Example and check

Below the Moho the P-wave speed jumps from about 6 to 8 km/s; the core–mantle boundary lies at a depth of 2891 km; the inner-core radius is 1221 km — the Earth's layering was established from these facts.

Range of validity

Anisotropy and scattering are neglected; fine structure requires tomographic inversion.

Independent calculation

Show solution

A liquid has no shear modulus, shear waves cannot propagate, and the speed is zero.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can the PKIKP phase pass through the inner core?

Solution to the transfer problem

Every leg of PKIKP (P, K, I, K, P) is a longitudinal wave, which can propagate in both the liquid outer core and the solid inner core; the direct seismological evidence that the inner core is solid is the PKJKP phase (whose J leg is a shear wave) and the splitting of the Earth's normal modes.
