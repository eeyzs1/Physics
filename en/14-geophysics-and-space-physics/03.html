Inversion Is Not Unique: Seismic Travel-Time Models

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: linear equations; wave speed; experimental error. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Seismic travel times are determined by the integral of slowness along the path. After discretization, the observations can be written as a path-length matrix times a slowness vector; a finite set of paths cannot necessarily determine the wave speed of every region uniquely.

tᵢ=Σ_jLᵢⱼs_j；s_j=1/v_j

From assumptions to results

  1. Split the path into the length within each grid cell.
  2. The travel time of each segment is its length divided by its speed.
  3. Sum the segments to obtain the linear slowness model.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

A path is 10 km and 20 km long in two layers, both with a speed of 5 km/s; the time is 2+4=6 s; a single travel time cannot determine the two unknown speeds separately.

Range of validity

The paths themselves are affected by the wave speeds; this linear form assumes the paths are known, and real tomography often requires iteration.

Transfer and counterexample

Does adding a smoothing constraint prove that the resulting subsurface structure is the true, unique structure?

Solution to the transfer problem

No; the constraint selects the solution that matches prior preferences, and one must report resolution, sensitivity, and alternative models.
