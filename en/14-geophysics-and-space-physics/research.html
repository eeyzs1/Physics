Research training · Suggested 1–3 weeks, adjusted to prerequisites and tasks

Non-Uniqueness in Two-Layer Travel-Time Inversion

This is a reproduction exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare model and evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Starting conditions: be able to complete the written lessons of this field and fill in the mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced with public or synthetic data, and sources must be clearly marked.

Research question and implementation steps

Build your own two-layer slowness and path matrices; start with one observation, then add independent paths; add known noise.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, variables, assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First do a minimal example that is analytic or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

One path is not enough to determine two unknowns; independent paths raise the rank; ill-conditioned path combinations still amplify noise.

If results disagree, first check the implementation, the units, and the assumptions, and only then discuss new physical interpretations. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

True values and estimates, the matrix condition number, noise sensitivity, and a discussion of resolution.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: problem, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Treating the regularized smooth image as the unique subsurface structure; not stating the priors.

Extension questions

Compare zeroth-order and first-order smoothing regularization, and show the influence of the prior.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field paper-classification entry, find a relevant topic and select a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and computations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; before a review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched and verified again while carrying out the project.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. Each item: 0 = missing, 1 = partial, 2 = adequate. A full score of 12 only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
