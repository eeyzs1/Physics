Magnitude, Energy, and Frequency Statistics

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the seismic-waves lesson; logarithms. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Magnitude is a logarithmic quantity: each Richter step of 1 corresponds to a factor of 10 in amplitude and about 32 in energy; the Gutenberg–Richter law gives an exponential frequency–magnitude distribution — many small earthquakes, few large ones — and is the backbone of global earthquake statistics.

log₁₀E=1.5M+4.8 (joules); log₁₀N=a−bM (b≈1)

From assumptions to results

  1. The local magnitude defined on the Wood–Anderson seismometer was generalized into the surface-wave and moment magnitudes.
  2. The moment magnitude is defined through the seismic moment M₀=μAD and ties into the energy scale.
  3. The energy–magnitude relation gives the slope 1.5 (a factor of 10^1.5≈32 in energy per magnitude step).
  4. The b value of the G–R law is ≈1, explainable by fault fractality and the stress state; a drop in the b value is often regarded as a candidate precursor of large earthquakes.

Example and check

An M9 releases about 2×10¹⁸ J (equivalent to several thousand megatons of TNT), an M6 about 6.3×10¹³ J — a millionfold energy difference within the same range; globally there is on average 1 M8+ earthquake per year and about 150 M6–M7 events.

Range of validity

Scale saturation and finite faults; probabilistic rather than deterministic prediction.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is it said that “earthquakes cannot be precisely predicted but can be statistically assessed”?

Solution to the transfer problem

Stress accumulation and release is a deterministic chaotic process, but the G–R statistics are stable, and seismic design relies on probabilistic seismic hazard analysis.
