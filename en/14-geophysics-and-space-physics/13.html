Coriolis Scales on the Rotating Earth

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: vectors; Newtonian mechanics; order-of-magnitude estimation. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

In a reference frame rotating with the Earth, the equation of motion acquires a Coriolis term. In large-scale slow flows it can balance the pressure gradient; in small-scale fast flows inertia usually dominates.

f=2Ωsinφ；Ro=U/(fL)

From assumptions to results

  1. Estimate the inertial acceleration as U²/L and the Coriolis acceleration as fU.
  2. Dividing one by the other gives the Rossby number. Ro≪1 signals that rotational effects are important.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

With U=1 m/s, f=10⁻⁴ s⁻¹, and L=10⁵ m, Ro=0.1. If the scale shrinks to 100 m, then at the same flow speed Ro=100.

Range of validity

This is a scale judgment; near the equator f is very small, so the geostrophic approximation must be treated with special care and not applied mechanically.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Ro=2/(10⁻⁴×10⁵)=0.2.

Transfer and counterexample

Why should the direction of the vortex in an everyday sink not be decided by hemisphere alone?

Solution to the transfer problem

At small scales, factors such as the initial flow and the geometry of the basin — inertia and boundary effects — are more prominent, and the Earth's rotation is usually not enough to dominate on its own.
