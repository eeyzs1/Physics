Plate Tectonics and Geochronology

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: radioactive decay; the seismic-waves lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The lithosphere is divided into a dozen or so rigid plates moving over the asthenosphere: creation at mid-ocean ridges, destruction at subduction zones, and offset along transform faults (the Wilson cycle); radioactive decay provides the absolute time scale of this evolution, and the Earth is 4.567 billion years old.

t=(1/λ)·ln(1+D/P) (when no initial daughter is present)

From assumptions to results

  1. Marine magnetic stripes record seafloor spreading through the geomagnetic reversal history (a few cm/yr).
  2. Earthquake belts trace the plate boundaries, and Benioff zones indicate subduction.
  3. Geochronology relies on the accumulation of daughter nuclides from the decay of parents: minerals below their closure temperature act like an hourglass, the two U–Pb systems check each other, and the isochron method removes the interference of initial daughters.

Example and check

The Atlantic opens at about 2.5 cm/yr and began rifting about 180 million years ago, matching the ages of the oldest oceanic crust on its two sides; the oldest zircon U–Pb age is about 4.4 billion years; K–Ar covers million-year scales, and ¹⁴C has a half-life of 5730 years with an upper applicability limit of about 50,000 years.

Range of validity

The driving forces of the plates (slab pull vs. mantle plumes) are still under study; each geochronological method has a closure temperature and a contamination limit.

Transfer and counterexample

Why do the magnetic stripes prove both seafloor spreading and geomagnetic reversals at once?

Solution to the transfer problem

The symmetric, alternating normal/reversed magnetized stripes on the two sides of a mid-ocean ridge can only be produced jointly by “continuous two-sided spreading + periodic reversals of the field”; a single mechanism cannot produce a symmetric sequence.
