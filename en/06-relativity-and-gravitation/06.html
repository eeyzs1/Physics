The Einstein Equation: Matter Curves Spacetime

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: the differential geometry lesson; conservation of energy. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

The field equation links spacetime curvature to energy and momentum: matter tells spacetime how to curve, and spacetime tells matter how to move. The Newtonian limit recovers the Poisson equation, and the vacuum still admits nontrivial solutions.

G_μν+Λg_μν=(8πG/c⁴)·T_μν

From assumptions to results

  1. The left-hand side is the unique rank-2 tensor built from the second derivatives of the metric whose covariant divergence vanishes (the Bianchi identity guarantees ∇^μG_μν=0, consistent with ∇^μT_μν=0).
  2. In the weak-field, slow-motion limit it reduces to ∇²Φ=4πGρ, which fixes the coefficient 8πG/c⁴.
  3. The Λ term represents the vacuum energy density.
  4. The vacuum equation with T=0 still has the Schwarzschild black hole and gravitational wave solutions.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

The depth of the potential well at the Sun’s surface is GM/(c²R)≈2×10⁻⁶ (weak field), about 0.2 at the surface of a neutron star, and about 1 at a black hole horizon — one and the same equation covers everything from planets to black holes; the observed value of Λ corresponds to a density of about 6×10⁻²⁷ kg/m³ (linking to the cosmology field).

Range of validity

A classical theory; singularities and the Planck scale require quantum gravity.
