Understanding Gravitational Time Dilation from the Metric

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: proper time; the line element; basic calculus. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

In general relativity the metric determines the spacetime interval between neighboring events. In the Schwarzschild coordinates of the static, spherically symmetric vacuum exterior, how the rate of a clock at a fixed spatial position relates to coordinate time depends on the radius.

dτ=√(1−r_s/r)dt；r_s=2GM/c²

From assumptions to results

  1. Set dr=dθ=dφ=0 in the Schwarzschild line element.
  2. Compare the remaining time term with the proper-time line element of a clock at rest, obtaining the square-root factor.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

If r=4r_s, the time elapsed on a local clock at rest is √(3/4) times the corresponding coordinate time. Coordinate time can be tied to a clock at rest far away.

Range of validity

Spherically symmetric, non-rotating, vacuum exterior with r>r_s; holding a fixed position requires being held at rest, and this formula cannot be applied directly to the whole course of free fall.

Independent calculation

Show solution

1−r_s/r=1−1/4=0.75.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does this formula not mean that a freely falling observer’s own clock stops at the horizon?

Check the transfer solution

It compares a clock at rest at a fixed radius with coordinate time; a freely falling body follows a different worldline and cannot maintain such a state of rest at the horizon.

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; switch to this problem when reviewing on days 1, 3, and 7 to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

Gravitational redshift about +45 μs/day and the velocity effect about −7 μs/day, for a net of about +38 μs.
