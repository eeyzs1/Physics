Gravitational Lensing: Light Rays Bent into Images

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: the classical tests lesson; the concept of a lens. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Massive bodies bend the light of background sources and focus it into images: strong lensing produces arcs and multiple images, weak lensing maps the dark matter distribution through statistical shear, and microlensing identifies dark compact objects and exoplanets.

Einstein radius θ_E=√(4GM·D_LS/(c²·D_L·D_S))

From assumptions to results

  1. The point-mass deflection α=4GM/(c²b), combined with a geometric factor, gives the image equation θ²−βθ−θ_E²=0.
  2. For β<θ_E there are two images; for β=0 a complete Einstein ring forms.
  3. Strong-lensing arcs in galaxy clusters calibrate the central mass; weak lensing measures statistical shear from correlations of background galaxy shapes; microlensing is identified by spikes in brightness.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

The Sun’s Einstein radius for a source at infinity is about 0.7″; the 4×10⁶ M☉ black hole at the Galactic center gives about 0.02″ for a source at 1 kpc; arc analysis of the galaxy cluster CL0024 yields a map of the cluster’s dark matter distribution.

Range of validity

Thin-lens approximation; strong fields and multiplane lensing require numerical ray tracing.

Independent calculation

Show solution

The Einstein ring radius is exactly θ_E.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can weak lensing “see” dark matter?

Check the transfer solution

Shear is caused by the mass distribution and does not care whether the matter glows — statistical shape distortions reconstruct the mass map.
