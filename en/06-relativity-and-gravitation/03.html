Four-Momentum and Collision Invariants

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: special relativity; conservation of energy and momentum. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Different inertial frames distribute total energy and total momentum differently, but the Minkowski norm of the system’s four-momentum is invariant. This makes center-of-mass energy a direct tool for comparing the energy a collision can make available.

M²c⁴=E_total²−p_total²c²

From assumptions to results

  1. Add the four-momenta of the particles, then take the invariant.
  2. In the center-of-mass frame p_total=0, so Mc²=E_cm.
  3. You must not simply add the particles’ rest masses together as a substitute for the system invariant.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Two photons, each with energy 1 GeV, meet head-on; the total momentum is zero and the system’s invariant mass corresponds to an energy of 2 GeV; the rest mass of each photon is still zero.

Range of validity

Four-momentum is required, and direction information must not be lost; the system invariant of photons moving in the same direction differs from the head-on case.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Total energy 6 GeV, total momentum zero, so Mc²=6 GeV.

Transfer and counterexample

If the two photons keep the same energies but move in exactly the same direction, what is the system’s invariant mass?

Check the transfer solution

E_total=p_total c, so M=0; this shows that the system mass is not determined by the single-particle energies and masses alone.

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; switch to this problem when reviewing on days 1, 3, and 7 to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

γ=E/mc²=3/0.94=3.19.
