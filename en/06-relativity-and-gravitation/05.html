Undergraduate core · about 30–60 min

Introduction to Differential Geometry: Metric and Geodesics

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: introductory relativity; multivariable calculus. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Gravity is written in the language of geometry: the metric gives the spacetime interval, geodesics are the worldlines of freely falling bodies, and curvature is built from derivatives of the metric. The Einstein equation links curvature to the distribution of matter.

ds²=g_μν dx^μ dx^ν；geodesic equation d²x^μ/dτ²+Γ^μ_αβ (dx^α/dτ)(dx^β/dτ)=0

From assumptions to results

  1. Define the metric from the invariance of the interval.
  2. Varying the action S=−m∫ds gives the geodesic equation; the Christoffel symbols Γ^μ_αβ = ½ g^μν (∂_α g_νβ + ∂_β g_να − ∂_ν g_αβ) are built from first derivatives of the metric (this lesson uses the (−,+,+,+) metric signature, so the Schwarzschild metric has g₀₀=−(1−r_s/r)).
  3. For a flat metric the Christoffel symbols vanish, and geodesics reduce to straight lines.
  4. Null geodesics of photons must instead be described with an affine parameter.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

The full line element of the exterior Schwarzschild solution is ds²=−(1−r_s/r)c²dt²+(1−r_s/r)⁻¹dr²+r²dΩ² (r_s=2GM/c²); the gravitational time dilation of a static clock can be read off directly from g₀₀=−(1−r_s/r): near the ground, for every 1 m of altitude gained, Δf/f=gh/c²≈1.1×10⁻¹⁶, an effect satellite navigation must account for.

Range of validity

Classical differential-geometry tools; singularities and the Planck scale require a theory of quantum gravity.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Δf/f=gh/c²=9.8/(3×10⁸)²≈1.1×10⁻¹⁶.

Transfer and counterexample

In what sense is a free-fall path a “straight line” (a geodesic)?

Check the transfer solution

Under the equivalence principle, free fall locally eliminates gravity; in the comoving inertial frame it is simply straight-line motion.
