Comparing Moving Clocks

Lesson goal: explain “Comparing Moving Clocks” in your own words and independently complete the calculations and validity-condition judgments below.

Prerequisites: classical mechanics; algebra; general relativity requires tensors and differential geometry. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about one question first

What is γ when v = 0? Write down your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Special relativity compares measurements of space and time in different inertial frames. Proper time is the time measured along the clock’s own worldline. To compare a moving clock with laboratory time, you must specify where the events occur and how the clocks are synchronized.

Δt = γΔτ；γ = 1/√(1−v²/c²)

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the examples below.

Worked examples with reasoning

If v = 0.6c, then γ = 1.25. The moving clock itself elapses 4 s, while laboratory coordinate time elapses 5 s. This is not a clock malfunction but the structure of the spacetime interval.

Check the boundary cases

Relativistic effects must not be explained as light-signal travel delay; the comparison already includes the corresponding measurement conventions.
