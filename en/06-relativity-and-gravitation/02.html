Mass, Energy, and Gravity

Lesson goal: explain “Mass, Energy, and Gravity” in your own words and independently complete the calculations and validity-condition judgments below.

Prerequisites: classical mechanics; algebra; general relativity requires tensors and differential geometry. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about one question first

If the rest mass doubles, by what factor does the rest energy change? Write down your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Rest energy is tied to mass, but a particle’s total energy also includes kinetic energy. General relativity goes further and understands gravity as the effect of spacetime geometry on motion; in the weak-gravity, low-speed limit it reduces to Newtonian gravity.

E₀ = mc²；E² = p²c² + m²c⁴

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the examples below.

Worked examples with reasoning

Taking c ≈ 3×10⁸ m/s, a mass of 1 g corresponds to a rest energy of about 9×10¹³ J. This number is an energy equivalent; it does not mean that any process can release the entire mass in a controlled way.

Check the boundary cases

A photon has zero rest mass yet carries energy and momentum; E₀ = mc² must not be treated as the total-energy formula for all particles.
