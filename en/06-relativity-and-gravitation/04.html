Lorentz Transformations and Spacetime Diagrams: Relativity of Simultaneity and Length Contraction

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: invariance of the speed of light; inertial frames. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

The Lorentz transformation relates the coordinates of two inertial frames, and spacetime diagrams make the operational definitions of “simultaneity” and “length” plain at a glance: quantities perpendicular to the direction of relative motion are unchanged, while time intervals and lengths along the parallel direction both change with γ.

x′=γ(x−vt)；t′=γ(t−vx/c²)；γ=1/√(1−β²)

From assumptions to results

  1. From the invariance of the speed of light (x−ct=0 and x′−ct′=0 are the same null lines) and linearity, set x′=γ(x−vt) and solve for γ using the symmetry of the inverse transformation.
  2. Events simultaneous on a surface of t′=constant are not simultaneous on a t=constant surface — the relativity of simultaneity.
  3. The endpoints of a moving rod must be measured at the same instant, so its length contracts to L=L₀/γ.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

v=0.6c (γ=1.25): a spaceship 100 m long at rest is 80 m long in the ground frame; two events at the same location in the ship 4 s apart are measured as 5 s apart in the ground frame; when the ground frame photographs both ends “simultaneously”, the ship frame judges the front end to have been photographed first.

Range of validity

Inertial frames and flat spacetime; accelerated observers and gravity require differential-geometry tools (see later lessons).

Independent calculation

Show solution

L=100/1.25=80 m.

Transfer and counterexample

Each of two inertial frames sees the other’s clocks run slow — is that a contradiction?

Check the transfer solution

No: an asymmetry in proper time appears only when the comparison involves a round trip along the same worldline (the twin case); the symmetric inertial judgments each hold in their own frame.

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; switch to this problem when reviewing on days 1, 3, and 7 to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

L=L₀/γ, γ=1.667, L=0.6L₀.

Before moving to the next stage

  1. Close the page and explain the assumptions, derivation, and conclusions of this lesson’s model to an imagined classmate.
  2. Find a situation outside the range of validity and decide which term of the formula fails first.
  3. Within two days, redo both self-tests without looking at the solutions; whenever a step goes wrong, return to the corresponding section.
