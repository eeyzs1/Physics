Equations need initial conditions

Lesson goal: be able to explain "equations need initial conditions" in your own words and independently complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: algebra; calculus; linear algebra. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about one question first

When k = 0 with x₀ = 5, what is x at any time? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Physical laws often give relations among rates of change rather than the trajectory directly. Solutions of differential equations usually contain constants, and the initial conditions select the one solution corresponding to a particular experiment. The mathematical solution must also satisfy the physical boundaries.

dx/dt = −kx；x(t) = x₀e^(−kt)

Order of use: identify the system and its conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Worked example with reasoning

Take k = 1 s⁻¹ with initial x₀ = 10; after one time constant, 1 s, x is about 3.68. Different initial values give different solutions of the same equation; for positive k the behavior is decay.

Check the boundary cases

Having an equation does not mean the problem is determined; initial values and boundary conditions are also indispensable.
