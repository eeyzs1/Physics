Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Functions of a complex variable and the residue theorem

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: complex numbers and phases; Fourier analysis. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, return first to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

An analytic function is completely determined by its boundary values. Many real integrals can be continued into the complex plane and computed in one step from the residues at poles; dispersion relations and propagators in physics both rely on this tool.

∮f(z)dz=2πi·ΣRes

From assumptions to results

  1. Cauchy's theorem gives zero circulation in a region of analyticity.
  2. When the contour shrinks onto an isolated singularity, only the 1/(z−z₀) term of the Laurent expansion contributes 2πi.
  3. A real-axis integral is closed with a large semicircular arc on which the integral tends to zero.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Stuck? Show the key intermediate steps
  1. To compute ∫e^(imx)/(x²+1)dx (m>0): on the large semicircular arc in the upper half-plane, |e^(imz)|=e^(−m·Im z) decays exponentially and the arc integral tends to zero (Jordan's lemma).
  2. The only singularity inside the closed contour is z=i (a simple pole).
  3. Res=e^(−m)/(2i).
  4. Real-axis integral=2πi×Res=πe^(−m).
  5. If m<0 you must switch to the lower half-plane; the result is symmetric.

If you are still stuck, write down the specific equation and then discuss it; that is more effective than rereading repeatedly.

Worked example and check

∫dx/(1+x²) (from −∞ to ∞) = π: the pole z=i has residue 1/(2i), and 2πi×1/(2i)=π.

Range of applicability

Requires the integrand to decay on the arc and the singularities to be isolated; branch-point cases require treatment with a branch cut.

Independent calculation

The pole z=2i has residue 1/(4i); the integral=2πi/(4i)=π/2≈1.571.
