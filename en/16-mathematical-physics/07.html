Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Introduction to group theory and representations: from symmetry to conservation

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Noether lesson; linear algebra. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, return first to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

A group is the algebra of symmetry: a system's symmetry group is realized through representations (matrices acting on the state space); irreducible representations classify states and spectral lines, and selection rules are a direct application of representation theory — the rotation group SU(2) unifies orbital angular momentum and spin.

[Jᵢ,Jⱼ]=iℏεᵢⱼₖJₖ；SU(2) representation dimension 2j+1

From assumptions to results

  1. The generators of rotations satisfy the angular-momentum commutation relations.
  2. Raising/lowering operators build the 2j+1-dimensional irreducible representation for each j (connecting to the CG coupling lesson).
  3. If a symmetry transformation commutes with the Hamiltonian, it block-diagonalizes the problem; transition matrix elements are nonzero only when the direct product contains the identity representation.
  4. SU(2) double-covering SO(3) explains the 4π periodicity of spin ½.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Atomic electric-dipole transitions Δℓ=±1: the coordinate vector belongs to the l=1 representation, and the transition is allowed only if the direct product contains the identity representation; the eightfold way in particle physics used the SU(3) decuplet to predict the Ω⁻ (discovered in 1964) — a triumph of representation theory.

Range of applicability

Continuous groups are handled with Lie algebras; quantum anomalies can break some classical symmetries (in the field-theory field).
