Breaking complex waveforms into simple waves

Lesson goal: be able to explain "breaking complex waveforms into simple waves" in your own words and independently complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: algebra; calculus; linear algebra. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about one question first

To sample a band-limited signal whose highest frequency is 10 Hz without aliasing, what must the sampling rate be strictly greater than, in Hz? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Linear systems allow superposition. Fourier analysis decomposes a function, under suitable conditions, into components of different frequencies; each component can be studied separately and then recombined into the total response. Spatial frequency and temporal frequency must be distinguished.

f(t) = Σ[aₙcos(nωt)+bₙsin(nωt)]

Order of use: identify the system and its conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Check the boundary cases

A Fourier expansion is not a claim that every system in nature is linear; nonlinear systems do not necessarily satisfy the superposition principle.
