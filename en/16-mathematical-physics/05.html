Tensors and covariance

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: linear algebra; index notation. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, return first to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

The coordinate independence of physical laws is expressed with tensors: a tensor is a multilinear object that transforms according to definite rules; when both sides of an equation are tensors, the form is the same in any coordinate system.

T′^μ_ν=(∂x′^μ/∂x^α)(∂x^β/∂x′_ν) T^α_β

From assumptions to results

  1. Scalars are invariant, and vectors transform by the Jacobian matrix.
  2. A multilinear object transforms once for each index.
  3. Contraction consumes one upper and one lower index, and the metric is used to raise and lower indices.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The Maxwell field strength F_μν is a second-rank antisymmetric tensor that combines the electric and magnetic fields into a single Lorentz-covariant object; the contraction T^μ_μ of the energy–momentum tensor T_μν gives the trace of the source.

Range of applicability

Index notation relies on smooth coordinate systems; objects such as spinors require extending the tensor framework.
