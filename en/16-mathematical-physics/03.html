Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Separation of variables and boundary eigenvalues

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: partial differential equations; trigonometric functions; derivatives. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, return first to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Boundary conditions of a partial differential equation select the allowed spatial patterns. The heat equation ties each spatial pattern to a different decay rate; high-frequency structures usually vanish faster.

∂T/∂t=α∂²T/∂x²；T_n∝sin(nπx/L)e^(−α(nπ/L)²t)

From assumptions to results

  1. Set T=X(x)G(t), substitute, and divide by XG.
  2. The two sides depend only on x and on t respectively, so each equals the same separation constant.
  3. Zero-temperature boundaries give sine eigenfunctions.

Worked example and check

With the length L fixed, the decay rate of mode 2 is 4 times that of mode 1, so finer temperature variations vanish faster.

Range of applicability

Constant coefficients, one dimension, fixed zero temperature at both ends; for non-zero boundaries you can first subtract the steady-state solution, and other boundaries change the modes.
