Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

A trio of special functions: Legendre, Bessel, and Hermite

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the separation-of-variables lesson; series solutions. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, return first to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

The three most frequent Sturm–Liouville problems in physics: spherical symmetry gives Legendre polynomials and spherical harmonics (hydrogen atom, electrostatic multipoles); cylindrical symmetry gives Bessel functions (drumheads, waveguides, circular apertures); the harmonic oscillator gives Hermite polynomials (molecular vibrations, field modes).

P_l(cosθ) and Y_lm；J_m(kr)；H_n(ξ)

From assumptions to results

  1. Separating the Laplace equation in spherical coordinates, the finite solutions of the angular equation truncate to Legendre polynomials, which combine into spherical harmonics Y_lm forming a complete angular basis.
  2. The radial equation in cylindrical coordinates gives Bessel functions J_m, with boundary conditions (e.g. J_m(ka)=0) discretizing the eigenvalues.
  3. After introducing a dimensionless variable in the harmonic-oscillator equation, the series truncates to Hermite polynomials, and energy quantization appears with it.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The first zero of J₀, 2.405, sets the fundamental frequency of a circular membrane and the cutoff of a circular-aperture waveguide; P₂(cosθ)=(3cos²θ−1)/2 is the angular shape of a quadrupole potential; the harmonic-oscillator ground state is Gaussian (the n=0 Hermite polynomial is a constant), matching the minimum wave packet of the uncertainty relation.

Range of applicability

Standard boundaries and orthogonal bases; complicated boundaries require numerical eigen-solutions (connecting to computational physics).

Transfer and counterexample

Why do these three families of functions always appear paired with a particular symmetry?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

Separation of variables relies on the symmetry of the coordinate system: spherical, cylindrical, and harmonic-oscillator potentials pair with Legendre, Bessel, and Hermite respectively — symmetry determines the eigenbasis.
