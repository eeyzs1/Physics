The Laplace transform and linear response

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the ODE lesson; the Fourier transform lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, return first to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Mapping a function of time to an analytic function of complex frequency turns a linear differential equation into an algebraic equation with the initial values built in — standard equipment for circuit transients and control theory; it complements the Fourier transform: one handles initial-value problems, the other steady-state spectra.

F(s)=∫₀^∞f(t)e^(−st)dt；d/dt→s

From assumptions to results

  1. Converges where Re s is large enough.
  2. The derivative rule L[f′]=sF−f(0⁺) brings in the initial values.
  3. After solving the algebraic equation, expand in partial fractions and invert term by term (poles correspond to exponential modes).
  4. Poles in the left half-plane are stable, on the imaginary axis marginal, and in the right half-plane divergent.
  5. s=iω links to the Fourier transform.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

RC step response: sC·V+V/R=I/s gives V=IR/(1+sRC), and inversion yields 1−e^(−t/RC); the transfer-function pole −1/RC is the reciprocal of the time constant.

Range of applicability

Causal signals (t≥0); the region of convergence must be consistent with the inversion path.
