Symmetry and Noether conserved quantities

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Lagrangian mechanics; partial derivatives. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, return first to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

A continuous symmetry connects seemingly different states of motion. If the Lagrangian does not explicitly contain some generalized coordinate, that coordinate is cyclic and its conjugate momentum is conserved; this is a simple entry point into Noether's idea.

∂L/∂q=0 ⇒ d(∂L/∂q̇)/dt=0

From assumptions to results

  1. From the Euler–Lagrange equation it follows directly that the time derivative of the conjugate momentum is zero.
  2. In planar motion in a central potential the angular coordinate does not appear explicitly, so angular momentum is conserved.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

L=m(ṙ²+r²φ̇²)/2−V(r) gives p_φ=mr²φ̇. If the radius becomes 2 times as large while angular momentum is conserved, the angular velocity drops to 1/4.

Range of applicability

The full Noether theorem also allows the Lagrangian to change by a total derivative; only the simplest case is discussed here.

Independent calculation

Show solution

mr²φ̇ is conserved, so φ̇ is proportional to r⁻²; divided by 9.

Transfer and counterexample

If the external potential depends explicitly on the angle, can you still directly assert conservation of angular momentum?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

No; now ∂L/∂φ may be nonzero, and the external torque changes the corresponding conjugate momentum.
