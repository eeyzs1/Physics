Momentum Conservation and Collisions

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Newton's second law; vectors. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Internal forces occur in pairs, so the total momentum of an isolated system does not change. Collisions are classified by whether kinetic energy is conserved; in a perfectly inelastic collision the final velocity is determined by momentum alone, and the lost energy becomes internal energy.

Σp conserved (ΣF_ext=0); elastic collisions also conserve kinetic energy

From assumptions to results

  1. Sum Newton's laws over the system of particles; the internal forces cancel in pairs.
  2. A one-dimensional elastic collision is solved by combining the two equations for momentum and kinetic energy.
  3. In the perfectly inelastic case, the final velocity equals the center-of-mass velocity.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

A 2 kg object moving at 3 m/s strikes a stationary 1 kg object and they stick together in a perfectly inelastic collision: the final speed is 6/3=2 m/s; the initial kinetic energy is 9 J, the final kinetic energy is 6 J, and 3 J becomes internal energy.

Range of applicability

During the brief collision the external forces must be negligible; for a general collision the coefficient of restitution lies between 0 and 1, and extra information is needed.

Independent calculation

Show solution

The one-dimensional elastic formula gives v2=2m₁u₁/(m₁+m₂)=2×2×3/3=4 m/s; the 2 kg object then ends at 1 m/s, and kinetic energy 1+8=9 J is conserved.

Transfer and counterexample

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

The two objects exchange velocities; the one initially at rest acquires all the speed.

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a change of units; when you review on days 1, 3, and 7, switch to this problem to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

v₁=(m₁−m₂)u/(m₁+m₂)=(2−1)×3/3=1 m/s.
