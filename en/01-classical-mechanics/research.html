Comparing a Real Pendulum with the Small-Angle Model

This is a reproduction exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare the model against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Entry conditions: be able to complete the written lessons of this field, and make up whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. A real experiment may be replaced by public or synthetic data, whose source must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Derive the full-angle equation and the small-angle equation; integrate each numerically from the same initial conditions; compare the periods at 5°, 30°, and 60°.

  1. On one page, state the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and a falsifiable prediction.
  2. First work a minimal example that can be solved analytically or by hand, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions, and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

The small-angle limit approaches T=2π√(ℓ/g); in the undamped case the total-energy error converges with the step size; the period at large amplitude is longer than at small amplitude.

If the results disagree, first troubleshoot the implementation, the units, and the assumptions before discussing new physical interpretations. Agreement is no substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

A curve of period versus initial angle, a table of integration step sizes, an energy-residual plot, and an explanation of the errors.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, and references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Substituting degree values directly into sin; comparing without a consistent definition of amplitude; mistaking numerical drift for real dissipation.

Extension questions

Add linear damping and distinguish the change in period from the decay of amplitude.

Paper reading and review

  1. Use the paper taxonomy by field to find a relevant topic, and select a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or review articles.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures and tables.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send me the report and calculations for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review it; do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability before it has been reviewed.

This page does not fabricate specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched and verified anew when the project is carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

Testable question; complete theoretical derivation; reproducible results; credible error analysis; alternative explanations checked; clear argumentation and citations. For each item 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full score of 12 only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
