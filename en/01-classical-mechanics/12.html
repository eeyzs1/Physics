Elastic Continuum: Stress, Strain, and Elastic Waves

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the lesson on rigid bodies; the lesson on the wave equation. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

For a continuum it is not enough to speak only of the net force: stress (force per unit area, a tensor) and strain (relative deformation, a tensor) are connected through Hooke's law, and longitudinal and transverse waves propagate through a solid at different speeds — the common foundation of seismology and mechanics of materials.

σ=Eε (one-dimensional); c_L=√((K+4G/3)/ρ), c_T=√(G/ρ)

From assumptions to results

  1. Force balance on an infinitesimal element gives the Cauchy stress concept (a symmetric tensor with three principal directions).
  2. The geometric equations decompose the displacement gradient into strain (symmetric) and rotation (antisymmetric).
  3. The linear-elastic constitutive relation σ_ij=C_ijkl·ε_kl; for isotropy only Young's modulus E and Poisson's ratio ν remain (with Lamé parameter conversions).
  4. Momentum balance gives the Navier equation, which splits into the two kinds of body waves, longitudinal and transverse.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

For steel, E≈200 GPa and ρ≈7850: c_T≈3.2 km/s and c_L≈5.9 km/s; the seismological P/S velocity ratio √((K+4G/3)/G) gives the elastic profile of Earth's interior (the link to the Geophysics and Space Physics field); beam bending and resonance frequencies are determined by E and the moment of inertia of the cross section.

Range of applicability

Linear elasticity at small strain; large deformation, plasticity, and fracture require nonlinear constitutive laws.

The longitudinal wave involves bulk + shear restoring stiffness (K+4G/3) and is always faster than the transverse wave, which is pure shear.
