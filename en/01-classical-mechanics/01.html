How Forces Change Motion

Lesson goal: be able to explain in your own words how forces change motion, and complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below on your own.

Prerequisites: Learning Preparation; the advanced parts require calculus. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Start with a question

A 3 kg mass with a 12 N net force: what is the acceleration in m/s²? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understand the model

Choose one object as the system under study, draw the external forces acting on it, and define the positive direction first. The net force determines the acceleration; the velocity itself is not directly determined by the current net force. Use Newton's second law under constant mass, an inertial reference frame, and low speeds.

ΣF = ma

Order of use: identify the system and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

A 2 kg cart is pulled to the right with an 8 N force and acted on by a 2 N resistance to the left. Take rightward as positive: the net force is 6 N, so the acceleration is 3 m/s². Vertically, gravity and the normal force balance.

Check the boundary cases

Motion does not mean there must be a net force in the same direction; with zero net force, uniform motion is also possible.

Look back at the example: name at least one factor that was neglected, and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?
