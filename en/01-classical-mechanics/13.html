Hamilton's Equations and Phase Space

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: partial derivatives; Lagrangian mechanics. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Phase space represents the state by coordinates and conjugate momenta. The momentum is defined as the partial derivative of the Lagrangian with respect to the velocity and is not always equal to mass times velocity. In the canonical formulation, the Hamiltonian is generated through a Legendre transform.

p=∂L/∂q̇; H=pq̇−L; q̇=∂H/∂p, ṗ=−∂H/∂q

From assumptions to results

  1. For a one-dimensional spring, L=mq̇²/2−kq²/2.
  2. p=mq̇.
  3. Eliminate q̇ to obtain H=p²/(2m)+kq²/2.
  4. Take partial derivatives again to recover the equations of motion.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

For m=2 kg and k=8 N/m, at q=0.5 m and p=2 kg·m/s: q̇=1 m/s, ṗ=−4 N, and the total energy is 2 J.

Range of applicability

Requires the relevant Legendre transform to be nonsingular; systems with constraints and gauge degrees of freedom need more careful treatment.
