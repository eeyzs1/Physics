Non-Inertial Frames and Inertial Forces

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Newton's laws; rotational kinematics. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Newton's laws hold only in inertial frames. In an accelerating or rotating reference frame, introducing inertial forces (centrifugal, Coriolis) keeps the equations in the same form; they must be used in meteorology, ocean currents, and rotating machinery.

Rotating frame: F_inertial=−m[2Ω×v′+Ω×(Ω×r′)]

From assumptions to results

  1. Take two time derivatives in the rotating coordinate system, deriving that the inertial acceleration differs from the rotating-frame acceleration by 2Ω×v′ and Ω×(Ω×r).
  2. Substitute back into Newton's laws and move the reference-frame terms to the force side; these are the inertial forces.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

A freely falling body at 45° N deflects eastward: x≈Ωg t³cos45°/3, and with t=3 s this gives x≈4.5 mm; the Foucault pendulum is the long-time demonstration of the same effect.

Range of applicability

Earth's angular velocity of rotation is small, so inertial forces are negligible at everyday scales; relativistic non-inertial frames must be treated with the geometry of spacetime.

Transfer and counterexample

Why do cyclones in the Northern Hemisphere rotate counterclockwise?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

Air flowing toward the low-pressure center is continuously deflected to the right by the Coriolis force, producing a counterclockwise circulation.
