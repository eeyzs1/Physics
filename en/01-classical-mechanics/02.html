Energy and Fluid Continuity

Lesson goal: be able to explain energy and fluid continuity in your own words, and complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below on your own.

Prerequisites: Learning Preparation; the advanced parts require calculus. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Start with a question

The inlet cross-sectional area is 3 times the outlet's, and the inlet flow speed is 2 m/s. What is the outlet speed in m/s? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understand the model

Mechanical energy links motion and position. When only conservative forces do work, the sum of kinetic and potential energy stays constant. Fluids obey a conservation law as well: when an incompressible fluid flows steadily through a pipe, the volume entering equals the volume leaving in the same time interval.

K = mv²/2; A₁v₁ = A₂v₂

Order of use: identify the system and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

Water flows at 1 m/s in a pipe with a cross-sectional area of 4 cm²; when the pipe narrows to 2 cm², the speed is 2 m/s. Leakage is neglected here and the density is assumed constant; you cannot use this to infer the pressure in an arbitrary real pipe.

Check the boundary cases

Mechanical energy can be converted into internal energy by friction; the total energy is still conserved.

Look back at the example: name at least one factor that was neglected, and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?
