Two-Body Problem and Reduced Mass

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: central forces; conservation of momentum. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

The problem of two interacting point particles can be reduced to the motion of the center of mass plus the relative motion of an equivalent single particle, with the reduced mass appearing in the relative equation. The hydrogen atom, diatomic molecules, and two-body scattering all use it first for simplification.

μ=m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂); μ r̈=F(r)

From assumptions to results

  1. Separate the center-of-mass coordinates from the relative coordinates.
  2. Internal forces cancel in pairs, so the center of mass moves at constant velocity.
  3. The equation for the relative coordinates carries the mass μ.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

For the electron–proton system, μ≈(1836/1837)m_e, only 0.05% smaller than the electron mass; when a hydrogen molecule is replaced by HD, μ changes from m/2 to 2m/3, and the vibrational frequency, which scales as √(1/μ), drops to √(3/4)≈0.866 of its value — the origin of the isotope shift.

Range of applicability

Classical two-body problem with no external field; three bodies and beyond cannot be reduced exactly and require perturbation or numerical methods.
