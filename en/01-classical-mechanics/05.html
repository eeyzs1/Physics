Rigid-Body Rotation and Angular Momentum

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Newton's laws; circular motion. If the prerequisites feel shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Rigid-body rotation is described with torque, moment of inertia, and angular momentum, in one-to-one parallel with force, mass, and momentum. The moment of inertia depends not only on the mass, but also on how the mass is distributed with respect to the axis.

τ=Iα; L=Iω; I=Σmr²

From assumptions to results

  1. For each particle write r×F=dp/dt and sum; the internal torques cancel, giving τ_ext=dL/dt.
  2. For a rigid body about a fixed axis L=Iω, hence τ=Iα.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Uniform thin rod with m=3 kg and L=2 m about one end: I=mL²/3=4 kg·m²; apply a constant torque of 8 N·m, so α=2 rad/s², and after 2 s, ω=4 rad/s.

Range of applicability

Assumes a rigid body (no deformation) and a fixed axis; rotation in three dimensions and precession require the inertia tensor and Euler's equations.

Independent calculation

Show solution

About the center I=mL²/12=3×4/12=1 kg·m²; check with the parallel-axis theorem: 1+m(L/2)²=1+3=4 ✓.

Transfer and counterexample

Concentrate the mass at the two ends of the rod, 1.5 kg each (total mass and length unchanged); what is I about the center?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

2×1.5×1²=3 kg·m².

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a change of units; when you review on days 1, 3, and 7, switch to this problem to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

I=mL²/3=6×4/3=8.

  3. Redo both problems on days 1, 3, and 7, and send me the steps you do not understand.
