Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

The variational method: from the helium ground state to the chemical bond

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the perturbation theory lesson; the hydrogen atom lesson. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

No exact solution is needed: guess a parameterized trial wave function, and the energy expectation gives an upper bound on the true ground-state energy — the variational principle. It turns multi-electron atoms, which cannot be solved exactly, into controlled approximations, and it is the root of quantum chemistry.

E[ψ_trial]≥E₀; minimizing approaches the ground state

From postulates to results

  1. Expand the trial state in the exact eigenstates; the energy expectation is a weighted average of the eigenvalues and can never fall below E₀.
  2. Minimize with respect to the parameters.
  3. A variational treatment of the helium ground state with an effective charge gives E≈−77.5 eV (experiment: −79.0 eV); systematic variational methods (including correlation) converge rapidly.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

For helium (Z=2) the optimal Z_eff=27/16≈1.69 gives E≈−77.5 eV, an error of about 2%, better than first-order perturbation theory (−74.8 eV); the same kind of method gave the first quantitative explanation of bonding in H₂⁺.

Range of applicability

Upper-bound character — the quality of the trial function determines the accuracy; the systematic extensions are HF/DFT (the link to the chemical physics field).

Check your solution to the transfer problem

The expansion mixes in ground-state components, pulling the average below the target excited energy; the trial state must first be made orthogonal to the ground state.
