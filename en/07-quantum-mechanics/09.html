Scattering and cross sections

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: wave functions; probability current. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Scattering experiments are the primary tool for probing microscopic structure. The cross section is the effective area that converts incident flux into outgoing count rate; the distribution over angles reflects the spatial structure of the interaction.

dσ/dΩ=|f(θ)|²; σ=∫(dσ/dΩ)dΩ

From postulates to results

  1. The far-field wave function decomposes into an incident plane wave plus an outgoing spherical wave f(θ)e^(ikr)/r.
  2. The ratio of outgoing to incident probability current gives the differential cross section.
  3. Integrating over solid angle gives the total cross section.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

A hard sphere of radius R=1 fm has geometric cross section πR²=3.1×10⁻³⁰ m²≈31 mb, a typical scale for nuclear scattering.

Range of applicability

The geometric cross section holds only in the short-wavelength limit; in low-energy s-wave scattering the total cross section can reach 4πR²; near a resonance the cross section varies violently.

Independent calculation

Show solution

σ∝R², 2²=4.

Transfer and counterexample

What is the event rate, in s⁻¹, for a process with luminosity 10²⁸ cm⁻²s⁻¹ and cross section 100 mb?

Check your solution to the transfer problem

100 mb=10⁻²⁵ cm², Lσ=10²⁸×10⁻²⁵=1000 s⁻¹.

Advanced self-test

This one takes an extra step or requires a unit conversion; on the days 1, 3, and 7 reviews, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

σ=πR²=3.14×4 fm²=12.6 fm²; 1 fm²=10 mb, hence 126 mb.
