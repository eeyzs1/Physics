Many-body identical particles: Slater determinants and exchange energy

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the identical particles lesson; the variational method lesson. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The wave function of N fermions must be antisymmetric: building it from single-particle orbitals as a Slater determinant achieves this automatically; the direct consequences of the Pauli principle are the exchange energy and the Fermi-sea structure — the common root of atomic shells, metallic degeneracy pressure, and the support of white dwarfs.

Ψ=(N!)^(−1/2)·det[φᵢ(xⱼ)]

From postulates to results

  1. Antisymmetrizing a product state gives a determinant; swapping two columns flips the sign automatically.
  2. If two electrons share an orbital, the determinant vanishes — this is the Pauli principle.
  3. The energy from varying the determinant (HF) contains Coulomb integrals and exchange integrals; the latter arise purely from antisymmetry and have no classical counterpart.
  4. At T=0, filling the Fermi sphere builds the Fermi sea.

Worked example and check

The helium 1s2s configuration: in the triplet (antisymmetric in space) the electrons avoid each other and the Coulomb energy is lower — exchange energy turns spin statistics into an energy difference; the white-dwarf degeneracy pressure is precisely Fermi-sea pressure (the link to the stellar remnants lesson).

Range of applicability

Single-determinant (mean-field) approximation; strong correlations require multi-determinant expansions (the link to the chemical physics field).

Independent calculation

Show solution

A determinant with two identical columns is zero — the determinant statement of the Pauli principle.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the exchange energy said to have "no classical origin"?

Check your solution to the transfer problem

It arises from the antisymmetry of the wave function (quantum statistics); it is not a new interaction, and classical probability has no corresponding term.
