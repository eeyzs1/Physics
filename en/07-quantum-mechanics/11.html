The WKB semiclassical approximation

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the tunneling lesson; the concept of action. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

For large quantum numbers or slowly varying potentials, write the wave function as amplitude × phase, with the phase accumulating along the classical action: oscillation in the allowed region, exponential decay in the forbidden region, and matching at the turning points gives the quantization condition and the tunneling rate — the bridge joining the classical and quantum pictures.

ψ∝exp(±i∫p dx/ℏ)/√p; T≈e^(−2∫|p|dx/ℏ)

From postulates to results

  1. Substitute and expand ψ=A·e^(iS/ℏ): the leading term gives (S′)²=2m(E−V), the next term gives the amplitude ∝p^(−1/2).
  2. At the turning points p=0 the approximation fails; match the two sides with Airy functions.
  3. Single-valuedness of bound states requires ∮p dx=(n+½)h (semiclassical quantization).

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

For the harmonic oscillator, quantizing ∮p dx gives exactly E_n=(n+½)ℏω, the ½ coming from the Maslov phase of the two turning points; the Gamow factor of α decay is precisely the same tunneling integral (the link to the nuclear field).

Range of applicability

The potential must vary slowly on the scale of the de Broglie wavelength; near the turning points dedicated connection formulas are needed.
