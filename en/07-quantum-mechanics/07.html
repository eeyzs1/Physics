Angular momentum and spin

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: operators and commutation relations. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The components of angular momentum do not commute with one another; only L² and one component can be determined simultaneously. Spin is an intrinsic degree of freedom obeying the same algebra as orbital angular momentum, but it can take half-integer values.

L²→ℏ²ℓ(ℓ+1); Lz→mℏ; electron S=½

From postulates to results

  1. From the commutation relation [Lx,Ly]=iℏLz, construct ladder operators and obtain the allowed range of m together with ℓ(ℓ+1).
  2. Spin ½ is represented by the Pauli matrices, with eigenvalues ±½ℏ.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

For ℓ=1, |L|=√2 ℏ and the component takes the values −ℏ, 0, +ℏ; the squares of the Pauli matrices are the identity, giving S_z=±½ℏ.

Range of applicability

For orbital angular momentum ℓ takes only integer values; half-integers belong only to spin; coherent superposition states retain phase freedom.
