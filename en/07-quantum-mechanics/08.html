Identical particles and the Pauli principle

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: spin; symmetrization of wave functions. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Identical particles are indistinguishable, and the many-body wave function must be symmetric (bosons) or antisymmetric (fermions). The Pauli principle is a direct consequence of antisymmetry and determines shell structure and the chemistry of the elements.

Fermions: swapping two particles → wave function multiplied by −1

From postulates to results

  1. Exchanging two particles changes no observable, so the wave function can differ by at most a phase.
  2. Exchanging twice returns the original state, so the phase can only be ±1.
  3. Antisymmetry requires the wave function to vanish when two fermions are in exactly the same state.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

If the two electrons of a helium atom occupy the same spatial state, their spins must pair into a singlet (S=0); a spin triplet instead requires an antisymmetric spatial part, with a different energy — this is precisely the difference between parahelium and orthohelium.

Range of applicability

Symmetrization applies only to genuinely identical particles; particles of different masses or species simply take product states.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Two electrons in the same orbital must have one spin ↑ and one ↓; the spin state is the antisymmetric superposition, the singlet (↑↓−↓↑)/√2, whose m=0 component gives S_z=+½ℏ−½ℏ=0.

Transfer and counterexample

How many components does the triplet state of two spin-½ particles have?

Check your solution to the transfer problem

Three, corresponding to m=1, 0, −1.

Advanced self-test

One with spin up and one with spin down; the Pauli principle forbids any more.
