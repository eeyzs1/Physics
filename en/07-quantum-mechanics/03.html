Overview / Quantum Mechanics / Lesson 3

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Operators, commutation relations, and uncertainty

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: linear algebra; probability variances; complex numbers. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The expectation value is a statistical prediction of the outcomes of experiments repeated on the same state. When two operators do not commute, in general you cannot prepare a state that is arbitrarily sharp in both; the uncertainty describes the spread of the state, not merely instrument error.

ΔAΔB≥|〈[A,B]〉|/2; [x,p]=iℏ

From postulates to results

  1. Apply the Cauchy inequality to (A−〈A〉)|ψ〉 and (B−〈B〉)|ψ〉.
  2. Split the inner product into symmetric and antisymmetric parts and keep the commutator term to obtain the Robertson inequality.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

In units where ℏ=1, if Δx=0.5 then Δp≥1; this gives only a lower bound, and an actual state need not saturate the equality.

Range of applicability

The state must lie in the domain of the relevant operators and the variances must exist; saturating the equality usually requires special states.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Δp≥1/(2Δx)=1/4=0.25.
