The one-dimensional harmonic oscillator

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Schrödinger equation; the idea of series solutions. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The quantum solution of a parabolic potential gives equally spaced energy levels; it is the prototype for molecular vibrations, phonons in solids, and modes of the light field. The zero-point energy is a direct corollary of the uncertainty relation.

Eₙ=(n+½)ℏω

From postulates to results

  1. After introducing a dimensionless variable, solve the Schrödinger equation; requiring the wave function to be finite everywhere forces the series to terminate into Hermite polynomials, and the energy is quantized as (n+½)ℏω.
  2. The ground state is exactly the minimum-uncertainty wave packet.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Stuck? Show the key intermediate steps
  1. Introduce the dimensionless variables ξ=√(mω/ℏ)x and ε=E/ℏω; the equation becomes ψ″+(2ε−ξ²)ψ=0.
  2. For large |ξ| the equation is approximately ψ″≈ξ²ψ, whose solutions behave like e^(±ξ²/2); the physical solution may keep only the e^(−ξ²/2) envelope.
  3. Substituting the remaining h(ξ) series gives the recursion a_(j+2)=(2j+1−2ε)/[(j+1)(j+2)]·a_j.
  4. The series must terminate into a polynomial; the condition is 2ε=2n+1, and the truncated polynomials are precisely the Hermite polynomials H_n.
  5. Normalization gives ψ_n and E_n=(n+½)ℏω — the zero-point energy arises because the uncertainty relation forbids a wave packet from being simultaneously at rest and of finite width.

If you are still stuck, write down the specific equation and then discuss it; that works better than rereading over and over.

Worked example and check

Let ℏω=0.10 eV (a typical scale for molecular vibrations): E₀=0.05 eV, the n=0→1 transition absorbs 0.10 eV, and the equal spacing of the levels is precisely the signature of the harmonic regime.

Range of applicability

Real molecular potentials deviate from a parabola at large amplitudes (anharmonicity); for coupled systems you must first find the normal modes.
