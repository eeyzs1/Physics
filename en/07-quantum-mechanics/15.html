Overview / Quantum Mechanics / Lesson 15

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Time-dependent perturbation theory and Fermi's golden rule: how atoms emit and absorb light

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the perturbation theory lesson; quantum superposition. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Stationary states do not transition spontaneously; transitions are driven by a time-dependent perturbation. In weak fields the transition rate is proportional to the spectral intensity of the perturbation at the transition frequency — Fermi's golden rule; spontaneous emission corresponds to treating the vacuum electromagnetic modes as the perturbation (the qualitative picture from QED).

Γ_(i→f)=(2π/ℏ)|〈f|V|i〉|²·ρ(E_f)

From postulates to results

  1. Insert the time-dependent perturbation into the first-order time evolution; the final-state amplitude is an integral over time.
  2. The energy window of the finite-time integral narrows with time while its peak grows; in the long-time limit the sinc² converges to a δ function, giving the golden rule.
  3. For a thermal light field ρ is given by the photon density of states, and the Einstein coefficients connect absorption, stimulated emission, and spontaneous emission.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

For a dipole perturbation, the angular integrals give the selection rules Δℓ=±1, Δm=0,±1; the spontaneous lifetime of hydrogen 2p→1s is about 1.6 ns (A≈6.25×10⁸ s⁻¹), corresponding to a natural linewidth of about 4×10⁻⁷ eV.

Range of applicability

First-order perturbation theory and weak fields; strong fields enter the Rabi-oscillation regime, and multiphoton processes and dressed states require nonperturbative treatment.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Γ∝|V_fi|²ρ; with the matrix element fixed it grows linearly with ρ.

Transfer and counterexample

With continuum final states, why do you get a constant rate instead of an ever-growing probability?

Check your solution to the transfer problem

Final states with mismatched energy oscillate and cancel; only the matched window of width ∝1/t keeps growing, and height × width gives exactly a linearly growing probability and a constant rate.
