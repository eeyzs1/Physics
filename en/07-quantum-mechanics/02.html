Why binding produces energy levels

Lesson goal: explain "why binding produces energy levels" in your own words and independently complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: waves; probability; the advanced lessons require complex numbers, linear algebra, and calculus. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about a question first

What is the ratio of the energy of the third level to the first? Write down your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understand the model

In a one-dimensional infinite potential well, the wave function must vanish at the walls; only standing waves with specific wavelengths can satisfy the boundary conditions. The momentum scale and the energy are therefore restricted to discrete values. This is the conclusion of a specific model.

Eₙ = n²h²/(8mL²), n = 1,2,…

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write down the equations → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The precise meaning and conventions of the variables are given in the worked examples below.

Work through the examples with the reasoning

In the same potential well, the second level is 4 times the first. When the well width doubles, all levels drop to 1/4 of their original values; the ground state is still nonzero.

Check the boundary cases

Quantization comes from the states and boundary conditions of a specific system; the energy of a free particle can be continuous.

Look back at the example: name at least one factor that was neglected, and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?
