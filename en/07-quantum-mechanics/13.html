Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

The density matrix and composite systems

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the framework of ensembles; the qubit lesson. If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Mixed states cannot be described by a wave function; you need the density matrix. The purity Tr ρ² distinguishes pure from mixed; tracing out part of a composite system gives the reduced state, and entanglement shows up as a mixed reduced state — the standard language of decoherence and open systems.

ρ=Σpᵢ|ψᵢ⟩⟨ψᵢ|; ⟨A⟩=Tr(ρA); Tr ρ²≤1

From postulates to results

  1. A probabilistic mixture gives a linear representation of expectations.
  2. Evolution is ρ→UρU†, projective measurement is ρ→ΣP_mρP_m.
  3. For a composite system ρ_A=Tr_Bρ_AB; for a Bell state ρ_A=I/2, maximally mixed — entanglement makes the local state lose purity.
  4. The von Neumann entropy S=−Tr ρ ln ρ measures the degree of mixing.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

A 50/50 mixture of |+x⟩ and |−x⟩ has ρ=½I: measurements along any direction give 50/50, distinguishable from any pure state; decoherence suppresses the off-diagonal elements exponentially to zero and ρ tends to ½I — the noise channels of the quantum information field are modeled in exactly this way.

Range of applicability

The ensemble interpretation depends on the preparation history; different mixtures with the same ρ cannot be distinguished operationally.

ρ=I/2 gives Tr ρ²=1/2; for a pure state it is 1.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can entanglement not be confirmed by local measurements alone?

Check your solution to the transfer problem

A local reduced state can also arise from classical correlations; you must perform coincidence (Bell-type) measurements to verify the non-classical correlations.
