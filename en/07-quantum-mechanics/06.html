Overview / Quantum Mechanics / Lesson 6

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

The hydrogen atom and the energy-level formula

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Schrödinger equation; separation of variables and central potentials (angular momentum is developed systematically in the next lesson). If the prerequisites are shaky, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Coulomb attraction plus quantization of angular momentum gives discrete energy levels and degeneracy: the energy depends only on the principal quantum number n, and the wave functions are labeled by n, ℓ, m. This is the starting point for the structure of the periodic table.

Eₙ=−13.6 eV/n²

From postulates to results

  1. After separating variables, the angular part yields spherical harmonics.
  2. Convergence of the radial series at infinity forces the energy to take the Bohr-formula values.
  3. The degeneracy is n² (excluding spin).

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Stuck? Show the key intermediate steps
  1. Separate variables in spherical coordinates: the angular equations give the spherical harmonics Y_lm with eigenvalues ℓ(ℓ+1).
  2. In the radial part, set u=rR to reduce it to one-dimensional form.
  3. Under Coulomb attraction, u must behave as ~r^(ℓ+1) at small r (excluding the collapsing solutions) and decay exponentially at large r.
  4. Substitute the series solution u=e^(−ρ/2)ρ^(ℓ+1)v(ρ); the recursion for v must terminate into a polynomial, and the condition gives exactly Eₙ=−me⁴/[2(4πε₀)²ℏ²n²] with principal quantum number n=n_r+ℓ+1 — the degeneracy in energy and ℓ is a special property of the Coulomb 1/r potential.

If you are still stuck, write down the specific equation and then discuss it; that works better than rereading over and over.

Worked example and check

The Lyman-α line (n=2→1): ΔE=13.6×(1−1/4)=10.2 eV, wavelength 1240/10.2≈121.6 nm, in the ultraviolet.

Range of applicability

Spin–orbit coupling, the Lamb shift, and relativistic corrections are not included (fine structure is of order α²).

Check your solution to the transfer problem
