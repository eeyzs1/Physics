Finite-difference computation of quantum well spectra

This is a reproduction exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare model against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Starting conditions: you can complete the written lessons of this field and fill any gaps in the mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced with public or synthetic data, but the source must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Discretize the second derivative to build the Hamiltonian matrix; diagonalize it to find the first five eigenstates; move from the infinite square well to a finite well.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, variables, assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First do a minimal example that is analytically or hand solvable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: reproduce these first

The infinite-well energy ratios are close to 1:4:9; grid refinement converges; the states are normalized and mutually orthogonal.

If the results disagree, first check the implementation, the units, and the assumptions, and only then discuss new physical interpretations. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

An energy-spectrum error table, probability densities, and a statement of the boundary conditions.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Omitting the square of the grid spacing; normalization without the integration weights; mistaking finite-box pseudo-continuum states for true bound states.

Extension questions

Add a small perturbation and test the range of validity of first-order perturbation theory.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field paper taxonomy portal, find a relevant topic and choose a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures or tables.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send me the report and calculations for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review it; do not treat your self-assessment as certification of research ability before a review.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched out and verified afresh when the project is carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. For each item 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full 12 points means only that this project's report meets the standard, not that you have mastered the field.
