Synchrotron radiation and bending energy loss

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: magnetic rigidity; relativistic energy. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

A charged particle on a curved orbit emits synchrotron radiation. For electrons the energy loss per turn grows as the fourth power of the energy; it is both the main burden of circular colliders and the output of high-intensity light sources.

U₀[eV]≈88.5·E[GeV]⁴/ρ[m] (electrons)

From assumptions to results

  1. The radiation of a relativistic particle is beamed into a cone of opening angle on the order of 1/γ.
  2. Integrating the radiation spectrum over one turn of the orbit gives a loss per turn ∝E⁴/ρ.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

E=3 GeV, ρ=10 m: U₀=88.5×81/10≈717 eV per turn; a storage ring with 6 GeV and ρ=23 m loses about 5 keV per turn and needs the RF system to replenish that energy.

Limits of validity

An electron formula; for protons the loss is suppressed by m⁻⁴ and becomes significant only at extremely high energies.

Independent calculation

Show solution

U₀∝E⁴, so 2⁴=16.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can proton rings reach far higher energies than electron rings?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

Synchrotron radiation losses decrease sharply as 1/m⁴; protons hardly radiate at all.

Advanced self-test

One step beyond the previous problem, or a change of units; when you review on days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

2⁴=16.
