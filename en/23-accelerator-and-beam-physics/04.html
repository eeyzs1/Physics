Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Radio-frequency acceleration and the synchronism condition

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: magnetic rigidity; resonance. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

A traveling-wave or standing-wave cavity gives the particle energy at a precise phase; the particle must stay in phase with the RF — the synchronism condition ties the cavity frequency, the bending field, and the orbit period together, and the principle of phase stability maintains synchronism automatically.

f_rf=h·f_rev; energy gain qV cosφ_s

From assumptions to results

  1. The period per turn is set by the magnetic field and the momentum.
  2. The RF is taken as an integer multiple of the revolution frequency.
  3. Particles slightly off the synchronous phase receive automatic corrections in energy gain or loss, forming longitudinal bunching.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The LHC revolution frequency is about 11 kHz with harmonic number 35640: f_rf≈392 MHz (400 MHz in actual operation); protons gain energy cavity by cavity and bunch together.

Limits of validity

Single-particle picture; space charge and collective instabilities require collective treatment.

Independent calculation

Show solution

35640×11×10³=3.92×10⁸ Hz≈392 MHz.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the principle of phase stability important?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

Particles deviating from the synchronous phase feel a restoring force, so the bunch automatically stays together longitudinally.
