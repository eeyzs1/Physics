Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Collider luminosity and event rates

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: cross section; particle collisions. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

A collider's performance is measured by luminosity: event rate = luminosity × cross section. Luminosity is set by the beam intensity, the beam-spot size, and the collision frequency; it is the lifeline of small-cross-section processes.

L=N₁N₂ f/(4πσ_x σ_y); R=Lσ

From assumptions to results

  1. The overlap integral of two colliding Gaussian beams gives the luminosity.
  2. Luminosity is raised by increasing the beam current and shrinking the beam spot, the product of emittance and the β function.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

At an LHC peak luminosity of 2×10³⁴ cm⁻²s⁻¹ and a Higgs cross section of about 55 pb, the production rate is about 1.1 Hz; over a day this accumulates candidate events at the hundred-thousand level.

Limits of validity

Gaussian beams and constant luminosity assumed; backgrounds and pile-up rise with luminosity.

Independent calculation

Show solution

50 pb=5×10⁻³⁵ cm², R=2×10³⁴×5×10⁻³⁵=1 s⁻¹.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is raising the luminosity as important as raising the energy?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

At a fixed energy scale, accumulating statistics is the only viable path for studying small-cross-section processes.

Advanced self-test

One step beyond the previous problem, or a change of units; when you review on days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

10 pb=10⁻³⁵ cm², R=2×10³⁴×10⁻³⁵=0.2 Hz.
