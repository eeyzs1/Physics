Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Transverse dynamics and the β function: Courant–Snyder theory

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: the beam-matrices lesson; 2×2 matrices. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

In a circular accelerator the transverse oscillation of a particle is described by Hill's equation; Courant and Snyder introduced the β(s) function and the Twiss parameters, casting beam size, phase, and stability into the design language of the closed ring.

σ(s)=√(β(s)·ε); stability condition |Tr(M)|≤2

From assumptions to results

  1. The transverse motion is stable when the trace of the one-turn transport matrix M of the periodic focusing lattice satisfies |Tr M|≤2.
  2. β(s) satisfies a Riccati-type equation and is uniquely determined by the magnet layout.
  3. The beam size σ=√(βε) varies around the ring following β, while the emittance ε is the beam's invariant quality.
  4. The phase advance μ′=1/β connects to the resonance-line condition nQ=integer.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Light-source storage rings squeeze β to the centimeter scale at the interaction point/insertion devices to shrink the beam spot and raise the brightness; the Σ propagation of the beam-matrices lesson and the β evolution here are two ways of writing the same theory; an emittance of 1 nm·rad with β=10 m gives σ=√(10⁻⁸)=0.1 mm.

Limits of validity

A linear, ideal lattice; nonlinear field errors, beam–beam interactions, and collective effects limit the real brightness.

Independent calculation

Show solution

σ=√(10×10⁻⁸)=√(10⁻⁷)≈0.316 mm.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the β function squeezed small at the interaction point?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

Luminosity ∝1/(σxσy)∝1/β*; squeezing β at the interaction point directly shrinks the beam spot and raises the event rate.
