Overview / Accelerator and Beam Physics / Lesson 6

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Free-electron laser

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: synchrotron radiation; electromagnetic waves. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

A relativistic electron beam radiates in undulator magnets; when the electrons bunch longitudinally to the scale of the radiation wavelength, coherent superposition makes the power grow as N² — the brightness of an X-ray FEL exceeds storage-ring light sources by many orders of magnitude.

λ_r=λ_u(1+K²/2)/(2γ²)

From assumptions to results

  1. In the undulator the electron follows a zigzag path, and the Doppler effect compresses the radiation wavelength by a factor ∝1/γ².
  2. Energy exchange between the electrons and their own radiation drives density bunching and then coherent amplification (SASE).

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

With λ_u=3 cm, 10 GeV electrons (γ≈19570), and K=1: λ_r≈5.9×10⁻¹¹ m≈0.06 nm, hard X-rays; an XFEL can probe processes in femtochemistry.

Limits of validity

One-dimensional resonance formula; SASE starts from noise, and its coherence needs an external seed or self-seeding to improve it.

Independent calculation

Show solution

λ_r∝1/γ²; doubling γ → the wavelength becomes 1/4.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can FEL brightness exceed that of storage-ring light sources?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

Coherent radiation power ∝N² (the square of the number of electrons), while spontaneous radiation is only ∝N.

Advanced self-test

One step beyond the previous problem, or a change of units; when you review on days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

Halving γ makes 1/γ² grow by a factor of 4.

Before moving to the next stage

  1. Close the page and explain the assumptions, derivation, and conclusions of this lesson's model to an imaginary classmate.
  2. Find a case outside the limits of validity and decide which term in the formula fails first.
  3. Within two days redo both self-tests without looking at the solutions; for whichever step you get wrong, return to the corresponding section.
