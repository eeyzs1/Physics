Synchrotron oscillations and longitudinal dynamics

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: the RF-acceleration lesson; the beam-matrices lesson. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

In a circular accelerator the particles must stay in phase with the RF: the longitudinal motion is a coupled phase–energy oscillation whose stable region (the bucket) is bounded by the synchrotron frequency and the energy acceptance — the backbone of longitudinal beam physics.

synchrotron frequency Ω_s=hω₀√(qV|ηcosφ_s|/(2πE)); bucket height ΔE_max

From assumptions to results

  1. A deviation in the particle energy changes the revolution period, the phase slips accordingly, and the RF field deviation creates a restoring force — the synchrotron oscillation.
  2. The phase-slip factor η=α−1/γ² changes sign at the transition energy (proton machines must cross it).
  3. The bucket area determines the bunch size and lifetime that can be accommodated.
  4. Longitudinal emittance and coupling impedance (linking to the collective-effects lesson).

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The LHC's 400 MHz cavities give a harmonic number of about 3.6×10⁴ for the 27 km ring; the synchrotron frequency is about 60 Hz — two to three orders of magnitude below the revolution frequency; synchrotron-radiation damping in electron storage rings (linking to the synchrotron-radiation lesson) naturally compresses the bunch.

Limits of validity

Small-amplitude linearization; near the transition energy and at high beam current, nonlinear and impedance models are required.
