Magnetic rigidity: how magnets select the beam orbit

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation breaks down.

Prerequisites: Lorentz force; circular motion; relativistic momentum. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

A charged particle moving perpendicular to a uniform magnetic field is deflected along a circular arc. The magnetic rigidity Bρ measures the size of the momentum relative to the charge; for high-energy particles the relativistic momentum must be used, and one cannot always use mv.

Bρ=p/|q|

From assumptions to results

  1. The magnetic force has magnitude |q|vB, and in circular motion the rate of change of the momentum direction is pv/ρ.
  2. Setting the two equal and canceling v yields the relation. The magnetic force itself does not change the energy.

Worked example and check

For a particle of the same charge, doubling the momentum at unchanged field also doubles the bending radius; to keep the radius unchanged you must double the field.

Limits of validity

A uniform perpendicular bending field and the reference-orbit approximation; real magnets also have fringe fields, focusing, and dispersion.

Transfer and counterexample

Why do bending magnets and RF cavities play different roles?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

An ideal static magnetic field changes the direction of the momentum without doing work; the electric field in an RF cavity can do work on the beam and change its energy.
