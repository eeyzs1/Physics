Quantum error correction codes

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: entanglement and reduced states; quantum gates. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Quantum error correction spreads one logical qubit across the joint space of several physical qubits, detecting and correcting a small number of errors without learning the quantum state itself; the surface code is the scheme most widely adopted in current hardware.

three-qubit bit-flip code：|0_L⟩=|000⟩，|1_L⟩=|111⟩

From assumptions to results

  1. Measure error syndromes rather than the logical state: the bit-flip code uses two parity checks to locate a single-qubit flip.
  2. Dealing with bit flips and phase flips at the same time requires CSS or surface codes, implemented through local syndrome measurements and gates.

Worked examples and checks

For the three-qubit code, the syndromes 00/01/10/11 indicate no error and a flip of qubit 3/2/1 respectively; the surface code uses a d×d distance to suppress the logical error rate exponentially as the distance grows, and it is the mainstream route on superconducting platforms.

Limits of applicability

It requires error rates below threshold and errors that act locally; the syndrome measurements and gates are themselves noisy, so fault-tolerant constructions are needed.

It corrects one X flip. Its bit-flip distance is 3, but its distance against general quantum errors is 1: a single Z causes an undetected logical phase flip. It cannot correct arbitrary single-qubit errors.
