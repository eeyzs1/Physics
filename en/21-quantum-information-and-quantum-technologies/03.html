Quantum gates and the circuit model

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: qubits; linear algebra. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Quantum computing writes an algorithm as a sequence of unitary gates. Single-qubit rotations plus the controlled-NOT gate form a universal set that can approximate any unitary operation; a final measurement reads out the classical result.

H=2^(−1/2)[[1,1],[1,−1]]；{single-qubit gates, CNOT} universal

From assumptions to results

  1. The H gate rotates the computational basis into the superposition basis.
  2. CNOT entangles the two qubits.
  3. Any 2×2 unitary decomposes into rotations, and together with two-qubit gates these assemble any n-qubit unitary (universality theorem).

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked examples and checks

H takes |0⟩ to (|0⟩+|1⟩)/√2; then using it as the control of CNOT gives the Bell state (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2, whose two-qubit measurement outcomes are fully correlated.

Limits of applicability

Ideal, noiseless gates; real devices require error correction and a fault-tolerance threshold, and circuit depth is limited by decoherence.

Independent computation

|⟨0|ψ⟩|²=(1/√2)²=0.5.

Transfer and counterexamples

For the Bell state (|00⟩+|11⟩)/√2, if the first qubit is measured as 1, what is the outcome for the second qubit?

Compare with the transfer solution

It must be 1; the two qubits are fully correlated.
