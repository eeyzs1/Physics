Entanglement entropy and entanglement measures

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the density-matrix lesson; information entropy. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Quantifying entanglement: for a pure state, take the von Neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix, S=−Tr ρ_A ln ρ_A; for a Bell state S=ln2 is maximal; the "area law" for many-body ground states (entropy ∝ boundary) underlies holography and tensor-network methods.

S=−Tr(ρ_A ln ρ_A)；Bell state S=ln2

From assumptions to results

  1. Bipartition the system; the entanglement entropy measures the quantum correlations between the two parts.
  2. For a maximally entangled state, ρ_A=I/d gives S=ln d.
  3. For a separable state, S=0.
  4. Area law: the entanglement entropy of the ground state of a local Hamiltonian is proportional to the dividing area rather than the volume — the root of the efficiency of DMRG and MERA, and also an analogy with the black-hole entropy conjecture (linking to the holography lesson).
  5. For mixed states, entanglement negativity/mutual information supplement it.

Worked examples and checks

A Bell state has S=ln2≈0.693; at a spin chain's critical point the entanglement entropy grows logarithmically (the c/3 law of CFT); certifiable entanglement criteria have been applied on processors with 20+ qubits.

Limits of applicability

Entanglement entropy is not a total order on entanglement (incomparable states exist); measuring the entropy requires state tomography or randomized measurements.
