Entanglement, reduced states, and the limits of error correction

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: tensor products; density matrices; probability. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A composite system may not admit a description in which each part has its own pure state. Taking the partial trace over the remaining system gives the local state; entangled correlations do not allow controllable faster-than-light signals to be sent to a distant site by local operations alone.

Worked examples and checks

Measuring A alone gives 0 and 1 half the time each; when both sides measure in the same computational basis, the results agree. This set of correlations by itself is not yet sufficient on its own to verify a violation of a Bell inequality.

Limits of applicability

Certifying entanglement requires suitable measurements and a noise analysis; classical correlations can also show up as agreement in a single basis.
