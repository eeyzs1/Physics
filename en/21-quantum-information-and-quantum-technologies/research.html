Research training · Suggested 1–3 weeks, adjusted to prerequisites and tasks

Multi-basis measurements and noise for Bell states

This is a reproduction-type training exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare model against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Starting conditions: be able to complete the written lessons of this branch, and fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced by public or synthetic data, whose sources must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Construct the density matrix of a Bell state; compute the local reduced states; evaluate the CHSH combination for several measurement directions, adding an explicit amount of white noise.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First work out a minimal example that is analytic or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seeds.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions, and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

The local state is I/2; under ideal, appropriate measurements ∣S∣ can reach 2√2; the classical local-model upper bound is 2.

If your results disagree, first check the implementation, the units, and the assumptions before discussing new physical interpretations. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

The density matrix, the correlation functions, a sweep over noise strengths, and the measurement conventions.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must come with uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Claiming that entanglement has been proven after measuring only in the computational basis; ignoring detection selection and finite statistics.

Extension questions

Analyze statistical confidence intervals, and explain why this does not provide faster-than-light communication.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the branch paper-classification entry point, find a relevant topic and choose a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures and tables.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and computations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; before a review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page has not fabricated specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched out and verified anew while carrying out the project.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations have been checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. For each item 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full score of 12 only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
