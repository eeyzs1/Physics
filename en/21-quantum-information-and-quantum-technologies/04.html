Decoherence and quantum noise channels

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: entanglement and reduced states; probability. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Entanglement between the environment and the system destroys phase coherence: the off-diagonal elements of the density matrix decay in time, and quantum information flows into the environment. Decoherence is the core noise that quantum computing and quantum communication must fight against.

ρ₀₁(t)=ρ₀₁(0)e^(−t/T₂)

From assumptions to results

  1. Model the environment as random phase kicks.
  2. After averaging over the environment, the off-diagonal elements are multiplied by the characteristic function of the noise; Gaussian noise gives exponential decay with T₂.
  3. The relaxation time T₁ constrains T₂≤2T₁.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked examples and checks

A superconducting qubit with T₂=100 μs retains coherence e⁻¹≈37% after waiting 100 μs; one thousand 20 ns gates take 20 μs in total, leaving coherence e^(−0.2)≈82%.

Limits of applicability

The Markovian noise approximation; non-Markovian environments and error correction require a full quantum-channel description.
