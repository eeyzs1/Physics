The CHSH inequality: a quantitative test of quantum correlations

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the entanglement lesson; probability. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Turning Bell's thought experiment into a number a laboratory can compute: four measurement directions combine into the correlation function S; local hidden-variable theories are bounded by |S|≤2, quantum mechanics allows up to 2√2, and experiments measure values significantly above 2 — local realism is quantitatively ruled out.

S=E(a,b)−E(a,b′)+E(a′,b)+E(a′,b′)；quantum upper bound 2√2

From assumptions to results

  1. For the spin singlet, quantum mechanics gives E(a,b)=−a·b=−cosθ.
  2. Taking a=0°, a′=90°, b=45°, b′=135° combines to give S=−2√2, whose absolute value reaches the Tsirelson bound.
  3. Any local hidden-variable theory writes the four correlations as averages over a hidden variable λ, and the ±1-valued inequality gives |S|≤2.
  4. Since 2015, experiments have closed the locality and detection loopholes simultaneously.

Worked examples and checks

Take the angle combinations 45°/135°: the four correlations are each ±√2/2, combining to S=2√2≈2.83, and experimental values significantly exceed 2; the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Aspect, Clauser, and Zeilinger for their series of experiments.

Limits of applicability

What is violated is the class of local hidden-variable theories, not every realist theory; the quantum upper bound 2√2 follows from the structure of quantum mechanics, while PR boxes allow 4 but are excluded by physics.
