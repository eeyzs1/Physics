Quantum sensing and metrology

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the decoherence lesson; the concept of interference. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The extreme sensitivity of quantum states to perturbations can be turned around and used as a measuring instrument: atomic interferometers, ion clocks, and spin squeezing accumulate phase down to the standard quantum limit Δφ=1/√N; entangled states can push through to the Heisenberg limit 1/N — a chain of applications running from gravitational-wave detection to magnetoencephalography.

standard quantum limit Δφ=1/√N；Heisenberg limit 1/N

From assumptions to results

  1. The statistical limit 1/√N for repeated measurements on independent particles (shot noise, isomorphic to the formula in the experimental-physics field).
  2. Entangled states such as NOON states let N quanta act in phase and reach 1/N, but they are extremely fragile against loss.
  3. Ramsey interferometry is the standard geometry for atomic clocks and magnetometers.
  4. Squeezed light has tangibly improved the precision of LIGO and of atomic clocks.

Worked examples and checks

Optical atomic clocks that drift by less than 1 second over 1.4 billion years (with applications in relativistic geodesy); diamond NV centers measuring nanotesla magnetic fields and millikelvin-level temperature changes at room temperature — quantum sensing is already commercialized.

Limits of applicability

Entanglement enhancement is sensitive to loss; the engineering challenges lie in readout and calibration.
