Variational quantum algorithms and quantum tomography

Goal: follow the derivations to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the quantum algorithms lesson; the density-matrix lesson. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The practical route in the NISQ era: the variational quantum eigensolver (VQE) and QAOA use parameterized circuits plus a classical optimizer to approximate ground states/combinatorial optima; quantum tomography instead reconstructs the density matrix in full — the metrological standard for verifying and calibrating every quantum technology.

VQE：E(θ)≥E₀ variational；tomography needs O(d²) measurements

From assumptions to results

  1. VQE minimizes the expectation value over trial states to approximate molecular ground states (linking up with the variational principle); noise makes it sensitive to the ansatz and to the optimizer.
  2. QAOA alternates cost and mixer layers to approximate adiabatic evolution, solving MaxCut-type problems.
  3. Tomography reconstructs ρ by measuring a complete operator basis (e.g., Pauli); with dimension d=2ⁿ the readout burden O(d²) makes n>10 expensive, while shadow tomography cuts the overhead to logarithmic.

Worked examples and checks

VQE has computed the spectra of small molecules (H₂, LiH) on superconducting and ion-trap devices to the edge of chemical accuracy; randomized benchmarking and gate tomography are daily practice for chip calibration; classical shadows, introduced in 2020, have become standard for hardware verification.

Limits of applicability

Noise and barren plateaus limit the scale; the tomography overhead grows exponentially with the number of qubits.
