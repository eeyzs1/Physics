Born–Oppenheimer Approximation and the Potential Energy Surface

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: quantum mechanics; mass scales; molecular structure. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and Physical Picture

Electrons are usually far lighter than nuclei, so one may first solve the electronic eigenvalue problem at fixed nuclear coordinates and then let the nuclei move on the associated potential energy surface. This is the starting point for organizing molecular calculations, not a claim that electrons and nuclei are uncoupled.

H_e(R)φ_n(r;R)=E_n(R)φ_n(r;R)

From Assumptions to Results

  1. Expand the full wave function in the basis of electronic states.
  2. The nuclear kinetic energy's derivatives with respect to R act on the nuclear amplitudes and on the electronic basis alike.
  3. Only by neglecting the small derivative couplings does one obtain the single-surface approximation.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked Example and Check

Near a local minimum the potential energy surface is written V≈V₀+k(R−R₀)²/2, and the nuclear vibrational frequency is √(k/μ), connecting electronic structure to vibrational spectroscopy.

Limits of Applicability

Near crossings of energy levels, conical intersections, and ultrafast nonadiabatic processes invalidate the single-surface approximation.
