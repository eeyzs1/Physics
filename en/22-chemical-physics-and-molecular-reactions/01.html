Reaction Rates and the Activation Scale

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: exponential functions; temperature; basics of chemical kinetics. If a prerequisite feels shaky, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and Physical Picture

The dependence of reaction rates on temperature is often strongly exponential. The Arrhenius model summarizes this variation with a single effective activation energy, but a real reaction may be governed jointly by several channels and quantum effects.

k=Ae^(−E_a/RT)

From Assumptions to Results

  1. Comparing two temperatures, take logarithms to eliminate the nearly constant A, giving ln(k₂/k₁)=−E_a/R(1/T₂−1/T₁). Temperatures must be in kelvin.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked Example and Check

If some parameter set gives ln(k₂/k₁)=ln3, the rate ratio is 3; experimentally, the slope of ln k plotted against 1/T estimates the activation scale.

Limits of Applicability

A and the activation energy may vary with temperature; multistep reactions, diffusion control, and tunneling deviate from a simple straight line.

Independent Calculation

Show solution

Exponentiating both sides, k₂/k₁=e^(ln5)=5.

Transfer and Counterexample

Why does fitting to a straight line not by itself determine the microscopic reaction mechanism?

Show the transfer-exercise solution

Different mechanisms can produce similar effective activation parameters over a limited temperature range; spectroscopic, isotopic, and other kinetic evidence is required.
