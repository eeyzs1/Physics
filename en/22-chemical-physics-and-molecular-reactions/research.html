Potential-Energy Curvature, Isotopes, and Vibrational Spectroscopy

This is a reproduction exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare model and evidence. Finishing it does not automatically amount to original work.

Starting conditions: you can complete this branch's written lessons and fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced by public or synthetic data, with sources clearly labeled.

Research Question and Implementation Steps

Given a molecular potential energy curve; fit the curvature near the minimum; compare two reduced masses; test large-amplitude deviations.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First do a minimal example that can be solved analytically or by hand, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known Results: Reproduce These First

The harmonic-approximation frequency is proportional to √(k/μ); the fit near the minimum stabilizes as the window shrinks; away from the minimum, anharmonic differences appear.

If results disagree, first check the implementation, units, and assumptions, and only then discuss new physical interpretations. Agreement is no substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

A potential-energy fit, frequency ratios, window sensitivity, and physical interpretation.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must carry uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common Misjudgments

Mistaking the total molecular mass for the reduced mass; using a wide-interval quadratic fit that masks the anharmonicity.

Extension Questions

Use the Morse potential and compare how the level spacing changes with excitation number.

Paper Reading and Review

  1. From the branch paper-category gateway find a relevant topic and choose a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures or tables.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the original paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and calculations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review; before any review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched out and verified again while carrying out the project.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

Testable question; complete theoretical derivation; reproducible results; credible error analysis; alternative explanations checked; clear argumentation and citations. Each item: 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full 12 points only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
