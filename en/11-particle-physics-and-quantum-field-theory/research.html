Likelihood Inference of Scattering Counts

This is a reproduction-type training project: first reproduce known results, then compare models against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Starting conditions: you can complete the written lessons of this field and fill in the mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced with public data or synthetic data; the source must be clearly marked.

Research question and implementation steps

Build a signal-plus-background Poisson counting model; scan the signal strength; compare the approximations at high and low counts.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, variables, assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First do a minimal example that is analytically solvable or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

The Poisson variance equals the mean; the mean over repeated simulated experiments returns to the input parameters; parameter boundaries are handled clearly.

If they disagree, first check the implementation, units, and assumptions before discussing a new physical interpretation. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

Likelihood curves, parameter intervals, and sensitivity under different background assumptions.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Calling a single upward fluctuation a discovery; ignoring trials-factor effects; treating the background as a known quantity without error.

Extension questions

Add a control region that constrains the background and the signal simultaneously.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field’s paper-category entry find a relevant topic, then choose a suitable paper from that field’s textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures and tables.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one unresolved question; distinguish the paper’s conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and calculations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; before a review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page does not fabricate specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be re-searched and verified when the project is carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. For each item 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. Even a full 12 points only means this project’s report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
