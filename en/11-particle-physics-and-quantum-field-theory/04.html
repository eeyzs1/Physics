Running Couplings and Grand Unification

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the effective field theory lesson; the Standard Model lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Coupling constants “run” with the energy scale (renormalization group): in QED they grow stronger at high energy, while in QCD they grow weaker (the flip side of asymptotic freedom); extrapolated to high energy, the three gauge couplings nearly meet — the quantitative motivation for grand unification.

dg/d ln μ=β(g)；α_s(μ)∝1/ln μ

From assumptions to results

  1. The logarithmic dependence of loop diagrams gives the β function.
  2. The non-Abelian self-interaction of gluons makes the gluon loop contribute a negative sign, weakening the coupling at high energy — asymptotic freedom (2004 Nobel Prize).
  3. The three Standard Model couplings nearly intersect at 10¹⁴–10¹⁶ GeV.
  4. The proton decay predicted by minimal SU(5) has not been observed; supersymmetric corrections improve the meeting of the couplings.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

α_s falls from about 0.5 at 1 GeV to 0.118 at the Z mass (consistent across τ, Υ, and LHC experiments); α_EM rises from 1/137 at zero energy to about 1/128 at the Z mass.

Limits of validity

The strong-coupling region cannot be treated perturbatively; unification schemes depend on assumptions about new physics.
