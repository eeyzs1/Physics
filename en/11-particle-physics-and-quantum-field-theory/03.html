Simple Harmonic Modes of a Field and Particle Number

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the quantum harmonic oscillator; Fourier modes. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A free field can be decomposed into many independent vibrational modes; after quantization each mode has a ladder of energy levels. The particle number is the excitation number of the corresponding mode; interactions couple the modes and produce scattering.

H=Σ_kℏω_k(n_k+1/2)

From assumptions to results

  1. First expand the linear field equation in spatial modes.
  2. The Hamiltonian of each mode has the harmonic-oscillator form.
  3. Quantize each mode to obtain creation and annihilation operators and the number operator.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Adding 3 quanta to a single mode raises its energy by 3ℏω; the zero-point energy does not affect this difference. A global treatment of the vacuum energy requires further regularization and physical interpretation.

Limits of validity

This is the mode picture of a free bosonic field; fermionic fields follow different occupation rules, and the definition of particles in an interacting field also has limits of validity.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the claim that “a field is just many classical balls” inadequate?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

Quantum states have superposition, entanglement, and operator-algebra structure; a collection of classical particles cannot reproduce these properties in general.
