Effective Field Theory and Energy-Scale Expansion

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Taylor expansion; symmetry; dimensional analysis. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Low-energy observations do not necessarily require keeping all high-energy degrees of freedom explicitly. An effective theory describes processes with low-energy fields and the allowed operators; the effects of higher-dimensional operators are usually suppressed by powers of the high-energy scale.

correction scale ∼ c(E/Λ)^p

From assumptions to results

  1. First list the operators allowed by the low-energy symmetries.
  2. Assign negative powers of Λ according to mass dimension.
  3. For E≪Λ, order terms by E/Λ; the truncation error is estimated from the first omitted term.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

If the leading relative correction is (E/Λ)², with E/Λ=0.1 and the coefficient taken as 1, the estimate is 1%; this is not a universal error guarantee for all processes.

Limits of validity

Power counting depends on symmetries, couplings, and dynamics; unknown coefficients need not be exactly 1.

Independent computation

Show solution

(0.2)²=0.04, i.e. 4%.

Transfer and counterexample

If E is close to Λ, why does adding one more term not necessarily make the result reliable?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

The expansion parameter is no longer small; terms at different orders can be comparable, and the truncation is out of control — new degrees of freedom or a more complete theory are usually needed.

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a unit change is needed; on your review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

(1/10)²=0.01=1%.

Before moving to the next stage

  1. Close the page and explain the assumptions, derivation, and conclusions of this lesson’s model to an imagined classmate.
  2. Find a case outside the limits of validity and determine which term of the formula fails first.
  3. Within two days, redo both self-test problems without looking at the solutions; for each step you get wrong, return to the corresponding subsection.
