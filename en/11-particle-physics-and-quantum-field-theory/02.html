How Collisions Reveal Structure

Lesson goal: be able to explain “how collisions reveal structure” in your own words, and complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below on your own.

Prerequisites: quantum mechanics; relativity; field theory needed for the advanced parts. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

First, think about a question

If 200 events are produced and the efficiency is 0.5, how many do you expect to record? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Scattering experiments infer interactions from the incoming particles, the target, and the outgoing products. The cross section characterizes the probability scale for a process to occur; the event rate also depends on the beam luminosity and the detection efficiency, so a large number of events cannot be taken directly as evidence of a strong interaction.
