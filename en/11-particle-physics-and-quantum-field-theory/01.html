What Is Matter Made Of

Lesson goal: be able to explain “what matter is made of” in your own words, and complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below on your own.

Prerequisites: quantum mechanics; relativity; field theory needed for the advanced parts. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

First, think about a question

In units of e, what is the total charge of two up quarks plus one down quark? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

The Standard Model describes matter particles with quarks and leptons, describes the corresponding interactions with gauge bosons, and also includes the Higgs field. The strong, weak, and electromagnetic interactions lie within its scope; gravity is not included in the Standard Model.

Work through the example with reasoning

The up quark has charge +2/3 e and the down quark −1/3 e. The proton is made of uud, with total charge +1 e; the neutron udd has total charge zero.

Check the boundary cases

The proton is not an elementary particle; nor does the Standard Model amount to having explained dark matter and gravity.

Look back at the example: name at least one neglected factor and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?
