The Standard Model Particle Spectrum and an Introduction to Feynman Diagrams

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case on your own, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the collisions lesson; the simple harmonic modes of a field lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The Standard Model = three generations of quarks and leptons + three kinds of gauge bosons + the Higgs. Feynman diagrams draw the processes out, and the calculation rules are countable: one propagator per internal line, one coupling per vertex, and energy–momentum conserved at every vertex.

vertex ∝ coupling (e.g. e⁻γ vertex ∝√(4πα)); cross section ∝|M|²×phase space

From assumptions to results

  1. The particle spectrum is classified by spin and interaction: three generations of quarks (u,c,t and d,s,b) carrying color charge and fractional charge, and three generations of leptons (e,μ,τ and their neutrinos).
  2. The propagator is the internal-line factor (1/q² for the photon, linear in q and m for fermions); the vertex factors come from the Lagrangian.
  3. Integrating |M|² over phase space gives the cross section, e.g. σ=4πα²/(3s) for e⁺e⁻→μ⁺μ⁻ in the high-energy limit.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. When you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For e⁺e⁻→μ⁺μ⁻ via a single-photon s-channel: at √s=10 GeV, σ≈4πα²/(3s)≈0.9 nb, consistent at order of magnitude with resonance-scan experiments; near the Z resonance the weak propagator takes over, and electroweak unification must be used.

Limits of validity

This is tree-level QED; loop corrections (vacuum polarization, vertex corrections) and the weak neutral current must be included at higher precision.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can virtual particles “fail to satisfy” the mass–energy relation?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

A virtual particle is a mathematical structure of the propagator, not an observable asymptotic state; energy and momentum are conserved at each vertex, and a virtual particle’s mass does not take its on-shell value.
