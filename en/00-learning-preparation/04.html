Linear Algebra: Why Eigenvectors Matter

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: simultaneous equations, vectors, and matrix multiplication. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

A linear transformation usually changes a vector's direction, but an eigenvector only gets scaled. This turns coupled problems into independent ones in a suitable basis, and it is the common mathematical structure behind normal modes and quantum observables.

Av=λv; det(A−λI)=0

From assumptions to results

  1. Rearranging Av=λv gives (A−λI)v=0.
  2. For a nonzero solution to exist, the matrix must be non-invertible, so its determinant is zero.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

For A=[[2,1],[1,2]], the characteristic equation is (2−λ)²−1=0, giving λ=3, 1, with corresponding directions (1,1) and (1,−1).

Limits of validity

Some matrices cannot be diagonalized with a complete eigenbasis; real symmetric matrices, however, can be given an orthogonal eigenbasis.

Independent calculation

Show solution

A(1,−1)=(1,−1); the scaling factor is 1.

Transfer and counterexample

If A's off-diagonal entries are changed to 0, what are the eigenvalues and eigendirections?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

A=2I; every nonzero vector is an eigenvector, and all eigenvalues equal 2.

Advanced self-test

This one takes an extra step or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

The characteristic equation (3−λ)²−1=0 gives λ=4, 2.

Before moving on to the next stage

  2. Find a case outside the limits of validity and determine which term of the formula fails first.
  3. Within two days, redo both self-test problems without looking at the solutions; whenever a step goes wrong, go back to the corresponding section.
