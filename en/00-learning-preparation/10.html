Introduction to Ordinary Differential Equations: Separation of Variables, First-Order Linear, and Second-Order Constant Coefficients

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: derivatives and integrals; rate formulations of physical laws. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Many physical laws are stated as rates of change: decay, cooling, charging, oscillation. This lesson assembles the solution methods for the three most commonly used classes of equations in one pass: separation of variables, integrating factors, and the characteristic equation.

dx/dt=−kx → x=x₀e^(−kt); second-order constant coefficients: aλ²+bλ+c=0

From assumptions to results

  1. Separating variables moves dx/x to the same side; integrating gives the exponential solution.
  2. A first-order linear equation, multiplied by the integrating factor e^(∫Pdt), becomes a total derivative.
  3. Substituting the trial solution e^(λt) into the second-order constant-coefficient equation gives the characteristic equation; by the discriminant, the cases are overdamping, critical damping, and damped oscillation.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Radioactive decay dx/dt=−kx gives exponential decay; the characteristic equation λ²+2γλ+ω₀²=0 of the RLC circuit gives, for γ<ω₀, the damped oscillation x∝e^(−γt)cos(ω′t), with ω′=√(ω₀²−γ²).

Limits of validity

Linear equations with constant coefficients; equations with variable coefficients or nonlinear terms need series solutions or numerical methods (see the Computational Physics and Mathematical Physics fields).

Before moving on to the next stage

  2. Explain how the conclusion would change if the strongest assumption in the derivation were removed.
