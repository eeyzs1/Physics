How Derivatives and Integrals Connect Motion

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: functions, slopes, and areas. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Average velocity takes the change in position over a time interval. Shrinking the interval gives the instantaneous velocity; conversely, adding up the displacements over many very short intervals gives the integral. What the integral recovers is the change; the starting position still has to be given by the initial conditions.

v=dx/dt; x(t)=x₀+∫₀ᵗv(s)ds

From assumptions to results

  1. Let x(t)=x₀+at²/2.
  2. Differentiate to get v=at.
  3. Integrating v returns at²/2; add back the constant x₀.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

With initial position 1 m and velocity v(t)=2t m/s, the displacement from 0 to 3 s is ∫2t dt=9 m, and the final position is 10 m.

Limits of validity

The position function must be differentiable over the interval discussed; with jumps and impulses, you need a piecewise treatment or a more general description.

Independent calculation

Show solution

The displacement is [2t²]₀²=8 m; adding the initial position 2 m gives 10 m.

Transfer and counterexample

If the same velocity function is integrated from t=1 s to 2 s, what is the displacement?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

∫₁²4t dt=2(4−1)=6 m; you cannot still treat the starting time as zero.

Advanced self-test

This one takes an extra step or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

∫₀⁴3t dt=1.5×16=24 m.

Before moving on to the next stage

  1. Plug a numerical case you invent yourself into this lesson's formulas; estimate the order of magnitude first, then compute exactly.
  2. Explain how the conclusion would change if the strongest assumption in the derivation were removed.
  3. In your next review session, do the advanced problem before the basic one, and compare the time spent and error causes of the two attempts.
