From Measurement to Model

Lesson goal: explain "from measurement to model" in your own words, and independently complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: no physics background required. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about a question first

A displacement of 20 m takes 5 s; what is the average velocity in m/s? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Before describing something, first decide what to measure, in which units, and what to ignore. Length, time, and mass are different kinds of quantities and cannot be added. A model does not need to include every detail, but it must state its conditions of validity.

v = Δx / Δt

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

A person walks along a straight road from position 2 m to 14 m in 4 s. The displacement is 12 m and the average velocity is 3 m/s. If they turn back partway, the distance traveled may exceed 12 m, and the average speed may also be greater than 3 m/s.

Check the boundary cases

Average velocity depends on displacement; it is not distance divided by time.

Revisit the example: name at least one factor that was ignored, and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After one condition is changed, does the original formula still apply?
