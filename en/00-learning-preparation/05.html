Vector Calculus: Gradient, Divergence, and Curl

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: vectors; partial derivatives. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

A field assigns a scalar or a vector to every point in space. The gradient points in the direction where a scalar increases fastest; the divergence measures the net outward flux per unit volume; the curl measures the circulation per unit area. Maxwell's equations, fluid mechanics, and the gravitational field equations are all written with these three operations.

∇f; ∇·F=∂Fx/∂x+∂Fy/∂y+∂Fz/∂z; ∇×F

From assumptions to results

  1. Shrink the flux through a closed surface to a point to define the divergence.
  2. Shrink the circulation around a closed loop to a point to define the curl.
  3. Take partial derivatives of the components to get the computational formulas in Cartesian coordinates.
  4. The gradient is perpendicular to the level surfaces and points in the direction of increase.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

The vector field F=(3x,0,0) has divergence 3 at every point, indicating a uniformly distributed source; the curl of F=(0,x,0) has only a z-component of 1, making it a rigid-body-style rotating field at angular velocity 0.5 rad/s.

Limits of validity

These hold directly only for smooth fields in Cartesian coordinates; at point sources (such as a point charge) you need the δ function or the integral form.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Divergence=∂Fx/∂x=3; the other components are zero.

Transfer and counterexample

What is the z-component of the curl of F=(−y,x,0)?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

∂(x)/∂x−∂(−y)/∂y=1+1=2.

Advanced self-test

This one takes an extra step or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

∂(3z)/∂z=3; the other components are zero.
