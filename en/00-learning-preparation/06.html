Complex Numbers, Phase, and the Representation of Oscillations

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: trigonometric functions; exponential functions. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, go back to Mathematics preparation first. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Complex numbers combine amplitude and phase into a single object. e^iθ moves around the unit circle, and Euler's formula connects exponentials with trigonometric functions. In linear systems, adding complex amplitudes first and taking the real part afterwards is far faster than expanding trigonometric identities term by term.

e^iθ=cosθ+i sinθ; A e^i(ωt+φ)

From assumptions to results

  1. Compare the power series of e^x, cos, and sin to get Euler's formula.
  2. Multiplying by e^iφ equals a shift in phase.
  3. Adding complex amplitudes corresponds to adding plane vectors; the modulus gives the resultant amplitude.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

A cos(ωt) and A cos(ωt+π/2) have complex amplitudes A and iA; the resultant has modulus √2 A and phase π/4, i.e. √2 A cos(ωt+π/4). With a phase difference of π, the two complex amplitudes add to zero.

Limits of validity

This holds only for linear superposition; in nonlinear systems you cannot add complex numbers first and take the real part afterwards.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Resultant amplitude=2A|cos(Δφ/2)|=2A×cos(π/3)=A, i.e. 1 times.

Transfer and counterexample

At what phase difference is the resultant amplitude largest?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

At Δφ=0 (in phase) the resultant is 2A.

Advanced self-test

This one takes an extra step or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

2|cos(Δφ/2)|=2cos(π/6)=1.73.
