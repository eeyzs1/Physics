Graphs and Rates of Change

Lesson goal: explain "graphs and rates of change" in your own words, and independently complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: no physics background required. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about a question first

A velocity of −3 m/s is maintained for 2 s; what is the displacement in m? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

When the horizontal axis is time and the vertical axis is position, the slope represents velocity. The slope of the tangent to the curve at a point is the instantaneous velocity; the signed area under the velocity graph is the displacement. This is the physical meaning of differentiation and integration.

Δx = v Δt (constant velocity)

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

The velocity stays at 2 m/s for 3 s; the velocity graph is a rectangle of height 2 and width 3, with area 6 m. If the velocity is negative, the signed area is negative as well.

Check the boundary cases

The height of a position graph is not the velocity; the slope is.

Revisit the example: name at least one factor that was ignored, and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After one condition is changed, does the original formula still apply?
