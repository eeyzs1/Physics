Molecular motors and thermal ratchets

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: diffusion; Brownian motion. If the prerequisites are shaky, first return to mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Molecular motors move directionally amid thermal noise: nonequilibrium work breaks detailed balance, and a ratchet mechanism converts the energy of ATP hydrolysis or of ion gradients into a directional force.

Stokes drag F=6πηrv; energy scale k_BT≈4.1 pN·nm

From assumptions to results

  1. In overdamped motion, random fluctuations balance the external force.
  2. An unbiased ratchet produces no net flow at equilibrium (forbidden by detailed balance).
  3. Once a time-dependent potential or a chemical cycle is introduced, directed transport emerges, with efficiency constrained by the second law of thermodynamics.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

k_BT≈4.1 pN·nm is the energy scale at molecular dimensions: a kinesin motor with 8 nm steps and a force of about 5 pN does 40 pN·nm≈10 k_BT of work per step, the same order of magnitude as the free energy of ATP hydrolysis.

Boundaries of applicability

Overdamped and Markov approximations; the structural and chemical details differ from motor to motor.

Independent calculation

Show solution

k_BT=1.38×10⁻²³×300 J=4.14×10⁻²¹ J=4.1 pN·nm.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can an equilibrium ratchet not produce a net flow?

Solution to the transfer problem

Detailed balance forbids probability currents; sustained directional motion must be maintained in nonequilibrium by an energy input.

Advanced self-test

This problem takes one more step than the previous one or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead, to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution
