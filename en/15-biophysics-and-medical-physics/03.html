The RC model of the membrane potential

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: capacitance; Ohm's law; first-order equations. If the prerequisites are shaky, first return to mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Under simplifying conditions, the cell membrane can be regarded as a capacitance in parallel with an ionic leak conductance. An injected current first charges the membrane capacitance and is then gradually balanced by the leak channels, producing an exponential approach to the steady state.

C dV/dt=I−g(V−E_L); τ=C/g

From assumptions to results

  1. Let u=V−E_L−I/g, which reduces the equation to du/dt=−u/τ.
  2. The solution is an exponential decay, giving V approaching E_L+I/g.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

For C=100 pF and g=10 nS, τ=0.01 s=10 ms. This passive model has no voltage-gated channels, so it cannot produce a full action potential.

Boundaries of applicability

Single compartment, linear passive membrane; the microscopic origin of E_L is the Nernst potentials of the individual ions, E_i=(kBT/q)ln([ion]_out/[ion]_in); spatial propagation and voltage-gated channels require the more complete Hodgkin–Huxley-type equations.

Independent calculation

Show solution

200×10⁻¹²/(10×10⁻⁹)=0.02 s=20 ms.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does the voltage initially change more slowly when the same total current is injected into a larger capacitance?

Solution to the transfer problem

With the initial leak current the same, dV/dt=(I−I_leak)/C; the larger the capacitance, the smaller the rate of change.

Advanced self-test

This problem takes one more step than the previous one or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead, to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution

τ=C/g=100×10⁻¹²/(5×10⁻⁹)=0.02 s=20 ms.
