Fluorescence microscopy and super-resolution

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the optics diffraction lesson; excitation and emission. If the prerequisites are shaky, first return to mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Fluorescent labels plus filters let microscopes see molecules against a dark background; but the diffraction limit, Abbe's d=λ/(2NA), restricts resolution — stimulated emission depletion (STED) and single-molecule localization (PALM/STORM) get around the limit and push resolution below 20 nm (Nobel Prize 2014).

Abbe d=λ/(2NA); STED narrows the effective PSF

From assumptions to results

  1. After excitation, a fluorophore emits at a red-shifted wavelength; separating the two with filters gives high contrast.
  2. The Abbe limit gives ~200 nm.
  3. STED uses a doughnut-shaped depletion beam to suppress the excited state at the periphery, narrowing the effective PSF.
  4. PALM/STORM stochastically switches on sparse single molecules and localizes their centroids, accumulating a super-resolved image.
  5. Minimizing photobleaching is a shared engineering challenge.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

With λ=500 nm and NA=1.4: d≈180 nm; STED at 20–50 nm resolves mitochondria and microtubules; STORM has imaged ~20 nm structures of the nuclear pore complex; single-molecule fluorescence also underlies smFRET for measuring conformational dynamics.

Boundaries of applicability

Trade-offs between live-cell speed and phototoxicity; localization precision is limited by the photon count.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can single-molecule localization beat the diffraction limit?

Solution to the transfer problem

The centroid of an isolated point source can be determined statistically to a precision far smaller than the PSF width — "seeing clearly" and "telling two objects apart" are two different things.
