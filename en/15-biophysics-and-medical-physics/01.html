How random motion produces diffusion

Lesson goal: explain in your own words how random motion produces diffusion, and independently complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: thermal physics; electromagnetism; probability. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

First, think about a question

D = 1 μm²/s, t = 2 s: what is the root-mean-square displacement in μm? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

A particle in thermal motion has no persistently consistent direction of travel, but its position distribution widens over time. In one-dimensional free diffusion, the mean-square displacement is proportional to time; the root-mean-square displacement is not an average directed displacement.

〈x²〉 = 2Dt (one-dimensional, starting from the origin)

Order of use: identify the system and its conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the example below.

Worked example with reasoning

For D = 2 μm²/s and t = 4 s, the mean-square displacement is 16 μm² and the root-mean-square displacement is 4 μm. The average displacement can still be zero; that does not mean the particle is motionless.

Check the boundary cases

Diffusion distances typically grow with the square root of time, not linearly as in uniform motion.
