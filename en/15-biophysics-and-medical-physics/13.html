Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Osmotic pressure and biological electrostatics

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the diffusion lesson; electrostatic-field concepts. If the prerequisites are shaky, first return to mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A solute difference across a semipermeable membrane produces osmotic pressure (van 't Hoff's law, analogous to an ideal gas), driving water uptake by cells and concentration in the kidney; the charges of biomolecules and their ionic environment are described by the Poisson–Boltzmann equation — the electrostatic origin of everything from salting-out to DNA packaging.

π≈cRT (van 't Hoff); ∇²φ=−ρ/ε (PB equation)

From assumptions to results

  1. Equilibrium of the solvent's chemical potential gives the osmotic pressure π=cRT (ideal approximation for small molecules; macromolecules need virial corrections).
  2. Cells balance it through osmoregulation and active pumps.
  3. Near a charged surface an ion cloud screens the charge; the linearized PB equation gives Debye screening (mathematically the same structure as in the plasma lesson), and DNA's ~50 nm screening length weakens the mutual repulsion of double helices in salt.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

For a 0.1 mol/L solute at 300 K: π≈100×8.314×300≈2.5×10⁵ Pa≈2.5 atmospheres — plants live with this through cell walls and turgor pressure; physiological saline at 0.9% NaCl is designed to be isotonic.

Boundaries of applicability

The ideal approximation neglects the Donnan effect and hydration; multivalent ions require the full nonlinear PB equation.

Independent calculation

Show solution

π=cRT=100 mol/m³×8.314×300≈2.5×10⁵ Pa.

Transfer and counterexample

Why must saline be 0.9% rather than pure water?

Solution to the transfer problem

Pure water abruptly lowers the osmotic pressure outside red blood cells; water flows in and they swell and burst (hemolysis); an isotonic solution keeps cell volume stable.

Advanced self-test

This problem takes one more step than the previous one or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead, to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution

0.9% is about 154 mmol/L, isotonic with human blood plasma.
