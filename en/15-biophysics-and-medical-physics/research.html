Research training · Suggested 1–3 weeks, adjusted according to prerequisites and tasks

Does a random walk reproduce the diffusion equation

This is a reproduction exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare the model against the evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Starting conditions: be able to complete the written lessons of this field, and fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced with public or synthetic data, but the sources must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Generate many symmetric one-dimensional random walks; compute the statistics of the mean and variance; shrink the step size at fixed macroscopic D.

  1. On one page, state the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First work out a minimal example that is analytically tractable or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions, and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

The mean displacement tends to zero; the variance is proportional to the number of steps; the continuum limit approaches a Gaussian distribution.

If the results disagree, first check the implementation, units, and assumptions before discussing a new physical interpretation. Even agreement is no substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

The slope of the mean-square displacement; distribution histograms compared with the Gaussian kernel; convergence with sample size.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, and references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Using the absolute value of the mean displacement in place of the mean-square displacement; treating a single trajectory as the population distribution.

Extension questions

Add biased walks and compare against the drift-diffusion model.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field's paper classification entry, find a relevant topic and select a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send me the report and calculations for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; before any review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page has not invented specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Cutting-edge conclusions must be re-searched and verified while carrying out the project.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. Each item scores 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full 12 points only means this project's report meets the standard, not mastery of the field.
