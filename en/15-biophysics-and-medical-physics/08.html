Action potentials and Hodgkin–Huxley

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the membrane-potential lesson; ion channel concepts. If the prerequisites are shaky, first return to mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Neurons use the voltage gating of ion channels to generate all-or-none action potentials: sodium channels open rapidly to depolarize, and potassium channels act with a delay to repolarize; the HH equations quantitatively reproduce the waveform with four rate constants, marking the beginning of computational neuroscience.

HH equations for V_m; propagation speed ~1–100 m/s

From assumptions to results

  1. The cable equation describes the spatial propagation of the membrane potential.
  2. Combining the fast activation and inactivation of the sodium conductance g_Na∝m³h with the slow activation of the potassium conductance g_K∝n⁴ yields the spike waveform and the refractory period.
  3. Myelinated fibers conduct in a saltatory fashion between the nodes of Ranvier, with speed ∝ diameter and up to about 100 m/s.
  4. The all-or-none behavior arises from positive feedback in the sodium channels.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

The squid giant axon (HH 1952, Nobel Prize 1963) conducts at about 25 m/s; human Aα fibers at ~100 m/s and unmyelinated C fibers at ~1 m/s; tetrodotoxin blocking the sodium channels demonstrates the necessity of the sodium current.

Boundaries of applicability

Single-compartment model; synaptic integration and network dynamics require extensions.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Human Aα fibers: about 70–120 m/s.

Transfer and counterexample

Why do myelinated fibers conduct faster?

Solution to the transfer problem

Myelin raises the membrane resistance and lowers the capacitance; the depolarization advances by "jumping" between the nodes of Ranvier.
