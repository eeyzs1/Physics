Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Protein folding and the energy landscape

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Boltzmann factor; diffusion. If the prerequisites are shaky, first return to mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A protein sequence folds to a near-global minimum on a funnel-shaped energy landscape; the Levinthal paradox shows that folding is a descent along guided pathways, not a random search.

Folding rate ∝ exp(−ΔG‡/k_BT)

From assumptions to results

  1. The number of backbone conformations grows exponentially; random enumeration would take far longer than the age of the universe.
  2. The native state is substantially lower in energy and the landscape is funnel-shaped; folding proceeds along the local gradient, with the rate set by a few small barriers.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Small proteins fold on millisecond timescales; with a barrier ΔG‡=10 k_BT the Boltzmann factor is about 4.5×10⁻⁵, meaning a successful crossing roughly once every twenty thousand attempts.

Boundaries of applicability

Single-funnel picture; multi-state proteins and amyloid aggregates lie outside this framework.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does the Levinthal paradox rule out a random search?

Solution to the transfer problem

Randomly trying 3¹⁰⁰ conformations would take far longer than the age of the universe, so guided folding pathways must exist.
