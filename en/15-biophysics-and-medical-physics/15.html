The diffusion equation and the Gaussian propagator

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: probability density; second derivatives; integration. If the prerequisites are shaky, first return to mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

In the continuum limit, microscopic random walks give rise to the diffusion equation. Probability initially concentrated at the origin gradually spreads out, while the total probability stays 1. The falling height and growing width of the distribution are joint consequences of normalization.

∂p/∂t=D∂²p/∂x²; p=e^(−x²/4Dt)/√(4πDt)

From assumptions to results

  1. Combining the flux J=−D∂p/∂x with the conservation form ∂p/∂t=−∂J/∂x yields the diffusion equation.
  2. The Gaussian kernel satisfies both the equation and normalization, and gives the variance 2Dt.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

For D=1 μm²/s and t=8 s, the variance is 16 μm² and the standard deviation 4 μm; the average displacement is still zero.

Boundaries of applicability

No boundaries, no drift, and constant D; crowded media, confined spaces, or active transport may not be ordinary diffusion.

Independent calculation

Show solution

Variance 2Dt=2×2×9=36.

Transfer and counterexample

With a constant drift velocity u, how do the mean and the variance each change?

Solution to the transfer problem

The mean becomes ut while the variance stays 2Dt; the distribution shifts and broadens, which distinguishes directed motion from random diffusion.
