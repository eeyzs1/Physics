Overview / Biophysics and Medical Physics / Lesson 6

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

X-ray diffraction and DNA: the physical foundations of structural biology

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: wave interference; the molecular concepts from the hydrogen-atom lesson. If the prerequisites are shaky, first return to mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The periodically arranged atoms in a crystal scatter X-rays coherently, and the Bragg condition translates diffraction-spot positions into structure; the DNA double helix and protein structures were both read out with this same physics — the structural basis of biology's central dogma.

Bragg: 2d·sinθ=mλ

From assumptions to results

  1. Incident X-rays are reflected by each set of crystal planes; constructive interference occurs when the path difference between neighboring planes, 2d·sinθ, is an integer multiple of the wavelength.
  2. Spot intensities, through a Fourier transform and the phase problem (solved by heavy-atom labeling / molecular replacement), reconstruct the electron density.
  3. Fiber diffraction's "Photo 51" showed the 0.34 nm base spacing and the 3.4 nm helical pitch, from which Watson and Crick built the double helix.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

DNA has one base every 0.34 nm and a pitch of 3.4 nm (10 bases per turn); for λ=0.154 nm (Cu Kα) reflecting off the d=0.34 nm planes at m=1: sinθ=0.154/0.68≈0.23, θ≈13°; protein crystallography has solved more than 100,000 structures (PDB).

Boundaries of applicability

Crystallography requires long-range order; membrane proteins and disordered systems are complemented by cryo-electron microscopy, and the phase problem is not fully automated.

Independent calculation

Show solution

sinθ=λ/(2d)=0.154/0.68≈0.23.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is fiber diffraction, rather than single-crystal diffraction, used for DNA?

Solution to the transfer problem

DNA naturally forms parallel double-helical fibers rather than three-dimensional periodic single crystals; the fibers give an orientation-averaged diffraction pattern, and the helical parameters can still be read out.

Advanced self-test

This problem takes one more step than the previous one or requires a unit conversion; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead, to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution

2d·sinθ=λ, sinθ=0.226, 2sinθ=λ/d=0.45.
