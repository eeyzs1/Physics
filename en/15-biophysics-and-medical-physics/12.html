Michaelis–Menten enzyme kinetics and inhibitors

Goal: build the model by following the derivation, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the chemical-physics reaction lesson; the concept of saturation. If the prerequisites are shaky, first return to mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Enzyme reaction rates rise linearly with substrate at first and then saturate: v=V_max[S]/(K_m+[S]) — the Michaelis–Menten equation; competitive inhibition raises K_m, noncompetitive inhibition lowers V_max, and kinetic curves can distinguish the inhibition type.

v=V_max[S]/(K_m+[S])

From assumptions to results

  1. The steady-state approximation (for the ES complex) yields the Michaelis–Menten equation.
  2. At [S]=K_m, v=V_max/2, so K_m is the "half-speed concentration."
  3. Double-reciprocal plots or direct fitting extract the parameters.
  4. A competitive inhibitor binds the free enzyme, increasing the apparent K_m; a noncompetitive one binds both E and ES, lowering V_max.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Typical K_m values lie in the μM–mM range; at [S]=3K_m, v=0.75V_max; product feedback inhibition (e.g., ATP on phosphofructokinase) is a universal theme of metabolic regulation (linking to the bioenergetics lesson).

Boundaries of applicability

Steady-state approximation (rapid equilibrium is a special case); allosteric enzymes show cooperative Hill kinetics.

Independent calculation

Show solution

3/(1+3)=0.75.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can the magnitude of K_m reflect an enzyme's "affinity" for its substrate?

Solution to the transfer problem

The smaller the K_m, the less substrate is needed to reach half-maximal speed (especially intuitive when it approximately equals the dissociation constant); catalytic efficiency is compared by k_cat/K_m.

Advanced self-test

The competitor contends with the substrate for the active site, so more substrate is needed to reach half-speed.
