Overview / Nuclear Physics / Lesson 10

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

The β-decay continuous spectrum and the neutrino: a complete map of the three decays

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the lesson on half-life; the lesson on binding energy. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

α, β, γ correspond to three mechanisms: α is quantum tunneling (two-body, discrete spectrum), γ is electromagnetic de-excitation (discrete), and β is a three-body weak-interaction process — the continuous spectrum was the first evidence for the existence of the neutrino.

β⁻: n→p+e⁻+ν̄_e; Q=(M_parent−M_daughter−m_e)c²

From assumptions to results

  1. A two-body decay partitions Q uniquely among the products (the α spectrum is discrete).
  2. If β decay involved only the electron and the daughter nucleus as two bodies, the electron energy would be single-valued; experiment instead shows a continuous distribution up to Q — from this Pauli postulated a neutral, light particle (the neutrino) that carries away the varying energy and angular momentum.
  3. Three-body phase space gives the continuous spectral shape, with the endpoint energy equal to Q.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Tritium β⁻ has Q=18.6 keV, with the electron energy extending continuously from 0 to 18.6 keV (KATRIN has used the endpoint shape to push the upper limit on the ν mass down to ~0.8 eV); free-neutron β⁻ has Q≈782 keV and a half-life of about 880 s.

Range of applicability

Allowed-transition spectral shape; forbidden transitions and the Coulomb Fermi function correct the shape near the endpoint.

Independent calculation

Show solution

The neutrino mass is nearly zero and the daughter-nucleus recoil is tiny, so the electron can carry away essentially all of Q.

Transfer and counterexample

The α spectrum is discrete while the β spectrum is continuous — what is the root cause?

Check the transfer exercise solution

α has a two-body final state, where the conservation laws fix the energy partition uniquely; β has a three-body final state, where the energy is shared continuously between the electron and the neutrino.
