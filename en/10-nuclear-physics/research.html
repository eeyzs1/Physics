Inferring lifetimes from simulated decay data

This is a reproduction-type exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare the model against the evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Entry conditions: be able to complete the written lessons of this field, and fill in any mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced with public or synthetic data, and the source must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Generate synthetic events with exponentially distributed waiting times; compare histogram fitting with unbinned likelihood; add a fixed observation window.

  1. On one page, state the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First build a minimal example that is analytically or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions, and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

As the sample grows, the estimate approaches the true value; in the untruncated case the maximum-likelihood estimate of the lifetime is the mean waiting time; the coverage of repeated experiments can be tested.

If the results disagree, first check the implementation, the units, and the assumptions before discussing new physical interpretations. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

Estimation bias, error bars, a sample-size scan, and the window-truncation correction.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Averaging only over surviving samples while ignoring truncation; using Gaussian errors, which are wrong, at low counts.

Extension questions

Add a known background and study the correlation between the lifetime and the background parameters.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field paper classification entry point, locate a relevant topic and choose a suitable paper from a textbook or review in that field.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures and tables.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and calculations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; before a review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research capability.

This page does not fabricate specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be re-searched and verified when the project is carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. For each item, 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full score of 12 only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
