The Coulomb barrier and nuclear tunneling

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: quantum potential barriers; exponential functions. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

As positively charged nuclei approach, they first experience Coulomb repulsion. When the classical energy is insufficient to go over the barrier, quantum tunneling can still give a nonzero reaction probability; the probability is highly sensitive to the energy.

T≈exp[−2∫κ(x)dx]; κ=√[2m(V−E)]/ℏ

From assumptions to results

  1. In the forbidden region the Schrödinger equation gives decaying waves.
  2. WKB accumulates the local decay exponent along the path.
  3. For a square barrier the exponent simplifies to −2κa.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Range of applicability

WKB requires the barrier to vary slowly and the turning points to be handled properly; the rectangular-barrier example uses the thick-barrier exponential approximation.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can a small change in energy strongly change the nuclear reaction rate?

Check the transfer exercise solution

The energy affects both κ and the width of the forbidden region, and the change enters the exponent; the full reaction rate also requires averaging over the particle energy distribution.
