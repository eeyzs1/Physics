Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Neutron moderation and resonance absorption

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the lesson on reaction cross sections; elastic collisions. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Fission neutrons, born at MeV energies, must be moderated down to thermal energies to raise the fission cross section: elastic collisions with light nuclei moderate efficiently, but resonantly absorbing nuclides (U-238, Xe-135) swallow neutrons — moderator and poison control are central to reactor physics.

Logarithmic energy loss per collision ξ≈2/(A+2/3); thermal-region σ_f(²³⁵U)≈585 b

From assumptions to results

  1. In an elastic collision with a nucleus of mass number A, the average logarithmic energy loss per collision is ξ (hydrogen is most efficient, ξ=1).
  2. Hydrogen absorbs strongly, heavy water absorbs little (natural uranium can be run), graphite is in between.
  3. Strong absorption in the U-238 resonance region requires separating the fuel lumps from the moderator so that neutrons cross it quickly.
  4. After thermalization, the 1/v law raises the fission cross section to several hundred barns.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

In water, slowing from 2 MeV to 0.025 eV takes about 20 collisions; the Xe-135 absorption cross section of 2.6×10⁶ b causes the "iodine pit" after shutdown; a heavy-water reactor can go critical with natural uranium, while a light-water reactor requires enriching 235U to a few percent.

Range of applicability

Group-constant simplification; real cores are computed with coupled diffusion/transport and burnup calculations.

Independent calculation

Show solution

ln(8×10⁷)≈18.2; divided by ξ≈0.92, about 20 collisions.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can a heavy-water reactor run on natural uranium while a light-water reactor must enrich it?

Check the transfer exercise solution

Hydrogen absorbs thermal neutrons fairly strongly, so natural uranium cannot sustain the chain reaction; heavy water absorbs little, and natural uranium suffices to reach criticality.

Advanced self-test

This one takes an extra step or requires a unit conversion; switch to it when reviewing on days 1, 3, and 7 to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

About 2.6×10⁶ b, the strongest neutron poison.
