Binding energy and nuclear reactions

Lesson goal: be able to explain "binding energy and nuclear reactions" in your own words, and complete the calculations and validity-condition judgments below on your own.

Prerequisites: quantum mechanics; basics of relativity. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Start with a question

If the mass difference doubles, how many times larger is the released energy? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understand the model

A bound nucleus has a smaller mass than the sum of the masses of its free nucleons; the difference corresponds to the binding energy. Whether a reaction releases energy should be judged by comparing the masses and energies of all participants before and after the reaction, not by looking at one nucleus alone.

B = Δmc²; Q = (m_i−m_f)c²

Order of use: identify the system and conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The precise meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

If the total mass in a reaction decreases by 0.002 u, using 1 u·c² ≈ 931.5 MeV, the energy released is about 1.863 MeV. The energy is subsequently divided among the kinetic energies of the products, radiation, and so on.

Check the boundary cases

Not every fusion or fission reaction releases energy; the specific mass difference and reaction channels must be checked.

Look back at the example: name at least one neglected factor and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?
