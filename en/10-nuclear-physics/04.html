Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

The chart of nuclides and the liquid-drop model

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: binding energy; half-life. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in the same field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Nuclides arranged by neutron number versus proton number form the chart of nuclides; the semi-empirical formula for the binding energy treats the nucleus as a charged liquid drop and explains the binding energy per nucleon curve and the divide between energy release in fission and fusion.

B=a_V A−a_S A^(2/3)−a_C Z(Z−1)/A^(1/3)−a_A(A−2Z)²/A±δ

From assumptions to results

  1. The volume term is proportional to the number of nucleons.
  2. The surface term subtracts the missing bonds at the surface.
  3. The Coulomb term accounts for the repulsion between protons.
  4. The symmetry term penalizes the neutron number departing from the proton number.
  5. The pairing term explains the odd–even staggering.

Fill in the intermediate algebra steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point to the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Near iron-56 the binding energy per nucleon peaks at about 8.8 MeV: fission of heavy nuclei (about 7.6) and fusion of light nuclei both move toward the peak and release energy; the position of the curve determines the sources of nuclear energy.

Range of applicability

Mean-field picture; shell effects at the magic numbers and deformation require corrections layered on the liquid-drop picture.
