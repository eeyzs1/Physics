A Device Model of the Competition between Absorption and Collection

This is a reproduction exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare the model against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to having produced original work.

Starting conditions: be able to complete the written lessons of this field, and fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced with public or synthetic data, and sources must be clearly marked.

Research question and implementation steps

Given the absorption coefficient and the diffusion length; write down the models for absorption versus depth and for collection probability; scan over thickness.

  1. Use one page to state the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and a falsifiable prediction.
  2. First work a minimal example that is solvable analytically or by hand, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions, and check whether the conclusion is robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

At zero thickness the absorption tends to zero; at infinite diffusion length all absorbed carriers can be collected within the model; the energy and particle ledgers are consistent.

If the results disagree, first check the implementation, units, and assumptions before discussing new physical interpretations. Agreement is no substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

A thickness-response plot, parameter sensitivities, the model assumptions, and omitted mechanisms.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Taking the optimal thickness of a teaching model as a conclusion for industrial device design; omitting reflection, contacts, and electric fields.

Extension questions

Introduce front-surface reflection and compare which loss dominates in which parameter region.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the paper-classification entry for this field, find a relevant topic and choose a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and calculations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; before a review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page has not fabricated specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched for and verified anew when the project is carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. Each item scores 0 = missing, 1 = partial, 2 = adequate. A full 12 points only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
