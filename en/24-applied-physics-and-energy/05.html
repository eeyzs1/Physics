Batteries, Capacitors, and Energy Storage Density

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: electric potential and capacitance; energy. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Different devices have different storage scales: electrostatic capacitors are limited by dielectric breakdown, while chemical batteries are set by electrode potentials and the mass of active material; power density and energy density form the Ragone trade-off.

E=½CV²; battery specific energy≈ΔG/active mass

From assumptions to results

  1. The parallel-plate storage density ½εE² is constrained by the breakdown field.
  2. A battery's open-circuit voltage equals the electrode potential difference, and its capacity is given by Faraday's law.
  3. Internal resistance limits the power.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

Lithium-ion batteries store about 0.7 MJ/kg and gasoline about 46 MJ/kg (roughly 66×), but the electric drive chain is several times more efficient; supercapacitors offer high power but low energy — each has its own role.

Range of applicability

Steady-state equivalent-circuit model; lifetime degradation and safety belong to systems engineering.

Transfer and counterexample
