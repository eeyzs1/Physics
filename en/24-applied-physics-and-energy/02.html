Diffusion Length and Device Scale

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: differential equations; diffusion; lifetime. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Whether carriers can reach the electrode before recombining affects the collection efficiency of many optoelectronic devices. The diffusion coefficient and the lifetime together define a length scale, linking material properties to device dimensions.

L_D=√(Dτ)

From assumptions to results

  1. The one-dimensional steady-state minority-carrier model satisfies Dn″−n/τ=0.
  2. Substituting n∝e^(−x/L_D) gives D/L_D²=1/τ.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

For D=10 cm²/s and τ=10⁻⁶ s, L_D≈0.00316 cm≈31.6 μm. An absorption layer that is too thick can cause recombination losses, but insufficient absorption must also be considered at the same time.

Range of applicability

Linear, homogeneous, steady-state diffusion–recombination approximation; drift from electric fields and recombination at contacts and interfaces must be accounted for separately.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can increasing the thickness improve absorption yet reduce carrier collection at the same time?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

A longer optical path helps absorption, but some carriers are then farther from the electrode, lowering the probability that they reach it before recombining.
