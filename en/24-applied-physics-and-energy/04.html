Heat Pumps, Refrigeration, and Energy Quality

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Carnot cycle; heat conduction. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A heat pump moves heat rather than generating it, so its coefficient of performance can exceed 1; energy differs in quality, and the second law sets the price of raising that quality. The reverse Carnot cycle gives the upper bound.

COP_heating upper bound=T_h/(T_h−T_c)

From assumptions to results

  1. The reverse Carnot cycle transports heat from the cold side to the hot side; the input work is the lower bound for the work needed to move that amount.
  2. Real units are derated by heat-transfer temperature differences and compressor efficiency.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

With 0 °C outdoors and 20 °C indoors, the COP upper bound is 293/20≈14.7, real units achieve 3–4, while electric resistance heating has COP=1 — that is where the heat pump's physical advantage lies.

Range of applicability

Quasi-static upper bound; real cycles have irreversible losses.
