Spintronics and Metamaterials

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the magnetic order lesson; the electromagnetic wave lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The electron's spin degree of freedom can serve as an information carrier: giant magnetoresistance (GMR) gave hard-disk read heads a leap in sensitivity (2007 Nobel Prize), while tunneling magnetoresistance (TMR) and MRAM point toward memory; at the other end, artificial periodic structures use negative permittivity/negative permeability to achieve negative refraction and invisibility cloaks — the engineering design of material response.

GMR: resistance varies with parallel/antiparallel magnetization; negative refraction requires ε<0 and μ<0

From assumptions to results

  1. GMR: in ferromagnetic/nonmagnetic multilayers, spin-dependent scattering makes the resistance low for parallel magnetization and high for antiparallel; the read head detects the magnetic field of the bits on the disk.
  2. TMR with MgO barriers pushes the ratio to several hundred percent, and MRAM uses current to switch the magnetization for nonvolatile memory.
  3. Metamaterials: wire arrays give ε<0 (below the plasma frequency) and split-ring resonators give μ<0; when both are negative, negative refraction and a perfect lens (Pendry) follow, and transformation optics designs invisibility cloaks.

Worked example and check

GMR read heads set hard-disk areal density on exponential growth from 1997 onward; Fe/MgO TMR room-temperature ratios >200%; the first negative-refraction experiment (2001, Shalaev/Schultz); the invisibility carpet was demonstrated successfully at microwave frequencies, and optical frequencies are still being pursued.

Range of applicability

Metamaterial loss and bandwidth limitations (causality constraints); the efficiency and endurance of spin–orbit-torque writing are still under study.

Independent calculation

Show solution

IBM's 1997 hard-disk read head, the starting point of a billion-fold growth in areal density.

Transfer and counterexample

Does negative refraction require both ε and μ to be negative, or only one of them (both = 1; one = 2)?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

Only a double negative gives a negative refractive index; ε negative alone is a plasma (total reflection). Follow-up: why is transformation optics called the mathematics of invisibility? — A coordinate transformation is equivalent to a redistribution of material parameters, and one can design parameter profiles that make the light bend around an object and return to its original path.
