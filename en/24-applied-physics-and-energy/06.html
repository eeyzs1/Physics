The Physical Picture of Nuclear and Fusion Energy

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the binding-energy curve; the triple product. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Fission energy comes from the difference in binding energy per nucleon of heavy nuclei and is already commercial; fusion must overcome the Coulomb barrier, ignition is set by the triple-product condition, and materials and tritium self-sufficiency are the engineering hurdles.

U-235 fission about 200 MeV/nucleus; D–T fusion 17.6 MeV/reaction

From assumptions to results

  1. The binding-energy curve gives the energy-releasing window.
  2. The D–T cross section peaks at a center-of-mass energy near 100 keV; combined with the confinement time this gives ignition.
  3. Neutron energy deposition and lithium breeding make up the tritium cycle.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

Complete fission of 1 g of U-235 releases (6.02×10²³/235)×200 MeV≈8×10¹⁰ J, equivalent to the chemical energy of about 2.7 tonnes of coal.

Why must a fusion reactor breed tritium?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

Natural tritium, with a half-life of 12 years, scarcely exists; self-sufficiency must come from neutron reactions in the lithium blanket.
