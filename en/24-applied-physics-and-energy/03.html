Photovoltaic Limits and Detailed Balance

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: heat engine efficiency; photon energy. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A solar cell converts photon energy into electrical work. The Shockley–Queisser limit comes from detailed balance: photons below the band gap are not absorbed, and the excess energy of photons above the band gap is lost to thermalization.

single-junction η_SQ≈33.7% (band gap≈1.34 eV, AM1.5)

From assumptions to results

  1. Apply detailed balance to the solar spectrum: photocurrent = absorbed current − radiative recombination current.
  2. Optimize the band gap to maximize the electrical output.
  3. The two losses, thermalization and transmission, trade off against each other.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

For silicon (1.1 eV) the single-junction limit is about 32%; with concentration it can reach about 41%, and the theoretical limit for infinite tandem stacks is about 68%.

Range of applicability

Ideal single junction, no concentration, standard spectrum; real cells also have recombination, series resistance, and optical losses.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can't thermalization losses be eliminated simply by increasing the band gap?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

As the band gap grows, transmission losses rise; the two trade off against each other, and the optimum lies in between.
