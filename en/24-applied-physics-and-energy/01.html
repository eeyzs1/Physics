Heat Engine Efficiency and Energy Assessment

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the first and second laws of thermodynamics. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Energy conservation constrains the energy ledger, and entropy production further constrains the fraction of it that can be converted into work. When assessing energy technologies, distinguish efficiency, power, reserves, and the complete system boundary; a single percentage cannot summarize all of the performance.

η=W/Q_h; η_C=1−T_c/T_h

From assumptions to results

  1. Over a cycle the internal-energy change is zero, so W=Q_h−Q_c.
  2. For a reversible cycle, Q_h/T_h=Q_c/T_c.
  3. Combining these gives the Carnot bound.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first point out the equation where you got stuck, then ask me about it.

Worked example and check

With a hot reservoir at 600 K and a cold reservoir at 300 K, the Carnot efficiency bound is 0.5; real heat engines are usually lower because of irreversible processes.

Range of applicability

This is the upper bound for a heat engine operating between two constant-temperature reservoirs; it cannot be taken directly as the same efficiency formula for batteries, photovoltaics, and every other device.

Transfer and counterexample

Why does increasing a heat engine's power output alone not guarantee higher efficiency?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

Power is the work done per unit time, while efficiency is the fraction of the input energy converted into work; speeding up the process may increase irreversible losses.
