How two beams of light add

Lesson goal: be able to explain "how two beams of light add" in your own words and independently complete the calculations and validity-condition checks below.

Prerequisites: waves; electromagnetism; trigonometric functions. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about one question first

With everything else held fixed, if the wavelength is doubled, by what factor does the fringe spacing change? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understand the model

The field amplitudes of coherent waves superpose, and the brightness is related to the square of the resultant amplitude. When the phase difference is stable, bright and dark fringes can appear. The paraxial far-field approximation for the double slit converts the optical path difference into the fringe spacing on the screen.

Δy ≈ λL/d

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write down the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The precise meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Worked example with reasoning

With a wavelength of 500 nm, a screen distance of 2 m, and a slit separation of 0.5 mm, the fringe spacing is about 2 mm. First convert all quantities to meters before substituting; the slit width puts a diffraction envelope on the fringes.

Check the boundary cases

Directly adding the two intensities usually misses the cross term that appears when the beams are coherent.

Look back at the worked example: name at least one factor that was neglected, and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?
