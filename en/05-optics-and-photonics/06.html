Michelson and Fabry–Pérot interferometers

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: thin-film interference; coherence length. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The Michelson interferometer uses amplitude division to compare the optical paths of its two arms and is the prototype of precision measurement (the basic layout of LIGO); the Fabry–Pérot uses a resonant cavity with many round trips to achieve extremely narrow transmission peaks and is the standard for laser cavities and high-resolution spectroscopy.

One fringe shifts per ΔL=λ/2; FP line width δν=FSR/ℱ

From assumptions to results

  1. The beam splitter divides the light into two arms and recombines them; an optical path difference of 2ΔL corresponds to a phase of 4πΔL/λ.
  2. For the multiple-beam interference in an FP cavity, summing the geometric series gives the Airy transmission function; the higher the reflectivity, the larger the finesse ℱ≈π√R/(1−R), and the line width is compressed to the free spectral range/ℱ.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

LIGO's arms are 4 km long with a cavity finesse of about 450: the effective optical path is hundreds of kilometers, and the mirror-displacement sensitivity is on the order of 10⁻¹⁹ m; Michelson-type interferometry was also used for the null ether-drift result (a link to the history-of-science field).

Range of applicability

Assumptions of ideal parallelism and a single frequency; thermal noise and quantum noise set the real sensitivity limits.

Transfer exercise and counterexample

Why can an FP cavity greatly improve resolution?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

The light makes thousands of round trips; the phase difference is amplified by a factor of ℱ, and the line width is compressed to FSR/ℱ.
