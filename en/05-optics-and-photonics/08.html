Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Gratings and thin-film interference: two ways of splitting light

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: double-slit interference; optical path difference. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Two routes to producing interference: wavefront division (gratings) and amplitude division (thin films). A grating makes the principal maxima of multiple-slit interference both bright and narrow, and is the standard dispersive element of spectroscopy; a thin film uses the optical path difference between two reflections to explain the colors of soap films and anti-reflection coatings.

d·sinθ=mλ (principal maxima); 2nt·cosθ′=(m−½)λ (destructive reflection near normal incidence → anti-reflection)

From assumptions to results

  1. Adjacent slits have a phase difference δ=2πd·sinθ/λ.
  2. Equal-amplitude superposition of N slits is constructive at δ=2πm; the principal maxima have intensity ∝N² and angular width ∝1/N.
  3. The optical path difference between light reflected from the top and bottom surfaces of a film is 2nt·cosθ′; including the half-wave loss gives the constructive and destructive conditions.
  4. An anti-reflection coating at normal incidence uses a thickness of λ/(4n).

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

A 600 lines/mm grating has d≈1.67 μm; the first-order diffraction angle for λ=500 nm satisfies sinθ=0.30 (about 17.5°); an MgF₂ (n=1.38) anti-reflection coating for 550 nm has thickness 550/(4×1.38)≈100 nm.

Range of applicability

Scalar diffraction and an ideal slit distribution; the blaze angle, polarization effects, and the actual resolving power require further correction.

Transfer exercise and counterexample

Why do spectral lines get sharper as the total number of grating rulings increases?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

The angular width of the principal maxima ∝1/N; the coherent superposition of N slits concentrates the energy into a narrower angle.
