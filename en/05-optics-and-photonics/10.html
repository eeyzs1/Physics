A rate-equation model of the laser threshold

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: exponential growth; absorption and stimulated emission. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A laser needs gain to compensate losses and cavity feedback to select modes. The threshold is set by the balance of small-signal gain and loss; above threshold, gain saturation changes the populations and the photon number, so growth cannot continue as unlimited exponential growth.

dn/dt=(G−κ)n+S

From assumptions to results

  1. Write the net stimulated growth of the single-mode photon number as Gn, the cavity loss as κn, and the source term from spontaneous emission into that mode as S.
  2. Combine them to get the rate equation.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

If G=3 s⁻¹, κ=5 s⁻¹, and S=0, then n(t)=n₀e^(−2t); if G=κ, the linear homogeneous part neither grows nor decays.

Range of applicability

This is a linear, single-mode, small-signal model; it does not describe the full steady state above threshold or multimode competition.

Independent calculation

Show solution

The net growth rate is G−κ=3 s⁻¹.

Transfer exercise and counterexample

Why doesn't a population inversion alone guarantee reaching the laser threshold?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

The inversion can provide gain, but if the gain is smaller than the total cavity loss, the photon number still cannot grow continuously.

Advanced self-test

This one takes one more step than the previous problem, or requires a unit conversion; switch to this problem when reviewing on days 1, 3, and 7, to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution

When G=κ the net growth rate is zero: constant amplitude.
