Birefringence and wave plates

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the polarization lesson; the concept of anisotropy. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

An anisotropic crystal has different refractive indices for two orthogonal polarizations (the o ray and the e ray), so one incident beam produces two — birefringence; a crystal plate of the right thickness accumulates a phase difference of π/2 or π, making λ/4 and λ/2 wave plates, the basic elements for polarization control.

Δφ=2πΔn·d/λ; λ/4 plate Δn·d=λ/4

From assumptions to results

  1. Anisotropy of the dielectric tensor makes the two eigenpolarizations travel at different speeds; a thickness d accumulates a phase difference of 2πΔn·d/λ.
  2. Taking Δn·d=λ/4 gives π/2 (linear to circular polarization); λ/2 gives π (rotation of the plane of polarization).
  3. The effect is strongest when the optic axis makes 45° with the incident polarization.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For quartz, Δn≈0.009: a λ/4 plate for 600 nm has thickness λ/(4Δn)≈16.7 μm; calcite with Δn≈0.17 needs only 0.88 μm — completely different fabrication difficulty.

Range of applicability

Monochromatic design (the thickness depends on λ); broadband systems need achromatic combinations.
