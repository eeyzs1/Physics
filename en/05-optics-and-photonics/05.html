Coherence length and Fourier optics

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: interference; decomposing complicated waveforms into simple waves. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Interference requires the two beams to maintain a stable phase difference. The finite coherence time comes from the spectral line width, and the two are linked by a Fourier transform: the broader the spectrum, the shorter the coherence; spatial coherence is set by the angular size of the source.

L_c≈c/Δν; Δν·τ_c≈1

From assumptions to results

  1. Write the field as a superposition of frequency components.
  2. Once the phase difference spreads by more than 2π after a time τ, the interference visibility vanishes, giving Δν τ_c≈1.
  3. The spatial part is handled by the van Cittert–Zernike theorem.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

A spectral line with τ_c=1 ns corresponds to L_c=0.3 m and Δν≈1 GHz; a sodium lamp has a coherence length on the order of millimeters, while a single-mode laser can reach the kilometer scale.

Range of applicability

Assumes a statistically stationary scalar light field. The numerical factor depends on the definitions of coherence time and linewidth and the line shape; here use L_c≈c/Δν≈λ²/Δλ as an estimate. Ultrashort pulses require treatment of the full spectrum.

Transfer exercise and counterexample

If the spectral width doubles, how does the coherence length change?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

It is halved (the product Δν·τ_c≈1).

Advanced self-test

This one takes one more step than the previous problem, or requires a unit conversion; switch to this problem when reviewing on days 1, 3, and 7, to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution
