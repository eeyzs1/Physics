Why light refracts

Lesson goal: be able to explain "why light refracts" in your own words and independently complete the calculations and validity-condition checks below.

Prerequisites: waves; electromagnetism; trigonometric functions. If the formulas are hard to follow for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

Think about one question first

At normal incidence the angle of incidence is 0°. What is the angle of refraction? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understand the model

When light crosses the interface between two media, its frequency stays constant while its propagation speed and wavelength change. In the geometric-optics approximation, the angles the ray makes with the normal satisfy the law of refraction; the angles are not measured against the interface.

n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write down the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The precise meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Worked example with reasoning

Light travels from air, approximated as n = 1, into glass with n = 1.5, at an angle of incidence of 30°. The sine of the angle of refraction is 1/3, so the angle of refraction is about 19.5°, bent toward the normal.

Check the boundary cases

The frequency is unchanged when the light slows down; you cannot find the frequency in the medium by dividing the vacuum speed of light by the wavelength in the medium.
