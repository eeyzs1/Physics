Research training · Suggested 1–3 weeks, adjusted to prerequisites and tasks

Diffraction patterns of a finite aperture

This is a reproduction-type training exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare the model against evidence. Completing it does not automatically mean you have produced original work.

Starting conditions: you can complete the lessons written for this field, and you fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced with public or synthetic data, but the source must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Take the discrete Fourier transform of the aperture-plane amplitude; do a single slit first, then a circular aperture; test changes in aperture and wavelength.

  1. Use one page to state the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. Build a minimal example first that can be solved analytically or computed by hand, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions, and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: reproduce these first

The first zero of the single slit lies at an angle of about λ/a; the first Airy zero of the circular aperture lies at about 1.22λ/D; keep the normalization consistent throughout.

If your results disagree, first check the implementation, the units, and the assumptions, and only then discuss new physical interpretations; agreement also does not substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

A two-dimensional intensity map, axial cross sections, and an explanation of the sampling and zero padding.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must come with uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common pitfalls

Mistaking amplitude for intensity; overlooking the units of the Fourier coordinates; treating zero padding as new physical resolution.

Extension questions

Add an aberrated phase and compare the point spread functions and the resolving power.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field paper-classification entry point, find a relevant topic and choose a suitable paper from a textbook or a review in that field.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and the computations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; do not treat a self-assessment as certification of research ability before it has been reviewed.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched out and verified afresh while the project is being carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

A testable question; a complete theoretical derivation; reproducible results; a credible error analysis; alternative explanations checked; clear argumentation and citations. For each item, 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full score of 12 only means this project's report meets the standard, not that you have mastered the field.
