Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Optical cavities and Gaussian beams: the optical half of a laser

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, independently compute a new case, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: interference; the laser rate-model lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to satisfying all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A laser is not just a gain medium: the resonator provides feedback and mode selection, and the eigenmodes of a stable cavity are Gaussian beams. The beam waist and Rayleigh length are set by the cavity geometry; only light that simultaneously satisfies the gain and cavity-mode conditions can oscillate continuously.

Stability condition 0≤g₁g₂≤1, g_i=1−L/R_i; w(z)=w₀√(1+(z/z_R)²), z_R=πw₀²/λ

From assumptions to results

  1. A paraxial beam is represented by the complex q parameter.
  2. The ABCD matrix for one round trip inside the cavity requires q to reproduce itself; solving yields the Gaussian-mode parameters and the stability condition.
  3. At the beam waist the wavefront is planar; during propagation the beam expands hyperbolically, and z_R=πw₀²/λ gives the collimation range.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce one, first identify the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

A symmetric confocal cavity (R₁=R₂=L) sits at the center of the stability region, with w₀=√(λL/2π): λ=633 nm and L=30 cm give w₀≈0.17 mm and z_R≈0.15 m — the cavity length sets the scale of the Rayleigh length.

Range of applicability

Paraxial and fundamental-transverse-mode approximations; higher-order transverse modes, diffraction losses, and thermal lensing correct real cavities.

Transfer exercise and counterexample

Why is a plane-parallel cavity hard to use in practice?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

g₁=g₂=1 lies on the stability boundary and is extremely sensitive to misalignment and diffraction losses; in practice, stable cavities or weakly coupled end-face configurations are used.
