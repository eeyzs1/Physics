Galileo and the establishment of the experimental method

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: how to distinguish models from evidence. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Galileo established "prediction—controlled experiment—quantitative comparison" as the standard practice of physics: the inclined-plane experiments were extrapolated to the frictionless limit, and thought experiments argued that heavy and light bodies fall alike. Physics thereby separated itself from natural philosophy.

s∝t² (kinematic signature of uniform acceleration)

From assumptions to results

  1. Using an inclined plane to dilute gravity and a water clock for timing, he found s/t² to be constant.
  2. Extrapolating to free fall by pushing the incline angle toward 90°.
  3. Then idealizing away air resistance, he predicted that a hammer and a feather fall together.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

On an inclined plane the distances in successive equal time intervals are in the ratio 1:3:5 (the odd-number rule); Apollo 15 demonstrated in the lunar vacuum that a hammer and a feather fall together, confirming the idealized extrapolation.
