How to distinguish models, explanations, and evidence

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: basic probability; experimental method. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

The same set of data may fit several models; a model's predictive success does not mean that every explanation has been uniquely confirmed. Scientific argument must distinguish observations, modeling assumptions, chains of inference, and possible alternative explanations.

posterior odds = prior odds × likelihood ratio

From assumptions to results

  1. Expand Bayes' theorem for each of the two hypotheses and divide one by the other; the common evidence normalization factor cancels.
  2. This yields how the data update the relative degree of support.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

If two models start from prior odds of 1:1, and the data are 3 times more probable under model A than under model B, the posterior odds are 3:1; this is not a proof that A must be true.

Conditions of validity

The result depends on whether the candidate models, the prior, and the likelihood are reasonable; an omitted alternative hypothesis is not automatically excluded by the calculation formula.

Independent computation

Show solution

2×3=6, so the posterior odds are 6:1.

Transfer and counterexamples

Why do falsifiable predictions carry more information than arbitrary post hoc explanations?

Show solution to the transfer exercise

They restrict in advance which observations can occur, and a failed prediction forces the model to be adjusted; a narrative flexible enough to accommodate any outcome lacks this constraint.

Show solution

The data are the judge; a model may be revised, or its range of applicability narrowed.
