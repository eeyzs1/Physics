The debate over quantum interpretations and Bell's inequality

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: entanglement; probability. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

There is still no consensus on the measurement problem: Copenhagen, many-worlds, pilot-wave, and decoherence-based schemes make the same predictions for current experiments while picturing "reality" differently; Bell's theorem turned part of the debate into a testable question.

CHSH combination |S|≤2 (local hidden variables); quantum upper bound 2√2

From assumptions to results

  1. The local hidden-variable assumption forces the correlations to satisfy a Bell-type inequality.
  2. Quantum mechanics gives stronger correlations for entangled states.
  3. Aspect's and subsequent experiments consistently supported quantum mechanics, ruling out a large class of local realist theories.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

The quantum maximum of the CHSH combination is 2√2≈2.83, and experiments measure values significantly above the classical bound of 2 — the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded for the related experiments.

Conditions of validity

What the experiments rule out is the class of local hidden-variable theories, not every form of realism; there is still no decisive experiment between the interpretations.

Transfer and counterexamples

Why does decoherence both "solve and not solve" the measurement problem?

Show solution to the transfer exercise

It explains the disappearance of interference and the emergence of classical behavior, but it does not select a unique measurement outcome.
