Research training · Suggested 1–3 weeks, adjusted to prerequisites and tasks

Design a controlled study that can test learning transfer

This is a reproduction-type exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare model against evidence. Finishing it does not automatically amount to producing original results.

Starting conditions: you can complete the written lessons of this branch and fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced by public or synthetic data, and the source must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Restrict yourself to one concept; write a pretest, an immediate posttest, and delayed transfer problems; define the scoring and comparison plan in advance; you may first generate synthetic data to validate the analysis.

  1. On one page, state the research question, the variables, the assumptions, and a prediction that could be falsified.
  2. First build a minimal example that is analytic or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

The two groups are comparable in time investment and baseline; the scoring rules are fixed before looking at the results; report uncertainties and attrition.

If the results do not match, first check the implementation, the units, and the assumptions, and only then discuss new physical explanations. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

Research plan, test items, scoring rubric, synthetic-data analysis, and an ethics and privacy statement.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Reporting only significant results; treating student self-assessment as the sole outcome measure; asserting causality without a comparison condition.

Extension questions

Switch to a within-learner crossover design and discuss practice and order effects.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the paper classification entry for the fields find a relevant topic, and choose a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and calculations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; before any review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be re-searched and verified when the project is carried out.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. Each item scores 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full 12 points only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
