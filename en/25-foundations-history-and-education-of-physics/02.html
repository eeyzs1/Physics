How to test whether a teaching method works

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: experimental design; means; standard deviation. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

Education research must operationalize learning outcomes, for example performance on transfer problems and delayed tests. Immediately correct answers may come from short-term memory or familiarity with the question type; the comparison group and confounding factors determine how credible the interpretation of an effect is.

d=(mean_A−mean_B)/pooled standard deviation

From assumptions to results

  1. First fix the measurement goal and the comparison conditions, then divide the difference between the two group means by a common scale.
  2. Effect size is not the same as significance; sample size and selection bias still need to be reported.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Group A averages 80 points and group B 70 points, with a pooled standard deviation of 20 points, so d=0.5. If group A was better prepared to begin with, the difference cannot be attributed directly to the new method.

Conditions of validity

This is the simple two-group effect size; paired designs, clustered classrooms, and pretest differences require the corresponding statistical treatment.

Transfer and counterexamples

Advanced self-test

One step beyond the previous problem, or a change of units is needed; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead, to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

Only pre- and post-testing with a control group allows the effect to be attributed to the teaching method itself.

Before moving to the next stage

  1. Substitute this lesson's formula into a numerical case you invent yourself; estimate the order of magnitude first, then compute exactly.
  2. Explain how the conclusion would change if the strongest assumption in the derivation were removed.
  3. At the next review, do the advanced problem before the basic one, and compare the time spent and the causes of your errors on the two attempts.
