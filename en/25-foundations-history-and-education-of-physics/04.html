From the ether to relativity

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: introduction to relativity; interference. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

Problem and physical picture

The null result of the Michelson–Morley experiment shook the ether; Einstein rebuilt space and time from an operational definition of simultaneity. This is a model case of an experimental anomaly combined with conceptual reconstruction.

fringe shift Δm=2L(v²/c²)/λ

From assumptions to results

  1. Under the ether-drift hypothesis the difference in light travel time between the two arms is ∝v²/c², so rotating the interferometer should reveal a fringe shift.
  2. The null result supported the invariance of the speed of light and became one of the experimental pillars of special relativity.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first identify the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

With L=11 m, λ=500 nm, v=30 km/s: the expected fringe shift is about 0.4 fringes, while the instrument could detect 0.01 — the measured result was zero, ruling out simple ether drift.

Conditions of validity

A pedagogically simplified account; for the original data and discussions of error, see the history-of-science literature.

Independent computation

Show solution

2L(v²/c²)/λ=2×11×10⁻⁸/5×10⁻⁷≈0.4 fringes.

Transfer and counterexamples

How did the Lorentz contraction "rescue" the ether? Why was it ultimately abandoned?

Show solution to the transfer exercise

The contraction hypothesis could explain the null result, but it was an ad hoc patch, less simple and compelling than the principle of the invariance of the speed of light.

Advanced self-test

One step beyond the previous problem, or a change of units is needed; on review days 1, 3, and 7, do this problem instead, to avoid memorizing the answer.

Show solution

Δm=2Lv²/(c²λ)≈0.4 fringes; the measured shift was zero.

Before moving to the next stage

  1. Substitute this lesson's formula into a numerical case you invent yourself; estimate the order of magnitude first, then compute exactly.
  2. Explain how the conclusion would change if the strongest assumption in the derivation were removed.
  3. At the next review, do the advanced problem before the basic one, and compare the time spent and the causes of your errors on the two attempts.
