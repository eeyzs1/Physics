Circuits are channels for energy

Lesson goal: explain "circuits are channels for energy" in your own words and complete the calculations and conditions-of-validity judgments below on your own.

Prerequisites: vectors; classical mechanics; the advanced parts require vector calculus. If the formulas are unclear for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

First, think about a question

A 20 V potential difference across a 10 Ω resistor: how much current in A? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understanding the model

Current describes the amount of charge passing through a cross section per unit time; voltage describes the energy change per unit charge. Ohm's law applies to components whose voltage–current relationship is linear; resistance cannot be treated as a constant in all devices.

I = U/R; P = UI

Order of use: identify the object of study and the conditions → make the units consistent → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the worked example below.

Worked example with reasoning

With 12 V across a 6 Ω resistor, the current is 2 A and the power is 24 W. The resistor converts electrical energy into internal energy; the current does not mysteriously decrease after passing through a resistor.

Check the boundary cases

Voltage is not a flowing substance; in a series circuit, the current is not consumed by each resistor in turn.
