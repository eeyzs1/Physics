Overview / Electromagnetism / Lesson 5

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Electric potential, capacitance, and electric field energy

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Gauss's law; energy. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The electrostatic field is curl-free, so an electric potential can be defined: the potential energy per unit charge. Capacitance links charge and voltage; the stored energy can be written either as ½QV or as an integral of the energy density of the field.

V=−∫E·dl; C=Q/V; parallel plates C=ε₀A/d

From assumptions to results

  1. A vanishing loop integral guarantees path independence.
  2. Between parallel plates E=σ/ε₀ and V=Ed; substituting into the definition gives C=ε₀A/d.
  3. The two energy expressions are equivalent for a parallel-plate capacitor.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For vacuum parallel plates with A=1 m² and d=0.1 mm: C=8.85×10⁻¹²/10⁻⁴=8.85×10⁻⁷ F≈0.885 μF; charged to 10 V, the stored energy is ½CV²≈4.4×10⁻⁵ J.

Limits of applicability

Fringing effects and dielectric breakdown are neglected; when the gap is filled with a dielectric, the capacitance is multiplied by the relative permittivity.

Independent calculation

Show solution

C∝1/d, 0.885×2=1.77 μF.

Transfer and counterexample

If the voltage on the same capacitor is doubled, by what factor does the stored energy change?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

4 times (½CV² grows as V²).
