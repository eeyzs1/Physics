Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Polarization and magnetization: Maxwell's equations in media

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Gauss's law; Ampère's law. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

In a medium, charge and current reorganize into the polarization P and the magnetization M. Once D=ε₀E+P and H=B/μ₀−M are introduced, the equations involve only free charge and free current; the four boundary conditions at an interface govern all problems of refraction, shielding, and capacitance.

D=ε₀E+P; H=B/μ₀−M; n·(D₂−D₁)=σ_f; n×(H₂−H₁)=K_f

From assumptions to results

  1. Write the bound charge as ρ_b=−∇·P; separating the free charge out of the total Gauss's law gives the equation for D.
  2. Write the magnetization current as J_b=∇×M; separating the free current out of the total Ampère–Maxwell law gives the equation for H.
  3. Applying the integral forms to a flat pillbox and a narrow loop straddling the interface gives the jump conditions on the normal components of D and B and the tangential components of E and H.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

In a medium of relative permittivity ε_r=4, the same free-charge distribution corresponds to E reduced to 1/4 of its vacuum value (D unchanged); filling a parallel-plate capacitor with the medium multiplies the capacitance by 4; in a ferromagnet, M depends nonlinearly on H and on the magnetization history (hysteresis loop), so it cannot be described by a single χ_m.

Limits of applicability

An approximation of linear, isotropic media; ferroelectrics, high-field breakdown, and high-frequency dispersion require more complete material models.

C=ε_r·C₀=5×2=10 pF.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is Gauss's law written with D rather than E in a medium?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

D tracks only free charge, with the bound-charge contribution already absorbed into P; when solving boundary problems there is no need to know the bound-charge distribution inside the medium beforehand.
