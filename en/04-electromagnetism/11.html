Electromagnetic momentum and radiation pressure

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Poynting lesson; conservation of momentum. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Electromagnetic fields carry a momentum density S/c², and light shining on a surface exerts radiation pressure: p=I/c on an absorbing surface and 2I/c on a reflecting one — the physical basis of solar sails, optical tweezers, and the support of stellar atmospheres.

g=S/c²; p=I/c (absorbing), 2I/c (perfectly reflecting)

From assumptions to results

  1. Summing the Lorentz force over the charge–field system gives the momentum balance of the field.
  2. The momentum density is proportional to the energy flow, g=S/c².
  3. A normally incident beam delivers momentum = energy flow/c per second to an absorbing surface; on a reflecting surface the momentum is reversed, hence doubled.
  4. The radiation pressure of isotropic radiation (a blackbody cavity) is p=u/3.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Solar constant 1361 W/m²: the pressure on a perfectly reflecting sail is 2I/c≈9.1×10⁻⁶ Pa; a 1000 m² sail feels a force of about 9 mN — a route to fuel-free continuous acceleration.

Limits of applicability

Electromagnetic momentum must be accounted for together with mechanical momentum; the decomposition of momentum in media (the Abraham–Minkowski debate) is a subtle research topic.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the pressure on a reflecting surface twice that on an absorbing one?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

The momentum of the reflected light flips from +E/c to −E/c, so the momentum transfer is 2E/c.
