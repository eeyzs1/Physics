Overview / Electromagnetism / Lesson 6

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Steady magnetic fields and Ampère's law

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the electric field; the vector cross product. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A current produces a circulating magnetic field around it. In parallel with Gauss's law, Ampère's law uses symmetry to turn an integral into algebra.

∮B·dl=μ₀I_enc; long straight wire B=μ₀I/(2πr)

From assumptions to results

  1. Integrating the Biot–Savart law for a long straight wire gives B=μ₀I/2πr.
  2. Ampère's law with a concentric circular loop of radius r gives B·2πr=μ₀I.
  3. Inside a solenoid, a rectangular Amperian loop gives B=μ₀nI.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For a long straight wire with I=10 A, at r=0.05 m, B=4π×10⁻⁷×10/(2π×0.05)=4×10⁻⁵ T, the same order of magnitude as the geomagnetic field.

Limits of applicability

Applicable to steady or quasi-static currents; alternating fields need the displacement current correction; in a magnetic medium, replace μ₀ by μ.

Independent calculation

Show solution

B∝I/r, 2/2=1.

Transfer and counterexample

For a solenoid with n=1000 turns/m and I=2 A, roughly what is B inside in T?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

μ₀nI=4π×10⁻⁷×2000≈2.5×10⁻³ T.

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; switch to this problem when reviewing on days 1, 3, and 7 to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution

B=μ₀I/(2πr)=4π×10⁻⁷×10/(2π×0.02)=10⁻⁴ T=100 μT.

Before moving to the next stage

  1. Close the page and explain the assumptions, derivation, and conclusions of this lesson's model clearly to an imaginary classmate.
  2. Find a situation beyond the limits of applicability and judge which term of the formula fails first.
  3. Within two days, redo both self-test problems without looking at the solutions; for each step you get wrong, return to the corresponding subsection.
