Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Gauss's law and symmetric electric fields

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the concept of the electric field; surface integrals. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

The electric flux through any closed surface is determined only by the charge enclosed. In a highly symmetric system, choosing the Gaussian surface well turns the integral equation into an algebraic one.

∮E·dA=q_enc/ε₀

From assumptions to results

  1. Coulomb's inverse-square law plus the superposition principle is equivalent to Gauss's law.
  2. For spherical symmetry, take a concentric sphere; E is uniform and perpendicular to the surface, giving E·4πr²=q/ε₀.
  3. For cylindrical symmetry, take a coaxial cylinder.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For a spherically symmetric total charge Q=1×10⁻⁹ C, at r=0.1 m, E=kQ/r²=9×10⁹×10⁻⁹/0.01=900 N/C; inside a uniformly charged sphere only the enclosed charge contributes, and E∝r.

Limits of applicability

Direct solution requires spherical, cylindrical, or planar symmetry; general distributions still need superposition or numerical methods; in a medium, switch to D and the relative permittivity.

Independent calculation

Show solution

E∝1/r², 900/4=225.

Transfer and counterexample

What is the ratio of the field at r=R/2 inside a uniformly charged sphere to the field at the surface?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

The enclosed charge shrinks by volume to 1/8 while the area shrinks with the square of the radius to 1/4, so E(R/2)/E(R)=1/2.

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; switch to this problem when reviewing on days 1, 3, and 7 to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution

E=kQ/R²=9×10⁹×10⁻⁹/0.01=900 V/m.

Before moving to the next stage

  2. Explain how the conclusion would change if the strongest assumption in the derivation were removed.
