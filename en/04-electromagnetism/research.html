Research training · Suggested 1–3 weeks, adjusted to prerequisites and tasks

Image solution versus numerical solution for a grounded plane

This is a reproduction-type exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare model and evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to having produced original work.

Starting conditions: be able to complete the written lessons of this field, and fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced by public or synthetic data, with the source clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Derive the analytic potential of the half-space; solve the same region with the discretized Poisson equation; vary the grid and the distance to the outer boundary.

  1. On one page, write out the research question, variables, assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First do a minimal example that can be solved analytically or by hand, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions, and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: must be reproduced first

The potential on the grounded plane is zero; away from the charge the solution converges to the image solution; grid refinement reduces the error.

If results disagree, first check the implementation, units, and assumptions, and only then discuss new physical interpretations. Agreement, likewise, cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

Equipotential lines, a comparison of the potential along specified paths, and a table of grid and boundary sensitivity.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Comparing finite-difference errors directly at the point-charge singularity; attributing an outer boundary placed too close to an error in the equations.

Extension questions

Switch to a conductor of finite size and explain why the analytic image method fails.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the paper-category entry for this field, find a relevant topic and choose a suitable paper from that field's textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures and tables.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and computations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; before any review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be searched for and verified again while carrying out the project.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. For each item, 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full 12 points only means this project's report meets the standard, not that the field has been mastered.
