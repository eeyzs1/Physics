The uniqueness theorem and spherical images

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the method-of-images lesson; the concept of Laplace's equation. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

An electrostatic boundary-value problem with given boundary conditions has a unique solution — this is the mathematical basis that makes the method of images "legitimate". The image charges of a conducting sphere give an analytic solution, which reduces to an induced dipole when d≫R.

grounded-sphere image: q′=−qR/d, located R²/d from the center

From assumptions to results

  1. Prove it with Green's identity: the difference of two solutions satisfying the same boundary conditions obeys Laplace's equation with zero boundary values; the energy integral vanishes, so the difference is zero.
  2. For a point charge outside a grounded conducting sphere, placing q′=−qR/d at a distance R²/d from the center keeps the potential on the sphere identically zero.
  3. For an insulated, uncharged sphere, add a further image charge at the center to preserve charge conservation.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

A charge q=1 nC sits 10 cm from the center of a grounded sphere of radius 5 cm: q′=−0.5 nC located at 2.5 cm; for d≫R the induced dipole moment is p=4πε₀R³E₀, echoing the polarization formula for a dielectric sphere.

Limits of applicability

The electrostatic case of conductors; dielectric boundaries and situations involving magnetism require more general formulations.
