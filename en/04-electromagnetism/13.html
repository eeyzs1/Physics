Overview / Electromagnetism / Lesson 13

Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Inductance, LC oscillations, and AC impedance

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Faraday induction; capacitance. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

A coil stores magnetic energy and a capacitor stores electric energy; their exchange forms an electromagnetic oscillation. In the AC steady state, the complex impedance turns the differential equation into an algebraic one: resistances in series add, the inductive reactance is +iωL, the capacitive reactance is 1/(iωC), and at resonance the series LC impedance is zero.

V_L=L·dI/dt; ω₀=1/√(LC); Z=R+iωL+1/(iωC)

From assumptions to results

  1. Faraday's law gives the inductor voltage and the magnetic energy ½LI².
  2. KVL for the LC loop gives q̈+ω₀²q=0, with energy exchanging in a conserved way between ½LI² and ½CV².
  3. Substituting e^(iωt) for the steady-state AC turns differentiation into multiplication by iω, giving the complex impedance, voltage division, and the resonance condition Im Z=0.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

With L=1 mH and C=1 μF: ω₀=1/√(10⁻⁹)≈3.2×10⁴ rad/s (about 5 kHz); with R=10 Ω in series, Q=ω₀L/R≈3.2 and the resonance peak width is about ω₀/Q≈10⁴ rad/s.

Limits of applicability

Lumped parameters and linear components; at high frequencies, lead inductance and distributed capacitance break the lumped model.

Independent calculation

Show solution

ω₀=1/√(2×0.5)=1 rad/s.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can the voltage across the inductor at resonance far exceed the source voltage?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

At resonance the inductor and capacitor voltages have equal amplitudes and opposite phases; each can reach Q times the source voltage, while the external circuit sees only their difference.
