Advanced topic · About 30–60 min

Conductor boundaries and the method of images

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the electrostatic potential; boundary conditions. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium the field is zero and the surface is an equipotential. The method of images uses fictitious charges outside the region of interest to construct a solution satisfying the same boundary conditions; the uniqueness theorem guarantees the result is correct within the region of interest.

V=(q/4πε₀)(1/r_real−1/r_image)

From assumptions to results

  1. Put the grounded plane at z=0, with the real charge q at z=a.
  2. Place the image charge −q at z=−a.
  3. On the plane the two distances are equal, so V=0.
  4. In the half-space there are no sources besides the real charge.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

Doubling the real charge's distance a to the plane doubles its distance 2a to the image, so its attractive force falls to 1/4. The actual induced charge is distributed on the plane.

Limits of applicability

Applicable only to the corresponding geometry and boundary conditions; the image charge is not a particle that really exists beneath the conductor.

Independent calculation

Show solution

The image charge doubles too; the Coulomb force is proportional to q², hence 4 times.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can the system's energy not be taken directly from the interaction energy of two point charges?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

The induced charge builds up along with the real charge; the external work must be integrated along the change of configuration; the energy includes a corresponding 1/2 factor.
