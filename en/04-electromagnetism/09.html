Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Poynting's theorem and electromagnetic energy flow

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Maxwell's equations; conservation of energy. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

Electromagnetic fields carry energy and momentum. The Poynting vector gives the energy flow per unit area; balancing the field energy density against the energy flow yields energy conservation for the field.

S=(1/μ₀)E×B; u=½(ε₀E²+B²/μ₀)

From assumptions to results

  1. Dot Ampère's law with E and Faraday's law with B, subtract, and tidy up to get ∂u/∂t+∇·S=−J·E; the right-hand side is exactly the power density of the work done by the field on the charges.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

In a vacuum plane wave, E=cB and S=cu; the solar constant 1360 W/m² corresponds to a peak electric field √(2μ₀cS)≈1010 V/m.

Limits of applicability

The decomposition of the Poynting vector is not unique (a divergence-free curl term can be added); only its flux integral has physical meaning.

Independent calculation

Show solution

S=cu; the ratio is the speed of light, 3×10⁸ m/s.

Transfer and counterexample

When a wire delivers power, where is the energy mainly transported?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

Mainly in the electromagnetic field outside the wire, flowing along the wire's direction, not inside the wire.

Advanced self-test
