Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Electromagnetic waves from Maxwell's equations

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: curl; vector identities; differential equations. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics Preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

The problem and the physical picture

This lesson is an overview preview: we first look at one direct consequence of Maxwell's equations, while the foundations of static electric and magnetic fields are built up systematically in the lessons that follow. In a vacuum free of charge and current, the time variations of the electric and magnetic fields are linked to each other. The wave speed is set by the vacuum constants; in a plane wave the field directions are perpendicular to the direction of propagation.

∇×E=−∂B/∂t; ∇×B=μ₀ε₀∂E/∂t; c=1/√(μ₀ε₀)

From assumptions to results

  1. Take the curl of Faraday's law.
  2. Use ∇×(∇×E)=∇(∇·E)−∇²E and ∇·E=0.
  3. Obtain ∇²E=μ₀ε₀∂²E/∂t².

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point to the equation where you got stuck, then ask me.

Stuck? Show the key intermediate steps
  1. Take the curl of both sides of Faraday's law: ∇×(∇×E)=∇(∇·E)−∇²E.
  2. In the source-free region, ∇·E=0 removes the first term.
  3. Substitute the Ampère–Maxwell law into the right-hand side of ∇×E=−∂B/∂t: ∇×E=−μ₀ε₀∂²E/∂t².
  4. This gives the wave equation ∇²E=μ₀ε₀∂²E/∂t².
  5. Substituting the plane wave E₀e^(i(kx−ωt)) gives −k²=−μ₀ε₀ω², that is ω=ck with c=1/√(μ₀ε₀).
  6. Repeating the same steps for B gives a wave equation at the same speed, with k, E, and B mutually perpendicular.

If you are still stuck, writing down the specific equation and then discussing it is more effective than rereading over and over.

Worked example and check

If the electric field amplitude of a vacuum plane wave is 300 V/m, the magnetic field amplitude is B₀=E₀/c≈10⁻⁶ T; the two are not independent arbitrary choices.

Limits of applicability

What we have here is a wave in a vacuum, source-free region; dispersion and conduction in a medium change the propagation relations.

Independent calculation

Show solution

B₀=600/(3×10⁸)=2×10⁻⁶ T=2 μT.

Transfer and counterexample

Why can a static electric field not be called radiation merely on the strength of the same source-free condition?

Check the transfer-exercise solution

A static solution can be time independent, with no wave energy flowing outward; being source-free is only one necessary part of the setting and does not fix the solution's time dependence.

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; switch to this problem when reviewing on days 1, 3, and 7 to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution

B₀=E₀/c=150/(3×10⁸)=5×10⁻⁷ T=0.5 μT.

  3. Within two days, redo both self-test problems without looking at the solutions; for each step you get wrong, return to the corresponding subsection.
