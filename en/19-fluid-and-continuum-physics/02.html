Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Poiseuille flow: from the local equation to the flow rate

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: second derivatives; pressure; viscosity. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

In steady laminar flow through a circular pipe, the pressure gradient drives the flow, while viscous shear transports momentum toward the wall. The no-slip boundary condition makes the velocity zero at the wall and maximum on the axis.

Q=πR⁴Δp/(8μL)

From assumptions to results

  1. The axial momentum equation reduces to μ(1/r)d/dr(r du/dr)=−Δp/L.
  2. Integrating with u finite at the center and u(R)=0 at the wall gives u(r)=Δp(R²−r²)/(4μL).
  3. Integrating over the cross-section then gives Q.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

With everything else held equal, doubling the pipe radius makes the flow rate 16 times larger; this is a stronger dependence than the fourfold increase in area alone.

Limits of validity

Newtonian fluid, incompressible, fully developed laminar flow in a circular pipe with no slip; not applicable to turbulence or the entrance region.

Independent computation

Show solution

Q is proportional to R⁴, and 3⁴=81.

Transfer and counterexample

If the pipe length doubles and the radius is halved, what fraction of the original flow rate remains?

Compare with the transfer-exercise solution

(1/2)⁴/2=1/32; the changes in both radius and length must be taken into account.

Advanced self-test

This one adds a step beyond the previous problem or requires a unit conversion; on the days 1, 3, and 7 reviews, do this problem instead, to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution

Q∝R⁴/L, so 16/2=8.
