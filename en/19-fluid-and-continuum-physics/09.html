Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Low-Reynolds-number flow and microfluidics

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the Poiseuille lesson; the diffusion lesson. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

For Reynolds number ≪1 inertia vanishes, the flow is reversible, and viscosity balances pressure: Stokes drag 6πηav, bacteria propelled by helical flagella, microfluidic chips with reactors designed around laminar flow and diffusion — the rules of the microscopic fluid world are completely rewritten.

Re=ρvL/μ≪1; Stokes drag F=6πηav

From assumptions to results

  1. With the inertial terms removed, the Stokes equations are linear: a sphere's drag is 6πηav and its settling velocity is 2a²Δρg/(9η).
  2. Reversibility lets the dye recombine when the stirring is reversed (Taylor's demonstration).
  3. Propulsion must be non-reciprocal (helical flagella or traveling waves).
  4. The pressure drop in a microchannel ∝L/R⁴ (linking back to Poiseuille), and the diffusive mixing time L²/D favors small dimensions.
  5. Surface tension and the surface-to-volume ratio are amplified at the microscale (the capillary number compares viscous forces with capillarity).

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For E. coli (a≈1 μm, v≈30 μm/s), Re≈10⁻⁴: the thrust is of order 1 pN (linking to the molecular-motor lesson); laminar interfaces, T-junction mixers, and droplet factories in microfluidics are already used for single-cell sequencing and point-of-care testing.

Limits of validity

Newtonian fluid and no slip; non-Newtonian mucus and active Brownian motion require extensions.
