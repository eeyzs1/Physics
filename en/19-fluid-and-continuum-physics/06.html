Undergraduate core · About 30–60 min

Compressible flow and shock waves

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Bernoulli; speed of sound. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

When the flow speed approaches or exceeds the speed of sound, density changes are no longer negligible; in supersonic flow, disturbances cannot propagate upstream and pile up into shock waves — across a shock, pressure, temperature, and entropy jump abruptly.

Mach number M=v/c; normal shock p₂/p₁=1+2γ/(γ+1)·(M²−1)

From assumptions to results

  1. Conservation of mass, momentum, and energy across the shock gives the Rankine–Hugoniot jump relations.
  2. A supersonic flow slows to subsonic through a normal shock; the entropy increase makes the process irreversible.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For M=2 and γ=1.4: p₂/p₁=1+2.8/2.4×3=4.5, so the pressure jumps 4.5-fold behind the shock; the sonic boom of a supersonic aircraft is exactly the shock sweeping over the ground.

Limits of validity

Ideal-gas normal shock; oblique shocks and expansion fans require region-by-region analysis.

Independent computation

Show solution

1+2γ/(γ+1)(M²−1)=1+1.167×3=4.5.
