Verifying the fourth-power law for laminar pipe flow

This is a reproduction exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare model against evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Starting conditions: you can complete the lessons written for this field and fill in whatever mathematics, programming, or statistics the project requires. A real experiment may be replaced by public or synthetic data, but the source must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Derive the velocity profile; integrate the flow rate over the cross-section numerically; sweep the radius and compute the Reynolds number for each condition.

  1. Write out the research question, variables, assumptions, and falsifiable predictions on one page.
  2. First build a minimal example that is analytic or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: reproduce these first

The parabolic profile satisfies no-slip at the wall; the numerical integral approaches the analytic flow rate; the conditions for laminar flow are maintained.

If your results disagree, first check the implementation, units, and assumptions before discussing any new physical interpretation. Agreement, too, is no substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

The velocity profile; the log–log slope of flow rate versus radius; a table of the range of applicability.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Misjudgments to watch for

Demanding the fourth-power law after a larger radius has pushed the flow into a different regime; confusing diameter with radius.

Extension questions

Add slip boundaries or a non-Newtonian viscosity and explain how the model changes.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the field paper-classification entry find a relevant topic, then choose a suitable paper from a textbook or review in that field.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the original paper's conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send the report and computations to me for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review them; until such review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page does not invent specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be re-searched and verified while carrying out the project.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

The question is testable; the theoretical derivation is complete; the results are reproducible; the error analysis is credible; alternative explanations are checked; the argumentation and citations are clear. Each item: 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full 12 points only means this project's report meets the bar, not that the field has been mastered.
