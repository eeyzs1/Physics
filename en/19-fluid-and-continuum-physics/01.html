The Reynolds number: first judge which effects matter

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: Newtonian mechanics; dimensional analysis. If a prerequisite is unfamiliar, first go back to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this branch do not by themselves satisfy every advanced prerequisite.

The problem and the physical picture

Inertia and viscosity are both present in a flow. Estimating their relative sizes first and then choosing a model is more reliable than directly plugging numbers into some flow-velocity formula. Similarity of the dimensionless parameter also helps in designing scaled experiments.

Re=ρUL/μ

From assumptions to results

  1. The inertial force density scales as ρU²/L, and the viscous force density scales as μU/L².
  2. Dividing one by the other gives Re. Here ρ is the density, U the velocity scale, L the length scale, and μ the dynamic viscosity.

Fill in the intermediate algebra on paper. If you cannot reproduce a step, first pinpoint the equation where you get stuck, then ask me.

Worked example and check

For water take ρ=1000 kg/m³ and μ=0.001 Pa·s; with U=0.1 m/s and L=0.01 m, Re=1000. The flow regime also depends on geometry and disturbances.

Limits of validity

There is no single critical Reynolds number that applies to all geometries; do not treat the empirical value for pipe flow as a universal constant.
