Effective Mass and Holes

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the energy-band lesson; the tight-binding lesson. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

In a crystal the electron’s response to an external force is set by the curvature of the energy band: a=(1/ℏ²)(d²E/dk²)·F, which defines the effective mass m*=ℏ²/(d²E/dk²); at the top of a band the curvature is negative, and negative-mass states are equivalent to positively charged holes — this is where semiconductor physics begins.

m*=ℏ²/(d²E/dk²); band top m*<0 ⇔ hole

From assumptions to results

  1. The semiclassical equation of motion ℏk̇=F with group velocity v=(1/ℏ)dE/dk; differentiating gives the relation between acceleration and curvature.
  2. The curvature of the tight-binding band E(k)=E₀−2t·cos(ka) reverses sign at the top of the band.
  3. Treating the missing-electron state as a positively charged, positive-mass quasiparticle makes the current and Hall signs come out right automatically.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

At the bottom of silicon’s conduction band m*≈0.26 m_e, and for holes at the top of the valence band m*≈0.39 m_e; GaAs’s 0.067 m_e gives it a high mobility — the basis of high-speed devices and lasers.

Range of applicability

Parabolic-band approximation; states far from the band edge require a full band-structure calculation.

Independent calculation

Show solution

m*≈0.067 m_e.

Transfer and counterexample

Why are holes positively charged?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

A filled band carries zero current; after removing one negative electron, the remaining current equals that of a positive charge — behavior equivalent to a positively charged quasiparticle.
