The Quantum Hall Effect

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: cyclotron motion in a magnetic field; the Fermi energy. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

In a strong magnetic field the Hall resistance of a two-dimensional electron gas shows precisely quantized plateaus while the longitudinal resistance tends to zero. The precision of the plateaus comes from a topological invariant and has become the basis of the resistance standard.

R_H=h/(e²ν), ν=1,2,…

From assumptions to results

  1. In a strong magnetic field the levels condense into Landau levels (degeneracy per unit area eB/h).
  2. When the Fermi energy lies in a gap the extended states are all filled, and the Hall conductance equals the filling factor times e²/h — the rigorous argument from Landau levels to quantized conductance is the TKNN topological theorem, not an algebraic derivation.
  3. Chiral edge channels without backscattering make the longitudinal dissipation zero.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Stuck? Expand the key intermediate steps
  1. In a strong magnetic field the two-dimensional cyclotron levels are quantized, E_n=ℏω_c(n+½), with ω_c=eB/m.
  2. Each Landau level has degeneracy per unit area eB/h.
  3. Filling factor ν=N/(eB·A·h⁻¹).
  4. When the Fermi energy lies in a region of localized states, the extended states exactly fill ν Landau levels.
  5. Longitudinal σ_xx=0 (the extended states are dissipationless); the transverse current density is j=σ_xy·E, where σ_xy=νe²/h is computed by Kubo/TKNN.
  6. Jumps between plateaus occur when the Fermi energy sweeps across the extended states.

If still stuck, write down the specific equation and then discuss it — that is more effective than rereading repeatedly.

Example and check

At the ν=1 plateau R_H=h/e²≈25.8 kΩ (the von Klitzing constant); this precision is used to reproduce the unit of resistance.

Range of applicability

Requires two dimensions, low temperature, and a strong magnetic field; the fractional quantum Hall effect additionally needs many-body correlations and the composite-fermion picture.

Independent calculation

Show solution

h/(2e²)=25.8/2=12.9 kΩ.

Transfer and counterexample

Why is the longitudinal resistance nearly zero while the transverse resistance is finite?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

The chiral edge channels have no backscattering path; the transverse voltage is set by the quantized conductance.
