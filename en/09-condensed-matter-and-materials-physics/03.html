The One-Dimensional Tight-Binding Model and Energy Bands

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: complex exponentials; periodic boundary conditions; quantum eigenstates. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

An electron in a periodic lattice can be treated with localized orbitals as a basis. Keeping only hops between neighboring sites, translational symmetry makes the plane-wave coefficients the eigenstates, and the energy forms a band as a function of wave vector.

E(k)=ε₀−2t cos(ka)

From assumptions to results

  1. For the amplitude on site j write c_j=e^(ikja).
  2. Substitute into E c_j=ε₀c_j−t(c_(j−1)+c_(j+1)).
  3. The neighboring phases add up to 2cos(ka).

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

Taking ε₀=0 and t=1 eV, the band ranges from −2 to +2 eV and the bandwidth is 4 eV. Whether the band conducts also requires considering filling and scattering.

Range of applicability

A one-orbital, nearest-neighbor, one-dimensional non-interacting model; real multiband and correlated materials are more complex.
