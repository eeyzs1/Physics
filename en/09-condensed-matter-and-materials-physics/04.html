Superconductivity and the Meissner Effect

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: magnetic fields; the concept of a Fermi gas. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

In certain materials the resistance drops to zero below a critical temperature and the magnetic field is expelled from the body. Zero resistance and perfect diamagnetism (the Meissner effect) are two independent experimental signatures; the latter shows that a superconductor is not an ideal conductor.

B=0 in the bulk (Meissner); first London equation m(dJ/dt)=n_se²E; second London equation ∇×J_s=−n_se²B/m

From assumptions to results

  1. The first London equation assumes that the superfluid electrons are accelerated as a whole by the electric field, giving a dissipationless current, but it is equivalent only to an ideal conductor (frozen flux).
  2. Adding the second London equation ∇×J_s=−n_se²B/m and combining it with Maxwell’s equations gives ∇²B=B/λ_L²: the field decays exponentially to zero from the surface over the penetration depth λ_L=√(m/(μ₀n_se²)) — the Meissner effect is a new assumption beyond zero resistance.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Stuck? Expand the key intermediate steps
  1. Take the curl of the second London equation ∇×J_s=−n_se²B/m.
  2. Use the identity ∇×(∇×B)=∇(∇·B)−∇²B and ∇·B=0.
  3. Substitute the static Ampère law ∇×B=μ₀J_s (neglecting displacement current).
  4. Combine to get ∇²B=B/λ_L², with λ_L²=m/(μ₀n_se²).
  5. For a semi-infinite superconductor the boundary solution is B(x)=B(0)e^(−x/λ_L); the penetration depth is the decay length.

If still stuck, write down the specific equation and then discuss it — that is more effective than rereading repeatedly.

Example and check

Aluminum has Tc≈1.2 K and niobium Tc≈9.3 K; aluminum’s London penetration depth λ_L≈50 nm is far smaller than typical sample sizes, so B≈0 in the bulk; when the applied field exceeds the critical field Hc superconductivity is destroyed, and Hc rises as the temperature decreases.

Range of applicability

A simple picture for type-I superconductors; type-II superconductors have magnetic flux vortices and a mixed state; the mechanism of high-temperature superconductivity remains a research frontier.
