Bloch’s Theorem, the Reciprocal Lattice, and Diffraction: The Crystal’s Dictionary

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: the energy-bands lesson; vectors and Fourier series. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

In a periodic potential the wave function is modulated with the lattice period (Bloch’s theorem); in momentum space a crystal has its own dictionary — the reciprocal lattice. Diffraction conditions and band structure are both written in terms of the reciprocal lattice.

ψ=e^(ikx)·u_k(x) (u has the lattice period); |G|=2π/a

From assumptions to results

  1. The translational symmetry of a periodic potential makes the Hamiltonian commute with the translation operator; their common eigenstates are the Bloch waves.
  2. The set of wave vectors in the Fourier expansion of a periodic function forms the reciprocal lattice (2π/a in one dimension; in three dimensions, three vectors b_i=2πa_j×a_k/V).
  3. Bragg reflection is expressed in reciprocal space as k·G=−G²/2, and band gaps open at the Brillouin zone boundaries.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

For a one-dimensional lattice with a=0.3 nm: the first reciprocal-lattice vector is 2π/a≈20.9 nm⁻¹; as the electron wave vector approaches π/a, Bragg reflection opens a gap — the band of the tight-binding lesson and the zone-boundary picture here complement each other; the Laue and powder methods of X-ray diffraction are both read in terms of reciprocal-lattice points.

Range of applicability

An ideal periodic potential; surfaces, defects, and thermal vibrations (the Debye–Waller factor) introduce corrections to diffraction and to the bands.

Transfer and counterexample

Why do band gaps open exactly at the Brillouin zone boundary?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

At the boundary k and k−G satisfy the Bragg condition; two traveling waves of equal energy couple strongly into standing waves, and the resulting energy splitting is the band gap.
