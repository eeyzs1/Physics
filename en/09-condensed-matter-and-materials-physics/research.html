How the Spectrum of a Finite Chain Approaches a Band

This is a reproduction exercise: first reproduce known results, then compare model and evidence. Completing it does not automatically amount to producing original work.

Starting conditions: you can complete the written lessons of this field and fill in the mathematics, programming, or statistics the project uses. Real experiments may be replaced with public or synthetic data, whose sources must be clearly labeled.

Research question and implementation steps

Build the nearest-neighbor tight-binding matrix; compare open and periodic boundary conditions; increase the number of lattice sites step by step.

  1. On one page, state the research question, variables, assumptions, and falsifiable predictions.
  2. First work a minimal example that is analytically solvable or hand-computable, then extend the parameter range.
  3. Fix the units, initial conditions, boundary conditions, algorithm version, and random seed.
  4. Vary the resolution, sample size, or model assumptions and check whether the conclusions are robust.

Known results: reproduce these first

The periodic-chain energies follow ε₀−2tcos(ka); the bandwidth approaches 4t; the number of eigenstates equals the number of lattice sites.

If they do not match, first check the implementation, units, and assumptions before discussing any new physical interpretation. Agreement also cannot substitute for independent verification.

Deliverables

The discrete spectrum overlaid with the analytic curve, the density of states, and a comparison of boundary conditions.

Also submit a 4–8 page report: question, methods, results, errors and limitations, reproducible steps, and references. Figures must have axes, units, and captions; quantitative conclusions must be accompanied by uncertainties or evidence of numerical convergence.

Common misjudgments

Double-counting hopping terms; handling the end-to-end connection incorrectly; using too broad a broadening that hides finite-size structure.

Extension questions

Alternate the hopping strengths, observe edge states, and state the symmetry conditions explicitly.

Paper reading and review

  1. From the paper classification by field find a relevant topic, and choose a suitable paper from that field’s textbooks or reviews.
  2. Record the title, authors, year, stable link, research question, key assumptions, and core figures and tables.
  3. Reproduce one conclusion and write down at least one open question; distinguish the paper’s conclusions from your own inferences.
  4. Send me the report and calculations for checking, or ask a teacher or researcher in the field to review it; before a review, do not treat self-assessment as certification of research ability.

This page does not fabricate specific papers, data, or experimental results for you. Frontier conclusions must be re-searched and verified while carrying out the project.

Self-assessment rubric (0–2 points per item)

Testable question; complete theoretical derivation; reproducible results; credible error analysis; alternative explanations checked; clear argumentation and citations. For each item 0=missing, 1=partial, 2=adequate. A full 12 points only means this project’s report meets the standard, not mastery of the field.
