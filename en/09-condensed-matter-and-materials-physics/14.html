The Fermi Distribution and Thermal Broadening

Goal: follow the derivation to build the model, compute a new case independently, and point out when the approximation fails.

Prerequisites: probability; chemical potential; occupation of energy levels. If the prerequisites are unfamiliar, first return to Mathematics preparation. The two introductory lessons in this field do not amount to meeting all advanced prerequisites.

Problem and physical picture

Each single-particle state holds at most one fermion with the same quantum numbers. The Fermi distribution gives the equilibrium occupation numbers; the chemical potential is fixed by conditions such as the total particle number and does not always equal the zero-temperature Fermi energy.

f(E)=1/[exp((E−μ)/(kBT))+1]

From assumptions to results

  1. For a single-particle state that may be empty or occupied, the grand-canonical weights are 1 and exp[−β(E−μ)].
  2. The occupation probability is the latter divided by the sum of the two.

Fill in the intermediate algebraic steps on paper. If you cannot reproduce them, first point out the equation where you are stuck, then ask me.

Example and check

At E=μ the occupation probability is always 1/2; when E is kBT ln3 above μ it is 1/4. Thermal broadening occurs mainly near the chemical potential: in a typical metal E_F is about 1–10 eV, corresponding to a Fermi temperature of order 10⁵ K, so the room-temperature k_BT≈0.025 eV affects only a thin layer near the Fermi surface.

Range of applicability

Fermi systems in thermal equilibrium; in strongly interacting systems the particles cannot always be treated as independent bare levels.

Independent calculation

Show solution

f=1/(9+1)=0.1.

Transfer and counterexample

Why, in a metal at very low temperature, do not all electrons participate equally in transport changes?

Compare with the transfer-problem solution

Near deeply occupied states there is a lack of available final states; states near the Fermi surface change their occupation more easily under slight excitations.

Advanced self-test

One more step than the previous problem, or a unit conversion is needed; on days 1, 3, and 7, switch to this problem during review to avoid memorizing answers.

Show solution

At T=0 all states with E<E_F are filled and all with E>E_F are empty.
