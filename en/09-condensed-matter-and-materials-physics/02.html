Phase Transitions and Collective Phenomena

Lesson goal: explain “phase transitions and collective phenomena” in your own words, and independently complete the calculations and validity judgments below.

Prerequisites: quantum mechanics; statistical physics; electromagnetism. If the formulas are unclear for now, read the examples first, then return to Learning Preparation.

First, think about a question

At the same magnetization, if the volume increases to 3 times, by what factor does the total magnetic moment change? Note your intuitive answer first; after reading, compare which step of your thinking changed.

Understand the model

Macroscopic properties depend on how particles organize collectively. Magnetization can serve as the order parameter for some magnetic phase transitions. Near a continuous phase transition the correlation length grows; superconductivity additionally involves zero resistance and the Meissner effect, and cannot be captured by a single indicator.

M = total magnetic moment / volume

Order of use: identify the object and conditions → unify the units → write the equation → substitute → check the units and order of magnitude of the result. The specific meanings and conventions of the variables are given in the example below.

Work through the example with reasoning

If the volume of a uniform sample is halved while its magnetization stays the same, the total magnetic moment halves as well. M is an intensive quantity, while the total magnetic moment is extensive; judging whether a quantity changes with system size helps you read materials data.

Check the boundary cases

Do not treat simple addition of microscopic particle properties as the explanation of all collective phenomena.

Look back at the example: name at least one neglected factor and explain under what circumstances it could affect the result. After changing one condition, does the original formula still apply?
